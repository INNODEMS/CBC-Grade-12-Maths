## Part 2. Conventions we used

### Across the book

- Sets: ξ for the universal set, A′, A − B, both ∅ and { }, ⊆ for subset (and in Essential ⊂ for proper subset, ⊇ for superset), and a colon in set-builder notation. Your earlier books have no sets chapter. Natural numbers start at 1, whole numbers at 0, and "between a and b" excludes a and b.
- Vectors: an arrow over AB or bold a, with underlining as the handwritten form. Matrices use square brackets (as Grade 11) and column vectors round brackets (as Grade 10).
- Units are written m/s and m/s². Standard deviation is σ, dividing by n. Stretch factors are positive, so a negative one is described as a stretch and a reflection.
- Real brand names are avoided except M-Pesa, in Sets. Loans come from a "mobile lender". Contexts never involve betting.
- Online safety appears through a phone-app loan (Binomial Expansions), a note on edited photos (Matrices and Transformations) and a note on free 3D tools (Three Dimensional Geometry).
- Two STACK questions are drag-to-answer, with number boxes as well. 3D interactives use sliders, because JSXGraph 3D points drag only within a plane.
- Dropdowns of fixed words (solid/dashed, yes/no, step numbers) keep their natural order.
- Some full single lessons could use a second period: Successive Transformations, Application of Matrices and Transformations, Column Vectors in Terms of Unit Vectors, and Application of Vectors to Geometry.

### Your skeleton and the designs

- Outcome lines and section order are left exactly as in your skeleton. A few could be improved:
  - Set Notation and Types of Sets share one outcome line.
  - Perpendicular Bisector Loci claims only outcome a.
  - Vector Notation repeats the representation outcome.
  - Column Vectors in Terms of Unit Vectors does not list outcome b, which it teaches.
  - Distances on the Earth's Surface says "parallels of longitude".
  - Appreciation and Depreciation share one line.
  - Angle Bisector Loci and Angle Between Skew Lines sit earlier or later than in the design.
- Three points in the KICD designs you may wish to pass on:
  - Matrices: one experience line says "linear scale factor" where the outcome means area scale factor.
  - Vectors II: the rubric has no indicators for parallel vectors, collinearity, division of a line or the ratio theorem.
  - Differentiation II: the curve-sketching rubric row repeats the integration row.

### By chapter

- Sets:
  - Singleton, proper subset, superset and disjoint are taught in Essential only. The three-set formula and greatest and least overlap are Core only.
  - Equivalent sets are written n(A) = n(B), without a symbol.
  - Venn regions are numbered in a fixed layout, so a shaded region can be typed as numbers.
- Binomial Expansions:
  - "The row for power n" counts from 0.
  - Expansions are multiplied out to power 4, and coefficients go up to power 10. Negative and fractional powers are left out, and 1/x appears only in a Challenge.
  - Approximations are to 4 decimal places.
  - Giving more terms than asked, or the exact calculator value where an estimate is asked, scores 0 with feedback that explains why.
- Compound Proportions:
  - Compound proportion is taught in Compound Proportions for both editions, with a Core-only ratio method, and applied in Rates of Work.
  - "Indirect" proportion means inverse proportion.
  - We use "worker-hours" and the "balance" method (not alligation).
  - The driving-hours limit of 8 hours is an invented company rule, with a note pointing to NTSA.
  - Mixtures of milk and water are only ever chai or recipes.
- Linear Inequalities: compound inequalities are "between" statements only.
- Linear Programming:
  - The unknowns are x and y.
  - The search line is Core only, and Essential gets a sampling interactive instead.
  - "Least waste" uses odd sizes so the answer is unique.
- Matrices and Transformations:
  - STACK uses quarter turns about O and reflections in the four lines through O.
  - Images of triangles are typed as a 2 × 3 matrix.
- Area Approximations:
  - Counting under curves is shared by both editions.
  - Core derives both rules, while Essential is given them and checks them.
  - Exact areas come from known shapes, without integration.
  - Ordinates are y₀ to yₙ.
  - Hand counts are accepted within ±1 full and ±2 part squares.
- Loci: the centre of a constant-angle arc is found by drawing 90° − θ at A. Two planning-note activities are adapted for the classroom.
- Three Dimensional Geometry:
  - Trigonometry is done in triangles.
  - The angle between two lines is the non-obtuse one.
  - Lengths are given to 2 d.p. and angles to 1 d.p., and STACK also accepts exact surds.
  - The regular tetrahedron appears only in passing.
- Trigonometry III: x runs over 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°, and period and wavelength are distinguished in contexts.
- Vectors:
  - Directions are compass points.
  - Forces are given in newtons.
  - Subtraction appears once.
  - Road signs are simple drawings.
- Vectors II: external division is written AP : PB = m : n, and STACK also accepts m : −n.
- Navigation: 1 nautical mile = 1.853 km (1.852 noted, and STACK accepts both). The Date Line is a note only.
- Commercial Arithmetic III:
  - Tax is given to the cent and other money to the shilling.
  - Depreciation is reducing balance.
  - "No carrying charge" means price = deposit + instalments.
  - NSSF, SHIF and the Housing Levy are named but not computed. Send us the rates if you want them computed.
- Statistics: ogives are drawn with straight segments, and a smooth curve is allowed. Step deviation is a Core extension. Deciles and percentiles are Core only.
- Probability III:
  - Discrete probability covers distribution tables, without mean or variance.
  - Trees go to three stages.
  - Conditional probability is left out, and the general addition law is an extension.
  - Independence is stated as an assumption.
  - Unsimplified fractions are accepted.
- Differentiation II:
  - Kinematics is split between this chapter and Integration.
  - Only stationary points of inflection are taught.
  - Intervals of increase and decrease use strict inequalities.
  - The cylinder and open-box problems use negative powers.
- Integration: the constant is written c (STACK accepts C). Integrands are of degree at most 3. Simpson's rule is one extension exercise.
