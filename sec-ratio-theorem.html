    In triangle \(OAB\text{,}\) \(\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}\) and \(\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}\text{.}\) The point \(P\) on \(AB\) divides it internally in the ratio \(2 : 3\text{.}\)

    1. What fraction of \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) is \(\overrightarrow{AP}\text{?}\)
      🔗

      🔗
      🔗

    Key Takeaway 10.8.3 The Ratio Theorem.

    Let \(A\) and \(B\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\text{.}\) The point \(P\) dividing \(AB\) internally in the ratio \(m : n\) has position vector
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{p} = \frac{n\mathbf{a} + m\mathbf{b}}{m + n}. \end{equation*}
    The point dividing \(AB\) externally in the ratio \(m : n\) has position vector
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{p} = \frac{m\mathbf{b} - n\mathbf{a}}{m - n}. \end{equation*}
    This is the ratio theorem. The weight on \(\mathbf{a}\) is the part of the ratio nearer \(B\text{.}\) For the midpoint, \(m = n = 1\) gives \(\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})\text{,}\) as in Grade 10.
    🔗
    Both forms come from \(\mathbf{p} = \mathbf{a} + t(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = (1 - t)\mathbf{a} + t\mathbf{b}\text{,}\) so the coefficients always add to \(1\text{.}\) Turning this round: a point with position vector \(\lambda\mathbf{a} + \mu\mathbf{b}\text{,}\) where \(\lambda + \mu = 1\text{,}\) lies on the line \(AB\text{.}\) It is between \(A\) and \(B\) when \(0 \lt \mu \lt 1\text{,}\) beyond \(B\) when \(\mu \gt 1\text{,}\) and beyond \(A\) when \(\mu \lt 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 10.8.5 Using the Ratio Theorem.

    The points \(A\) and \(B\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ 9 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Find the point \(P\) that divides \(AB\) internally in the ratio \(3 : 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    With \(m = 3\) and \(n = 2\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{p} = \frac{2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}}{5} = \frac{1}{5}\left(\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -2 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 21 \\ 27 \\ -6 \end{pmatrix}\right) = \frac{1}{5}\begin{pmatrix} 25 \\ 25 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    so \(P\) is \((5, 5, 0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 10.8.6 External Division by the Ratio Theorem.

    \(A\) is \((1, 2, -1)\) and \(B\) is \((3, 4, 1)\text{.}\) Find the point \(Q\) that divides \(AB\) externally in the ratio \(3 : 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{q} = \frac{3\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}}{3 - 1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{pmatrix} 9 \\ 12 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ 10 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    so \(Q\) is \((4, 5, 2)\text{.}\) Check: \(\overrightarrow{AQ} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\overrightarrow{QB} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ -1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) so \(AQ\) is three times \(QB\) and \(Q\) is beyond \(B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 10.8.7 Finding the Ratio.

    For each point, decide whether it divides \(AB\) internally or externally, and find the ratio.
    🔗
    1. \(\displaystyle \overrightarrow{OP} = \frac{1}{4}\mathbf{a} + \frac{3}{4}\mathbf{b}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. \(\displaystyle \overrightarrow{OQ} = -2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    (a) The coefficients add to \(1\) and \(\mu = \frac{3}{4}\) is between \(0\) and \(1\text{,}\) so \(P\) is between \(A\) and \(B\text{.}\) The weight on \(\mathbf{b}\) is \(m\) and the weight on \(\mathbf{a}\) is \(n\text{:}\) \(AP : PB = 3 : 1\) internally. (Check: \(\overrightarrow{AP} = \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{a} = \frac{3}{4}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})\text{.}\))
    🔗
    (b) \(-2 + 3 = 1\text{,}\) so \(Q\) is on the line \(AB\text{,}\) and \(\mu = 3 \gt 1\) puts it beyond \(B\text{.}\) \(\overrightarrow{AQ} = \mathbf{q} - \mathbf{a} = -3\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b} = 3\overrightarrow{AB}\) and \(\overrightarrow{QB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{q} = 2\mathbf{a} - 2\mathbf{b} = -2\overrightarrow{AB}\text{.}\) The lengths are \(3AB\) and \(2AB\text{,}\) so \(Q\) divides \(AB\) externally in the ratio \(3 : 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Vectors in use: in 3D animation a point is often placed “three fifths of the way” between two others; the program computes it with exactly the weights \(\frac{2}{5}\mathbf{a} + \frac{3}{5}\mathbf{b}\) of the ratio theorem.
    🔗
    Type answers in terms of a and b like 3/5*a+2/5*b or (3a+2b)/5. Type fractions with a slash, for example 3/4, not as decimals.

    1.

    Use the ratio theorem to find the point dividing \(AB\) internally in the ratio \(2 : 3\text{,}\) where \(A\) is \((4, 0, -2)\) and \(B\) is \((-1, 5, 8)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    2.

    Use the ratio theorem to find the point dividing \(AB\) externally in the ratio \(3 : 2\text{,}\) where \(A\) is \((2, 5, 1)\) and \(B\) is \((4, 2, 3)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \((8, -4, 7)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\mathbf{p} = \dfrac{3\mathbf{b} - 2\mathbf{a}}{3 - 2} = \begin{pmatrix} 12 \\ 6 \\ 9 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 10 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ -4 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    In terms of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\text{,}\) write the position vector of the point dividing \(AB\) (a) internally in the ratio \(5 : 1\text{;}\) (b) externally in the ratio \(1 : 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(\dfrac{\mathbf{a} + 5\mathbf{b}}{6}\) (b) \(\dfrac{4\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}}{3}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    (b) \(\dfrac{1\mathbf{b} - 4\mathbf{a}}{1 - 4} = \dfrac{\mathbf{b} - 4\mathbf{a}}{-3} = \dfrac{4\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}}{3}\text{.}\) The coefficients \(\frac{4}{3}\) and \(-\frac{1}{3}\) add to \(1\text{,}\) and \(\mu \lt 0\text{:}\) the point is beyond \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    A water pipe at a school runs straight from a tank \(T(0, 0, 6)\) to a tap \(R(9, 12, 0)\text{,}\) in metres. A valve \(V\) divides \(TR\) internally in the ratio \(1 : 2\text{.}\) Find the position of \(V\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(V(3, 4, 4)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\mathbf{v} = \dfrac{2\mathbf{t} + \mathbf{r}}{3} = \dfrac{1}{3}\left(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 12 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 9 \\ 12 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\right) = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Each point lies on the line \(AB\text{.}\) Say whether it divides \(AB\) internally or externally, and give the ratio. (a) \(\overrightarrow{OP} = \frac{3}{7}\mathbf{a} + \frac{4}{7}\mathbf{b}\) (b) \(\overrightarrow{OQ} = 5\mathbf{b} - 4\mathbf{a}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) Internally, \(AP : PB = 4 : 3\text{.}\) (b) Externally, \(AQ : QB = 5 : 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    (b) \(\overrightarrow{AQ} = 5\mathbf{b} - 5\mathbf{a} = 5\overrightarrow{AB}\) and \(\overrightarrow{QB} = 4\mathbf{a} - 4\mathbf{b} = -4\overrightarrow{AB}\text{;}\) \(Q\) is beyond \(B\) and \(AQ : QB = 5 : 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Challenge: The Centroid.

    In triangle \(ABC\text{,}\) \(M\) is the midpoint of \(BC\text{.}\) Show that the point \(G\) dividing \(AM\) internally in the ratio \(2 : 1\) has position vector \(\frac{1}{3}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c})\text{.}\) Explain why the three medians of the triangle meet at \(G\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    The answer is symmetric in \(\mathbf{a}\text{,}\) \(\mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{c}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\mathbf{g} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{m}}{3}\) with \(\mathbf{m} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    By the ratio theorem, \(\mathbf{g} = \dfrac{1\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{m}}{3} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c}}{3}\text{.}\) The same working for the median from \(B\) (to the midpoint of \(CA\)) or from \(C\) gives the same expression, because it does not change when \(\mathbf{a}\text{,}\) \(\mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{c}\) are swapped. So the point two thirds of the way along each median is the same point \(G\text{,}\) and all three medians pass through it.
