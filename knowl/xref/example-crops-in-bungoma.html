Example 1.5.16 Crops in Bungoma.

An agricultural officer asked \(65\) farmers in Bungoma which of maize, beans and bananas they grow. \(35\) grow maize, \(29\) beans and \(24\) bananas; \(15\) grow maize and beans, \(11\) maize and bananas, and \(10\) beans and bananas; \(6\) grow all three. How many grow maize only? How many grow exactly two of the crops? How many grow none of them?
🔗
Solution.
  1. Centre: \(6\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Exactly two: maize and beans only \(15 - 6 = 9\text{;}\) maize and bananas only \(11 - 6 = 5\text{;}\) beans and bananas only \(10 - 6 = 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Only one: maize \(35 - 9 - 5 - 6 = 15\text{;}\) beans \(29 - 9 - 4 - 6 = 10\text{;}\) bananas \(24 - 5 - 4 - 6 = 9\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. None: the seven inner regions add up to \(58\text{,}\) so none \(= 65 - 58 = 7\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Check: maize \(15 + 9 + 5 + 6 = 35\text{,}\) beans \(9 + 10 + 4 + 6 = 29\text{,}\) bananas \(5 + 4 + 9 + 6 = 24\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
So \(15\) grow maize only, \(9 + 5 + 4 = 18\) grow exactly two crops and \(7\) grow none.
🔗
Check with the formula, writing \(M\text{,}\) \(B\) and \(N\) for the farmers who grow maize, beans and bananas: \(n(M \cup B \cup N) = 35 + 29 + 24 - 15 - 11 - 10 + 6 = 58\text{,}\) and \(65 - 58 = 7\text{.}\)
