    Specific Learning Outcomes

    Add vectors in different situations
    🔗
    Grade Level
    🔗
    12
    🔗
    Estimated Duration
    🔗
    40 minutes
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Upon completion of this lesson, learners will be able to:
    1. Know: vectors are added head to tail; the resultant goes from the first tail to the last head; columns are added component by component; the order does not matter.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Do: add two or three column vectors; find the resultant of a route; combine two pushes.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Apply: walking, riding and pushing.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Teacher Resource 6.4.1 Problem-Solving and Discovery.

    Suggested Time
    🔗
    14 minutes
    🔗
    Objective
    🔗
    Learners walk two displacements and find that the resultant is the single straight move from start to finish.
    🔗
    Teacher’s Role
    🔗
    Groups of four with a tape measure and a compass (or use a school wall whose direction is known). Mark the start \(S\) with a stone. Groups without outside space use the numbers on squared paper. Every learner in a group has a job: walker, measurer, recorder, checker.
    🔗
    🔗

    Learner Experience 6.4.2 Walking on the School Field.

    1. From \(S\text{,}\) walk \(6\,\text{m}\) east, then \(8\,\text{m}\) north, and mark the finish \(F\text{.}\) Draw the walk on squared paper with \(1\) square for \(1\,\text{m}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      Solution.
      See Figure 6.4.3.
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Write each leg as a column vector (east and north positive).
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Write \(\overrightarrow{SF}\) as a column vector.
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Measure the straight-line distance \(SF\text{.}\)
      🔗
      Solution.
      About \(10\,\text{m}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Start at \(S\) again and walk \(8\,\text{m}\) north first, then \(6\,\text{m}\) east. Where do you finish?
      🔗
      Solution.
      At the same point \(F\text{:}\) the order of the legs does not matter.
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Add a third leg of \(2\,\text{m}\) west to the first walk. What is the resultant now?
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Teacher Resource 6.4.4 Structured Instruction.

    Suggested Time
    🔗
    8 minutes
    🔗
    Objective
    🔗
    To formalise the triangle law and adding column vectors.
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaways & Teacher Connection.

    1. Connecting to Learner Work: part (c) is the sum of the two columns in part (b); part (e) shows that the order does not matter.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Head to tail, then from the first tail to the last head.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Add across with across and up with up.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. The resultant is shorter than the route unless every leg points the same way: compare \(10\,\text{m}\) with \(14\,\text{m}\) walked.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 6.4.5 Adding Vectors.

    To add \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\text{,}\) draw \(\mathbf{b}\) starting at the head of \(\mathbf{a}\text{.}\) The sum \(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}\) goes from the tail of \(\mathbf{a}\) to the head of \(\mathbf{b}\text{.}\) This is the triangle law, and the sum is called the resultant: head to tail, then from the first tail to the last head.
    🔗
    Column vectors are added component by component:
    \begin{equation*} \begin{pmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a_1 + b_1 \\ a_2 + b_2 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    The order does not matter: \(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{a}\text{.}\) A vector and its reverse add up to the zero vector: \(\mathbf{a} + (-\mathbf{a}) = \mathbf{0}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 6.4.8 Forces Add Like Displacements.

    When two pushes act on one object, they have the same effect as a single push equal to their sum, the resultant force. We add forces exactly as we add displacements: component by component.

    Example 6.4.10 Adding Column Vectors.

    \(\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) \(\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{c} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Find \(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Solution.

    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 + (-2) \\ -3 + 6 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Check by drawing: \(5\) right and \(3\) down, then \(2\) left and \(6\) up, ends \(3\) right and \(3\) up from the start.
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 6.4.12 A Boda Boda Route.

    A boda boda rider leaves the stage and rides \(3\,\text{km}\) east, \(2\,\text{km}\) north, \(5\,\text{km}\) west and \(1\,\text{km}\) south along roads on a grid (Figure 6.4.11). Find the resultant displacement from the stage and the distance ridden.
    🔗

    Solution.

    With east and north positive, the four legs are \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) \(\begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Adding:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \begin{pmatrix} 3 + 0 - 5 + 0 \\ 0 + 2 + 0 - 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The rider finishes \(2\,\text{km}\) west and \(1\,\text{km}\) north of the stage. The distance ridden is \(3 + 2 + 5 + 1 = 11\,\text{km}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    A rider keeps to the marked roads for safety, so it is the distance ridden, not the resultant, that decides the fuel used and the time taken.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 6.4.14 Two Pushes on a Handcart.

    Two learners push a loaded handcart (mkokoteni) across a flat yard. Seen from above, with east and north positive, their pushes are \(\mathbf{F}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 40 \\ 30 \end{pmatrix}\) N and \(\mathbf{F}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 60 \\ -30 \end{pmatrix}\) N (Figure 6.4.13). Find the resultant force.
    🔗

    Solution.

    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{F}_1 + \mathbf{F}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 40 + 60 \\ 30 + (-30) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 100 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \text{ N} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The resultant is \(100\) N due east: the north part of one push and the south part of the other cancel out.
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 6.4.19.

    🔗
    Vectors in everyday life: in many computer games a character moves by adding one small vector after another. What single vector takes the character from its start to where it ends?
    🔗

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    Find (a) \(\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -5 \end{pmatrix}\) (b) \(\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ -6 \end{pmatrix}\) (c) \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(\begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}\) (b) \(\begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) (c) \(\begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    \(\mathbf{p} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) \(\mathbf{q} = \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{r} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Find \(\mathbf{p} + \mathbf{q} + \mathbf{r}\text{,}\) then \(\mathbf{q} + \mathbf{r} + \mathbf{p}\text{.}\) What do you notice?
    🔗
    Answer.
    Both are \(\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}\text{:}\) the order does not matter.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    A matatu drives \(6\,\text{km}\) north, then \(4\,\text{km}\) east, then \(2\,\text{km}\) south. Write its resultant displacement as a column vector (east and north positive) and in words. How far did it drive?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\text{:}\) \(4\,\text{km}\) east and \(4\,\text{km}\) north (north-east of the start); \(12\,\text{km}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    At a sports day in Nyeri, with east as the positive direction, one team pulls a rope with a force of \(\begin{pmatrix} -350 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) N and the other with \(\begin{pmatrix} 420 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) N. Find the resultant force. Which way does the rope move?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\begin{pmatrix} 70 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) N; east, towards the second team.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Halima’s first walk has displacement \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) After a second walk her total displacement is \(\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Find the displacement of the second walk.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The first walk plus the second walk is the total, so the second walk is \(\begin{pmatrix} -1 - 3 \\ 4 - (-2) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Check: \(\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Challenge: Back to the Start.

    \(\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Find \(\mathbf{c}\) so that \(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{0}\text{.}\) Draw \(\mathbf{a}\text{,}\) \(\mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{c}\) head to tail. Why do they make a closed triangle?
    🔗
    Hint.
    The route ends where it began.
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\mathbf{c} = \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ -6 \end{pmatrix}\text{;}\) the resultant is zero, so the last head meets the first tail.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) so \(\mathbf{c}\) must be its reverse, \(\begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ -6 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Head to tail, the three arrows end where they began, so they close up into a triangle.
