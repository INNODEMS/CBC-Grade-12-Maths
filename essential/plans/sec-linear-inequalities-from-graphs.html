    Section 3.5 Forming Linear Inequalities from Graphs

    Curriculum Alignment

    Strand
    🔗
    1.0 Numbers and Algebra
    🔗
    Sub-Strand
    🔗
    1.3 Linear Inequalities
    🔗
    Specific Learning Outcomes
    🔗
    Form simple linear inequalities from inequality graphs
    🔗
    Grade Level
    🔗
    12
    🔗
    Estimated Duration
    🔗
    40 minutes
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Upon completion of this lesson, learners will be able to:
    1. Know: how to read a boundary’s equation, its line style and the clear side.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Do: write one or several inequalities for a clear region, and check them with a test point in \(R\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Apply: read a rule back from a graph, such as the road rules for boda boda riders.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Teacher Resource 3.5.1 Problem-Solving and Discovery.

    Suggested Time
    🔗
    14 minutes
    🔗
    Objective
    🔗
    Learners work backwards from a drawn region to its inequalities.
    🔗
    Teacher’s Role
    🔗
    Draw the activity figure on the board. Ask first: “Which side is wanted, the shaded or the clear?”
    🔗
    🔗

    Learner Experience 3.5.2 Reading a Region.

    Work with a partner. Use Figure 3.5.3.
    🔗
    1. Write the equation of each boundary line. The sloping line passes through \((0, 4)\) and \((14, 0)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(x = 1\text{,}\) \(y = 2\) and \(2x + 7y = 28\) (both \((0, 4)\) and \((14, 0)\) give \(28\)).
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Is each line solid or dashed?
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(x = 1\) solid, \(y = 2\) dashed, \(2x + 7y = 28\) solid.
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Choose a point in \(R\text{,}\) for example \((2, 3)\text{.}\) Which side of each line is it on?
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(x = 2 \gt 1\text{;}\) \(y = 3 \gt 2\text{;}\) \(2(2) + 7(3) = 25 \lt 28\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Write the three inequalities that define \(R\text{.}\)
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(x \ge 1\text{,}\) \(y \gt 2\text{,}\) \(2x + 7y \le 28\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Check: find the corners of \(R\text{.}\)
      🔗
      Solution.
      \((1, 2)\text{,}\) \((7, 2)\) and \((1, \tfrac{26}{7})\text{.}\) Each satisfies the two equations of the lines that meet there.
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Explain why the test point must come from the clear region.
      🔗
      Solution.
      The inequalities describe \(R\text{,}\) the clear region. A point from the shaded part would give the opposite sign for at least one line.
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Teacher Resource 3.5.4 Structured Instruction.

    Suggested Time
    🔗
    8 minutes
    🔗
    Objective
    🔗
    To fix the three steps for writing inequalities from a graph.
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaways & Teacher Connection.

    1. Connecting to Learner Work: part (c) chose the direction of each sign from one point in \(R\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Three steps: equation, line style, direction from a point in \(R\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Vertical and horizontal lines give inequalities with one letter.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 3.5.5 Inequalities from a Graph.

    1. Find the equation of each boundary. A vertical line is \(x = k\) and a horizontal line is \(y = h\text{.}\) For a sloping line, use where it crosses the axes: a line through \((7, 0)\) and \((0, 4)\) is \(4x + 7y = 28\text{,}\) because both points give \(28\text{.}\) Or use its gradient and \(y\)-intercept: \(y = mx + c\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. A solid line gives \(\le\) or \(\ge\text{;}\) a dashed line gives \(\lt\) or \(\gt\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Choose the direction with a test point in the clear region \(R\text{,}\) not in the shaded part. Substitute it and choose the sign that makes the statement true.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Teacher Resource 3.5.7 Practice and Application.

    Suggested Time
    🔗
    10 minutes
    🔗
    Objective
    🔗
    To apply the learned concepts and procedures to solve varied problems.
    🔗
    Teacher’s Role
    🔗
    The teacher works through the following examples with the class, asking questions and connecting to insights from the learner experience.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.5.8 One Boundary.

    Write the inequality for the region \(R\) in Figure 3.5.9.
    🔗

    Solution.

    The line passes through \((7, 0)\) and \((0, 4)\text{,}\) so it is \(4x + 7y = 28\text{:}\) check \(4(7) = 28\) and \(7(4) = 28\text{.}\)
    🔗
    It is solid, so the sign is \(\le\) or \(\ge\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \(R\) contains \((0, 0)\text{:}\) \(4(0) + 7(0) = 0\text{,}\) and \(0 \le 28\) is true. So \(R\) is \(4x + 7y \le 28\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.5.10 A Clear Triangle.

    Write the three inequalities that define \(R\) in Figure 3.5.11.
    🔗

    Solution.

    The boundaries are \(x = 0\) (solid), \(y = 1\) (dashed) and the line through \((9, 0)\) and \((0, 5)\text{,}\) which is \(5x + 9y = 45\) (solid).
    🔗
    Take \((1, 2)\) in \(R\text{:}\) \(1 \ge 0\text{;}\) \(2 \gt 1\text{;}\) \(5(1) + 9(2) = 23 \le 45\text{.}\)
    🔗
    So \(R\) is \(x \ge 0\text{,}\) \(y \gt 1\text{,}\) \(5x + 9y \le 45\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.5.12 Using the Gradient.

    A dashed line passes through \((0, -1)\) and \((6, 4)\text{.}\) The side above the line is shaded and \(R\) is below it, as in Figure 3.5.13. Write the inequality for \(R\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Solution.

    The gradient is \(\dfrac{4 - (-1)}{6 - 0} = \dfrac{5}{6}\) and the \(y\)-intercept is \(-1\text{,}\) so the line is \(y = \tfrac{5}{6}x - 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    It is dashed, so the sign is strict. \(R\) is below the line, and \((6, 0)\) is in \(R\text{:}\) \(0 \lt \tfrac{5}{6}(6) - 1 = 4\text{.}\) So \(R\) is \(y \lt \tfrac{5}{6}x - 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    With whole-number coefficients: multiply by \(6\text{,}\) \(6y \lt 5x - 6\text{,}\) that is \(5x - 6y \gt 6\text{.}\) Check \((6, 0)\text{:}\) \(30 \gt 6\text{,}\) true.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Inequalities Around Us.

    Inequalities describe the limits we live with every day.
    🔗
    • Matatus are fitted with speed governors that keep them at or below \(80\,\text{km/h}\text{.}\) Lower speeds give drivers more time to stop, and save lives.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Age limits: you must be at least \(18\) to get a driving licence or to vote.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Safe loads: a boda boda may carry only one passenger, and a lorry must not exceed its greatest permitted load.
      🔗
      🔗
    • A small business works within a budget and aims for a sales target: two inequalities in the numbers of items it buys and sells.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Health workers keep vaccines between \(2\,^{\circ}\text{C}\) and \(8\,^{\circ}\text{C}\text{,}\) a compound inequality.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Planning often means meeting several conditions at once. Drawing them as a region shows every choice that works, which is the starting point of linear programming.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    This answers the question we asked at the start of the chapter (Key Inquiry Questions 3.1.1): we use inequalities to write down rules and limits, to find every value or pair of values that obeys them, and to see those choices on a graph.
    🔗

    Exercises Exercises

    Exercise 3.5.1, Exercise 3.5.2 and Exercise 3.5.3 use the four panels of Figure 3.5.20.
    🔗

    1.

    Write the inequalities for \(R\) in panels (a) and (b).
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(-1 \le x \lt 4\text{;}\) (b) \(y \gt 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Write the inequality for \(R\) in panel (c).
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(3x + 7y \gt 21\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The line through \((7, 0)\) and \((0, 3)\) is \(3x + 7y = 21\text{,}\) dashed. \(R\) is away from the origin; the origin gives \(0\text{,}\) which is less than \(21\text{,}\) so \(R\) has \(3x + 7y \gt 21\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Write the inequality for \(R\) in panel (d).
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(7x - 2y \le 0\text{,}\) or \(2y \ge 7x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The line through \((0, 0)\) and \((2, 7)\) is \(y = \tfrac{7}{2}x\text{,}\) that is \(2y = 7x\text{,}\) solid. \((0, 1)\) is in \(R\text{:}\) \(2 \ge 0\text{,}\) so \(2y \ge 7x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Write the three inequalities for \(R\) in Figure 3.5.21.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(x \ge 1\text{,}\) \(y \gt 0\text{,}\) \(2x + 9y \le 18\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The vertical line is \(x = 1\text{,}\) solid, with the left side shaded: \(x \ge 1\text{.}\) The \(x\)-axis is dashed with the side below shaded: \(y \gt 0\text{.}\) The sloping line through \((9, 0)\) and \((0, 2)\) is \(\tfrac{x}{9} + \tfrac{y}{2} = 1\text{,}\) that is \(2x + 9y = 18\text{,}\) solid. The origin is on the clear side and \(0 \le 18\text{,}\) so \(2x + 9y \le 18\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    A graph has a boda boda rider’s age \(a\) years across (\(0\) to \(60\)) and the rider’s speed in town \(s\,\text{km/h}\) up (\(0\) to \(80\)). \(R\) is a clear rectangle: on the left the solid line \(a = 18\text{,}\) on top the solid line \(s = 50\text{,}\) at the bottom \(s = 0\text{.}\) Write the inequalities and say what rules they show.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(a \ge 18\) and \(0 \le s \le 50\text{:}\) riders must be at least \(18\) years old, and must keep to \(50\,\text{km/h}\) or less in town.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Challenge: A Clear Square.

    \(R\) is the square with corners \((1, 3)\text{,}\) \((5, 3)\text{,}\) \((5, 7)\) and \((1, 7)\text{.}\) Its left and bottom edges are solid and the other two edges are dashed. Write the inequalities that define \(R\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Use two compound inequalities.
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(1 \le x \lt 5\) and \(3 \le y \lt 7\text{.}\)
