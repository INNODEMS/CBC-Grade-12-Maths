    Subsection 8.2.2 Simple and Compound Interest Compared

    Upon completion of this lesson, learners will be able to:
    1. Know: simple and compound interest at the same rate give the same amount after one year; after that compound pulls ahead, and the gap widens.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Do: compare offers at different rates; find, by trial, the first whole year in which a target is passed.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Apply: choose a savings offer.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Learner Experience 8.2.20 Watching the Gap Grow.

    Ksh \(70\,000\) is saved for \(8\) years at \(9\%\) per annum. Use Figure 8.2.23 or a calculator.
    🔗
    1. Find the amount after \(8\) years with simple interest and with compound interest.
      🔗
      Solution.
      Simple: \(70\,000 + 8 \times 6300 = 120\,400\text{.}\) Compound: \(70\,000 \times 1.09^{8} = 139\,479\) to the nearest shilling.
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the gap between the two amounts after \(1\text{,}\) \(2\text{,}\) \(5\) and \(8\) years.
      🔗
      Solution.
      Ksh \(0\text{,}\) \(567\text{,}\) \(6204\) and \(19\,079\) (to the nearest shilling).
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Why is there no gap after one year?
      🔗
      Solution.
      In the first year both pay interest on the principal only. Compound interest starts to earn more only when interest has been added to the account.
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Move the rate slider to \(5\%\text{.}\) Does compound interest still pull ahead?
      🔗
      Solution.
      Yes, more slowly: the gap after \(8\) years is about Ksh \(5422\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Teacher Resource 8.2.21 Structured Instruction.

    Suggested Time
    🔗
    8 minutes
    🔗
    Objective
    🔗
    To compare the two kinds of interest and to decide between savings offers.
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaways & Teacher Connection.

    1. Connecting to Learner Work: the gap in part (b) was \(0\) after one year and grew every year after that.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Simple interest adds a fixed sum each year; compound interest multiplies by a fixed number each year.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. A higher simple rate can beat a lower compound rate over a short time; over a long time compound wins.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 8.2.22 Which Grows Faster?

    With simple interest the amount grows by the same number of shillings every year, so its graph against time is a straight line. With compound interest the amount is multiplied by the same number every year, so its graph is a curve that gets steeper. At the same rate the two are equal after one year; after that compound interest is always ahead, and the gap keeps growing.
    🔗

    Example 8.2.25 SACCO or Bank?

    Mumbi has Ksh \(80\,000\) to save. Her SACCO offers \(11\%\) per annum simple interest; a bank offers \(10\%\) per annum compound interest. Which is better over \(2\) years, over \(3\) years and over \(6\) years?
    🔗

    Solution.

    The SACCO adds \(8800\) a year; the bank multiplies by \(1.1\) a year.
    🔗
    Over \(2\) years the SACCO’s higher rate wins. From \(3\) years on, the bank’s compound interest is ahead, and the gap grows.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.2.27 How Many Years?

    A chama saves Ksh \(55\,000\) at \(9\%\) per annum compound interest. After how many whole years is the amount first more than Ksh \(85\,000\text{?}\)
    🔗

    Solution.

    Try values of \(n\) in \(55\,000 \times 1.09^n\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} n = 5\amp: \quad 55\,000 \times 1.09^5 = 84\,624.32\\ n = 6\amp: \quad 55\,000 \times 1.09^6 = 92\,240.51 \end{align*}
    The amount passes Ksh \(85\,000\) during the sixth year, so it is first more than Ksh \(85\,000\) after \(6\) whole years.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    Ksh \(32\,000\) is saved for \(5\) years at \(12\%\) per annum. Find the amount at simple interest and at compound interest, and the difference, to the nearest shilling.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Simple Ksh \(51\,200\text{;}\) compound Ksh \(56\,395\text{;}\) difference Ksh \(5195\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Ksh \(60\,000\) can be saved at \(13\%\) per annum simple interest or at \(12\%\) per annum compound interest. Which is better over \(2\) years, and which over \(8\) years?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(2\) years: \(75\,600\) against \(75\,264\text{,}\) simple. \(8\) years: \(122\,400\) against \(148\,558\text{,}\) compound.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Ksh \(30\,000\) for \(4\) years: is \(9\%\) per annum simple interest or \(8\%\) per annum compound interest better?
    🔗
    Answer.
    Compound, by about Ksh \(15\) (\(40\,800\) against \(40\,814.67\))
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    At \(8\%\) per annum compound interest, after how many whole years has money first doubled?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(10\) years
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(1.08^9 = 1.999\text{,}\) just short of \(2\text{;}\) \(1.08^{10} = 2.159\text{.}\) So the money has first doubled after \(10\) years, whatever the principal.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Ksh \(15\,000\) is saved at \(7.5\%\) per annum compound interest. After how many whole years is it first more than Ksh \(20\,000\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(4\) years
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    After \(3\) years: \(15\,000 \times 1.075^3 = 18\,634.45\text{.}\) After \(4\) years: \(15\,000 \times 1.075^4 = 20\,032.04\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Challenge: Why the Gap Starts in Year Two.

    Explain why simple and compound interest at the same rate give the same amount after \(1\) year, and show that after \(2\) years compound interest is ahead by exactly \(P\left(\dfrac{r}{100}\right)^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Expand \(P\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    Both pay \(\dfrac{Pr}{100}\) in year 1. In year 2 compound interest also pays \(r\%\) on the first year’s interest.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    After one year both amounts are \(P + \dfrac{Pr}{100}\text{.}\) After two years, compound gives \(P\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)^2 = P + \dfrac{2Pr}{100} + P\left(\dfrac{r}{100}\right)^2\) and simple gives \(P + \dfrac{2Pr}{100}\text{.}\) The difference is \(P\left(\dfrac{r}{100}\right)^2\text{:}\) the interest, at \(r\%\text{,}\) on the first year’s interest \(\dfrac{Pr}{100}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
