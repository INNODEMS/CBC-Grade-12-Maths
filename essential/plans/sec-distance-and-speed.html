    Teacher Resource 7.3.1 Problem-Solving and Discovery.

    Suggested Time
    🔗
    14 minutes
    🔗
    Objective
    🔗
    Learners find a ship’s speed in knots and in kilometres per hour from the positions of two ports and the times of leaving and arriving.
    🔗
    Teacher’s Role
    🔗
    Before part (b), remind learners that minutes are sixtieths of an hour: \(13\,\text{h}\ 45\,\text{min} = 13.75\,\text{h}\text{,}\) not \(13.45\,\text{h}\text{.}\) Introduce the word “knot” only in part (d).
    🔗
    🔗

    Learner Experience 7.3.2 The Ship to Dar es Salaam.

    A passenger ship sails due south from Mombasa to Dar es Salaam. Take both ports to be on the meridian \(39^\circ 30'\,\text{E}\text{,}\) with Mombasa at latitude \(4^\circ 03'\,\text{S}\) and Dar es Salaam at latitude \(6^\circ 48'\,\text{S}\text{.}\) The ship leaves Mombasa at 18:00 on Monday and arrives in Dar es Salaam at 07:45 on Tuesday.
    🔗
    1. How far does the ship sail, in nautical miles?
      🔗
      Solution.
      Both latitudes are south, so subtract: \(6^\circ 48' - 4^\circ 03' = 2^\circ 45' = 165'\text{.}\) Along a meridian \(1'\) is \(1\) nautical mile, so the ship sails \(165\,\text{nm}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    2. How long does the voyage take? Give your answer in hours as a decimal.
      🔗
      Solution.
      From 18:00 on Monday to midnight is \(6\,\text{h}\text{;}\) midnight to 07:45 is \(7\,\text{h}\ 45\,\text{min}\text{.}\) Total \(13\,\text{h}\ 45\,\text{min} = 13\tfrac{45}{60} = 13.75\,\text{h}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Find the ship’s average speed in nautical miles per hour.
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(165 \div 13.75 = 12\) nautical miles per hour.
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Sailors call a speed of \(1\) nautical mile per hour a knot. One nautical mile is about \(1.853\,\text{km}\text{.}\) Find the ship’s speed in kilometres per hour.
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(12 \times 1.853 = 22.2\,\text{km/h}\) (to 1 decimal place). The ship’s speed is \(12\,\text{knots}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Teacher Resource 7.3.3 Structured Instruction.

    Suggested Time
    🔗
    8 minutes
    🔗
    Objective
    🔗
    To formalise the knot, the speed formula and the conversions learners used in the activity.
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaways & Teacher Connection.

    1. Connecting to Learner Work: part (c) gave the speed in nautical miles per hour: that is the speed in knots.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Change hours and minutes to hours by dividing the minutes by \(60\text{.}\) Change a decimal part of an hour back to minutes by multiplying by \(60\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Knots to kilometres per hour: multiply by \(1.853\text{.}\) Kilometres per hour to knots: divide by \(1.853\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 7.3.4 Speed in Knots.

    A knot is a speed of one nautical mile per hour. Ships and aircraft give their speeds in knots because their charts measure distance in nautical miles.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \text{speed} \amp = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}, \amp \text{time} \amp = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}, \amp \text{distance} \amp = \text{speed} \times \text{time} \end{align*}
    with the distance in nautical miles, the time in hours and the speed in knots.
    🔗
    Since \(1\,\text{nm} = 1.853\,\text{km}\text{,}\) a speed of \(1\) knot is \(1.853\,\text{km/h}\text{.}\) Write a time in hours as a decimal before dividing: \(3\,\text{h}\ 20\,\text{min} = 3\tfrac{20}{60}\,\text{h} = 3.333\ldots\,\text{h}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.3.6 A Flight Along the Equator.

    An aircraft flies due west along the equator from longitude \(37^\circ\,\text{E}\) to longitude \(9^\circ\,\text{E}\text{.}\) The flight takes \(3\,\text{h}\ 30\,\text{min}\text{.}\) Find its speed in knots and in kilometres per hour.
    🔗

    Solution.

    Both longitudes are east, so the difference in longitude is \(37^\circ - 9^\circ = 28^\circ\text{.}\) The equator is a great circle, so the distance is \(28 \times 60 = 1680\,\text{nm}\text{.}\) The time is \(3.5\,\text{h}\text{.}\)
    \begin{equation*} \text{speed} = \frac{1680}{3.5} = 480\,\text{knots} = 480 \times 1.853 = 889.4\,\text{km/h} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.3.7 Sailing East Along a Parallel.

    A cargo ship leaves Mombasa, taken to be at \((4^\circ\,\text{S},\ 39^\circ 40'\,\text{E})\text{,}\) and sails due east along the parallel \(4^\circ\,\text{S}\) at \(15\,\text{knots}\) for \(30\) hours. Find its new position, to the nearest minute.
    🔗

    Solution.

    Step 1: distance. \(15 \times 30 = 450\,\text{nm}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 2: change in longitude. Along the parallel \(4^\circ\,\text{S}\text{,}\) one degree of longitude is \(60 \cos 4^\circ\,\text{nm}\) long. So
    \begin{equation*} \text{change in longitude} = \frac{450}{60 \cos 4^\circ} = 7.518^\circ = 7^\circ 31' \end{equation*}
    to the nearest minute (\(0.518 \times 60 = 31.1\)).
    🔗
    Step 3: new longitude. Sailing east increases an east longitude: \(39^\circ 40' + 7^\circ 31' = 47^\circ 11'\,\text{E}\text{.}\) The new position is \((4^\circ\,\text{S},\ 47^\circ 11'\,\text{E})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.3.8 From Kilometres per Hour to Knots.

    An airliner cruises at \(850\,\text{km/h}\text{.}\) Find its speed in knots, and the time it takes to fly \(1500\,\text{nm}\text{,}\) to the nearest minute.
    🔗

    Solution.

    \(850 \div 1.853 = 458.7\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) Time \(= 1500 \div 458.7 = 3.27\,\text{h}\text{.}\) The decimal part is \(0.27 \times 60 = 16\) minutes, so the flight takes \(3\,\text{h}\ 16\,\text{min}\text{.}\)

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    A ship sails along the meridian \(50^\circ\,\text{E}\) from latitude \(10^\circ\,\text{S}\) to latitude \(2^\circ\,\text{N}\) in \(2\) days \(12\) hours. Find its speed in knots and in kilometres per hour.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(12\,\text{knots}\text{;}\) \(22.2\,\text{km/h}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Opposite sides of the equator, so add: \(10^\circ + 2^\circ = 12^\circ = 720'\text{,}\) so \(720\,\text{nm}\text{.}\) Time \(2 \times 24 + 12 = 60\,\text{h}\text{.}\) Speed \(720 \div 60 = 12\,\text{knots}\) \(= 12 \times 1.853 = 22.2\,\text{km/h}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Convert \(520\,\text{knots}\) to kilometres per hour.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(963.6\,\text{km/h}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    One knot is \(1\,\text{nm}\) an hour, and \(1\,\text{nm} = 1.853\,\text{km}\text{.}\) So \(520 \times 1.853 = 963.56\text{,}\) which is \(963.6\,\text{km/h}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    A plane flies along the parallel \(60^\circ\,\text{N}\) from longitude \(10^\circ\,\text{E}\) to longitude \(40^\circ\,\text{E}\) at \(300\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) How long does the flight take?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(3\) hours
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Difference in longitude \(30^\circ = 1800'\text{.}\) Distance \(1800 \cos 60^\circ = 900\,\text{nm}\text{.}\) Time \(900 \div 300 = 3\,\text{h}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    A ship sails along the parallel \(20^\circ\,\text{S}\) from longitude \(30^\circ\,\text{E}\) to longitude \(50^\circ\,\text{E}\) in \(48\) hours. Find its speed in knots and in kilometres per hour, to 1 decimal place.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(23.5\,\text{knots}\text{;}\) \(43.5\,\text{km/h}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Difference in longitude \(20^\circ = 1200'\text{.}\) Distance \(1200 \cos 20^\circ = 1127.6\,\text{nm}\text{.}\) Speed \(1127.6 \div 48 = 23.5\,\text{knots}\text{;}\) \(23.49 \times 1.853 = 43.5\,\text{km/h}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. Challenge: A New Position.

    A ship at \((25^\circ\,\text{S},\ 80^\circ\,\text{E})\) sails due west along its parallel at \(18\,\text{knots}\) for \(40\) hours. Find its new position, to the nearest minute.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Find the distance first, then divide by the length of one degree of longitude on this parallel.
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \((25^\circ\,\text{S},\ 66^\circ 46'\,\text{E})\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Distance \(18 \times 40 = 720\,\text{nm}\text{.}\) Change in longitude \(720 \div (60 \cos 25^\circ) = 13.241^\circ = 13^\circ 14'\text{.}\) Sailing west decreases an east longitude: \(80^\circ - 13^\circ 14' = 66^\circ 46'\,\text{E}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
