Exercise 6. Challenge: A Square Off the Grid.

A square of side \(3.5\,\text{cm}\) is drawn with opposite corners at \((0.25, 0.25)\) and \((3.75, 3.75)\) on a grid of 1 cm squares. Count the full and part squares, estimate the area, and compare with the true area. Explain the difference.
🔗
Hint.
How much of each part square is covered?
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(F = 4\text{,}\) \(P = 12\text{,}\) estimate \(10\,\text{cm}^2\text{;}\) true area \(12.25\,\text{cm}^2\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The squares from \(1\) to \(3\) in both directions are full: \(2 \times 2 = 4\text{.}\) The other \(12\) squares of the \(4 \times 4\) block are part squares. Estimate \(4 + 6 = 10\,\text{cm}^2\text{;}\) true area \(3.5^2 = 12.25\,\text{cm}^2\text{.}\) Every part square here is more than half covered (the edge squares are \(\tfrac{3}{4}\) covered and the corner squares \(\tfrac{9}{16}\)), so counting each as \(\tfrac{1}{2}\) gives too little. The “half” works on average only when the edge cuts squares in all sorts of ways.
