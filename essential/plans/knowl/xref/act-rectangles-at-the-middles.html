Learner Experience 5.3.2 Rectangles at the Middles.

The region under \(y = x^2 + 2x + 3\) from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 4\) is cut into four strips of width \(1\text{,}\) as before. This time each strip is replaced by a rectangle whose height is the height of the curve at the middle of the strip.
🔗
  1. Write down the value of \(x\) at the middle of each strip.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(x = 0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Find the height of the curve at each of these values of \(x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(4.25, 8.25, 14.25, 22.25\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Find the total area of the four rectangles.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(1 \times (4.25 + 8.25 + 14.25 + 22.25) = 49\) square units.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Work in pairs. Use the mid-ordinate rule in the box after this activity with \(h = 1\) and check that you get the same answer as in part (c).
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(1 \times (4.25 + 8.25 + 14.25 + 22.25) = 49\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  5. The trapezoidal rule with the same strips gave \(50\text{,}\) and the exact area is \(49\tfrac{1}{3}\text{.}\) Compare the two estimates with the exact area.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The mid-ordinate estimate is too small by \(\tfrac{1}{3}\text{;}\) the trapezoidal estimate is too big by \(\tfrac{2}{3}\text{.}\) The exact area lies between them.
    🔗
