Exercise 6. Challenge: Doubling Your Money.

At what simple rate of interest does money double in \(8\) years? Explain why the principal does not matter.
🔗
Hint.
Doubling means the interest equals the principal: \(I = P\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(12.5\%\) per annum
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Money doubles when \(I = P\text{,}\) that is \(\dfrac{PRT}{100} = P\text{.}\) Dividing by \(P\) gives \(RT = 100\text{,}\) whatever \(P\) is. With \(T = 8\text{,}\) \(R = 100 \div 8 = 12.5\text{.}\)
