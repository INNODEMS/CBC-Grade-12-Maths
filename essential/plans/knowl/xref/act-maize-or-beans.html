Learner Experience 4.4.2 Maize or Beans?

A farmer in Trans Nzoia has \(10\) hectares of land for maize and beans this season. A hectare of maize needs \(7\) person-days of labour and a hectare of beans \(12\) person-days; she can hire \(100\) person-days in all. She has promised a buyer the beans from at least \(2\) hectares. She expects a profit of Ksh \(40\,000\) a hectare on maize and Ksh \(50\,000\) a hectare on beans. Let \(x\) be the number of hectares of maize and \(y\) the number of hectares of beans.
🔗
  1. Write the constraints and the profit \(P\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Land: \(x + y \le 10\text{.}\) Labour: \(7x + 12y \le 100\text{.}\) Beans: \(y \ge 2\text{.}\) Also \(x \ge 0\text{.}\) \(P = 40\,000x + 50\,000y\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    🔗
    Solution.
    As in Figure 4.4.3: four solid boundaries, with the unwanted side of each shaded.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Check your graph against Figure 4.4.3. Find the vertices of R exactly.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(x + y = 10\) and \(7x + 12y = 100\text{:}\) \(7(10 - y) + 12y = 100\text{,}\) so \(5y = 30\text{,}\) \(y = 6\text{,}\) \(x = 4\text{.}\) The others are \((0, 2)\text{,}\) \((8, 2)\) (where \(y = 2\) meets \(x + y = 10\)) and \(\left(0, \tfrac{25}{3}\right) \approx (0, 8.33)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Work out \(P\) at each vertex.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \((0, 2)\text{:}\) Ksh \(100\,000\text{.}\) \((8, 2)\text{:}\) Ksh \(420\,000\text{.}\) \((4, 6)\text{:}\) Ksh \(460\,000\text{.}\) \((0, 8.33)\text{:}\) about Ksh \(416\,667\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  5. What should the farmer plant? Check that your plan keeps every limit.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(4\) hectares of maize and \(6\) hectares of beans, for a profit of Ksh \(460\,000\text{.}\) Check: \(10\) hectares, \(28 + 72 = 100\) person-days, \(6 \ge 2\) hectares of beans.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  6. Must \(x\) and \(y\) be whole numbers here?
    🔗
    Solution.
    No: land can be divided, so a plan such as \(4.5\) hectares is possible. Here the best vertex happens to be a whole-number point anyway.
