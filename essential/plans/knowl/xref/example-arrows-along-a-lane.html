Example 6.7.11 Arrows Along a Lane.

A road crew paints arrows along a lane (Figure 6.7.10). On their plan, in metres, the tip of the first arrow is at \((3, 4)\text{,}\) and each next arrow is the previous one translated by \(\mathbf{T} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 15 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Find the tips of the second and third arrows, and the single translation that takes the first arrow to the third.
🔗

Solution.

Second tip: \((3, 4 + 15) = (3, 19)\text{.}\) Third tip: \((3, 19 + 15) = (3, 34)\text{.}\)
🔗
The first arrow goes to the third by \(\mathbf{T} + \mathbf{T} = 2\mathbf{T} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 30 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
🔗
Lane arrows tell drivers and riders which way each lane goes. Keeping to them prevents head-on crashes, so they are painted again and again along the road, each one a translation of the last.
