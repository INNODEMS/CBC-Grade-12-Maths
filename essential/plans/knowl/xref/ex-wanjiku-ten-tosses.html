Exercise 6. Challenge: Ten Tosses.

Wanjiku tossed a coin \(10\) times and got \(7\) heads. She says the coin is biased, with probability of a head \(0.7\text{.}\) Comment on her conclusion.
🔗
Hint.
What happens to a relative frequency as the number of trials increases?
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Ten tosses are too few: \(7\) heads in \(10\) is quite possible with a fair coin. She should toss it many more times.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
With few trials the relative frequency can be far from the probability. Run the simulator with \(10\) trials a few times: results such as \(0.7\) or \(0.3\) appear often for a fair coin. Only a large number of tosses would show whether the coin is biased.
