Example 10.2.12 Helmets on Boda Bodas.

At a junction in Nakuru, \(112\) of \(160\) boda boda riders observed were wearing a helmet. Estimate the probability that a rider at this junction (a) wears a helmet, (b) does not. (c) Of \(500\) riders passing the junction, about how many would you expect to have no helmet?
🔗

Solution.

(a) \(\dfrac{112}{160} = 0.7\text{.}\) (b) \(1 - 0.7 = 0.3\text{.}\) (c) \(500 \times 0.3 = 150\) riders.
🔗
A helmet greatly reduces the risk of a serious head injury in a crash, so a road-safety campaign might use figures like these to decide where to check riders.
