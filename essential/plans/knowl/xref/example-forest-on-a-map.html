Example 5.1.13 A Forest on a Map.

A forest block near Kakamega is drawn on a map of scale \(1 : 40\,000\) with a grid of 1 cm squares. Its outline covers \(18\) full squares and \(14\) part squares. Estimate the area of the forest in hectares (\(1 \text{ hectare} = 10\,000\,\text{m}^2\)).
🔗

Solution.

Step 1: the estimate in squares is \(18 + \tfrac{1}{2} \times 14 = 18 + 7 = 25\) squares.
🔗
Step 2: at a scale of \(1 : 40\,000\text{,}\) \(1\,\text{cm}\) on the map stands for \(40\,000\,\text{cm}\) \(= 400\,\text{m}\) on the ground. So one square on the map stands for a square of side \(400\,\text{m}\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} 400 \times 400 = 160\,000\,\text{m}^2 = 16 \text{ hectares} \end{equation*}
🔗
Step 3: the area is about \(25 \times 16 = 400\) hectares.
🔗
A square on the map stands for a square on the ground, so square the real length of its side. Multiplying the \(25\) squares by \(400\) alone would give a length, not an area.
🔗
