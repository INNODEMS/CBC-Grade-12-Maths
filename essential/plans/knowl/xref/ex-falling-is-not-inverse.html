Exercise 6. Challenge: Falling Is Not Enough.

Explain why “\(y\) goes down when \(x\) goes up” does not prove inverse proportion, and give a table that shows it.
🔗
Answer.
For example \(y = 20 - x\text{:}\) \(x = 1, 2, 4, 5\) give \(y = 19, 18, 16, 15\text{,}\) with products \(19, 36, 64, 75\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Inverse proportion needs \(x \times y\) to stay the same. In the table above \(y\) falls as \(x\) rises, but the products change, so it is not inverse proportion.
