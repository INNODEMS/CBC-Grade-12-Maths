Learner Experience 6.6.2 Measure, Then Calculate.

  1. Draw \(\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) \(\begin{pmatrix} -9 \\ 12 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\) with \(1\) square for \(1\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Measure the length of each arrow.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(10\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) \(15\,\text{cm}\) and about \(3.6\,\text{cm}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Each arrow, with its across move and its up move, makes a right-angled triangle. Use Pythagoras’ theorem to calculate each length.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10\text{;}\) \(\sqrt{81 + 144} = \sqrt{225} = 15\text{;}\) \(\sqrt{4 + 9} = \sqrt{13} \approx 3.61\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Does the minus sign in \(\begin{pmatrix} -9 \\ 12 \end{pmatrix}\) change the length?
    🔗
    Solution.
    No: \((-9)^2 = 81\text{,}\) the same as \(9^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Mark \(A(1, 2)\) and \(B(4, 6)\text{.}\) Write \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) and find its length.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}\text{;}\) length \(\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5\text{.}\)
