Inequalities Around Us.

Inequalities describe the limits we live with every day.
🔗
  • Matatus are fitted with speed governors that keep them at or below \(80\,\text{km/h}\text{.}\) Lower speeds give drivers more time to stop, and save lives.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Age limits: you must be at least \(18\) to get a driving licence or to vote.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Safe loads: a boda boda may carry only one passenger, and a lorry must not exceed its greatest permitted load.
    🔗
    🔗
  • A small business works within a budget and aims for a sales target: two inequalities in the numbers of items it buys and sells.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Health workers keep vaccines between \(2\,^{\circ}\text{C}\) and \(8\,^{\circ}\text{C}\text{,}\) a compound inequality.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Planning often means meeting several conditions at once. Drawing them as a region shows every choice that works, which is the starting point of linear programming.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
This answers the question we asked at the start of the chapter (Key Inquiry Questions 3.1.1): we use inequalities to write down rules and limits, to find every value or pair of values that obeys them, and to see those choices on a graph.
