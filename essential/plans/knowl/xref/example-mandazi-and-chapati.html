Example 3.2.6 Mandazi and Chapati.

Wanjiru sells mandazi and chapati. She makes a profit of Ksh \(4\) on each mandazi and Ksh \(9\) on each chapati. She wants a profit of at least Ksh \(360\) a day. Write an inequality for \(m\) mandazi and \(c\) chapati. Does selling \(45\) mandazi and \(20\) chapati meet her target? Does selling \(30\) mandazi and \(25\) chapati?
🔗

Solution.

The profit is \(4m + 9c\) shillings, and “at least” gives \(\ge\text{:}\) \(4m + 9c \ge 360\text{.}\)
🔗
\((45, 20)\text{:}\) \(4(45) + 9(20) = 180 + 180 = 360\text{,}\) and \(360 \ge 360\) is true: she meets the target exactly.
🔗
\((30, 25)\text{:}\) \(4(30) + 9(25) = 120 + 225 = 345\text{,}\) and \(345 \ge 360\) is false: she does not.
🔗
