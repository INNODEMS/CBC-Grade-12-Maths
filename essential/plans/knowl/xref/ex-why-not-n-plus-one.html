Exercise 5.

Use position \((n + 1)/4\) instead of \(n/4\) to find \(Q_1\) for the seedlings. Compare with your answer to Exercise 9.3.1.1. Why does this book use \(n/4\) for grouped data?
🔗
Answer.
\(20.25\,\text{cm}\) against \(20.17\,\text{cm}\text{:}\) a very small difference. With grouped data a position is a point on a continuous scale, not the number of a value in a list.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Position \(61/4 = 15.25\text{:}\) \(19.5 + \dfrac{15.25 - 13}{15} \times 5 = 20.25\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) The individual values are not known, so there is no 15th value to find; we are cutting a continuous scale at a quarter of the total frequency, which is \(n/4\text{.}\)
