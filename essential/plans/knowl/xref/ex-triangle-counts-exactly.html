Exercise 6. Challenge: When Counting Is Exact.

Count the unit squares under the line \(y = 2x\) from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 4\text{,}\) and compare the estimate with the area of the triangle. Explain why the estimate is exact here.
🔗
Hint.
Look at the two part squares in each column together.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(F = 12\text{,}\) \(P = 8\text{,}\) estimate \(16\text{,}\) the same as \(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 8 = 16\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
In the column from \(x = c\) to \(x = c + 1\) the line rises from \(2c\) to \(2c + 2\text{,}\) so it passes through two squares. The lower one is \(\tfrac{3}{4}\) covered and the upper one \(\tfrac{1}{4}\) covered: together they hold \(1\) square of area, exactly what \(2 \times \tfrac{1}{2}\) counts. Columns: full \(0, 2, 4, 6\text{,}\) so \(F = 12\text{;}\) \(P = 8\text{;}\) estimate \(12 + 4 = 16\text{.}\)
