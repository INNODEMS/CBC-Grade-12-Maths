Example 3.4.13 Vehicles for a School Trip.

In Example 3.2.7 the school needed \(7b + 2m \ge 28\) for \(b\) buses and \(m\) matatus. Only \(3\) buses and \(10\) matatus are available. Show the region and list the possible choices.
🔗

Solution.

The inequalities are \(7b + 2m \ge 28\text{,}\) \(b \le 3\) and \(m \le 10\text{,}\) all with solid boundaries. \(7b + 2m = 28\) crosses the axes at \((4, 0)\) and \((0, 14)\text{;}\) \((0, 0)\) gives \(0 \ge 28\text{,}\) false, so shade the side containing the origin. Shade to the right of \(b = 3\) and above \(m = 10\text{.}\)
🔗
The whole-number points in \(R\) are \(b = 2\) with \(m = 7, 8, 9, 10\text{,}\) and \(b = 3\) with \(m = 4, 5, \ldots, 10\text{:}\) \(11\) choices. For \(b = 0\) or \(1\) the school would need more than \(10\) matatus. \((2, 6)\) is not a choice: \(7(2) + 2(6) = 26 \lt 28\text{,}\) too few seats.
🔗
Which of the \(11\) choices is cheapest is a question for linear programming.
