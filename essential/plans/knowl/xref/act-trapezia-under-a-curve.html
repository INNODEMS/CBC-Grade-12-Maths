Learner Experience 5.2.2 Trapezia Under a Curve.

The region under the curve \(y = x^2 + 2x + 3\) from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 4\) is cut into four strips, each \(1\) unit wide. In each strip the curve is replaced by a straight line joining its two ends, so each strip becomes a trapezium.
🔗
  1. Copy and complete the table of values of \(y = x^2 + 2x + 3\) for \(x = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(y = 3, 6, 11, 18, 27\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Find the area of each strip as a trapezium.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 1 \times (3 + 6) = 4.5\text{,}\) then \(8.5\text{,}\) \(14.5\) and \(22.5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Add the four areas to estimate the area of the region.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(4.5 + 8.5 + 14.5 + 22.5 = 50\) square units.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Work in pairs. Use the trapezoidal rule in the box after this activity with \(h = 1\) and check that you get the same answer as in part (c).
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 1 \times [3 + 27 + 2(6 + 11 + 18)] = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 100 = 50\text{,}\) the same as part (c).
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  5. The exact area of the region is \(49\tfrac{1}{3}\text{.}\) Use the interactive in Figure 5.2.6 with \(8\) strips. Is the estimate closer?
    🔗
    Solution.
    With \(8\) strips the estimate is \(49.5\text{,}\) closer to \(49\tfrac{1}{3}\) than \(50\) is. Narrower strips hug the curve more closely.
