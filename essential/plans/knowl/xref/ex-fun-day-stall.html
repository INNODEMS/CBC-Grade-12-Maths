Exercise 6. Challenge: A Fun-Day Stall (Extension).

At a school fun-day stall you pay Ksh \(20\) to spin the spinner in Figure 10.2.26 and receive Ksh \(10\) times your score. Find the average amount paid out per spin in the long run. Does the stall make money?
🔗
Hint.
Imagine \(800\) spins. About how many of each score would there be?
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Ksh \(21.25\) per spin, so the stall loses Ksh \(1.25\) a spin on average.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
In \(800\) spins expect about \(300\) ones, \(200\) twos, \(200\) threes and \(100\) fours. The total score is \(300 + 400 + 600 + 400 = 1700\text{,}\) an average of \(1700 \div 800 = 2.125\) per spin. The stall pays out \(10 \times 2.125 = 21.25\) shillings a spin on average but takes only Ksh \(20\text{.}\) (The average score \(1 \times \frac{3}{8} + 2 \times \frac{1}{4} + 3 \times \frac{1}{4} + 4 \times \frac{1}{8}\) is called the expected value of \(X\text{.}\))
