Exercise 6. Challenge: Which Profit Keeps the Corner?

On the region of Exercise 4.3.1.5, the objective is changed to \(P = kx + y\text{,}\) where \(k \gt 0\text{.}\) For which values of \(k\) is \((7, 6)\) an optimal solution?
🔗
Hint.
Compare the values of \(P\) at \((8, 0)\text{,}\) \((7, 6)\) and \((0, 8)\text{:}\) \(8k\text{,}\) \(7k + 6\) and \(8\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(\tfrac{2}{7} \le k \le 6\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\((7, 6)\) is optimal when \(7k + 6 \ge 8k\) and \(7k + 6 \ge 8\) (the value at \((0, 0)\) is \(0\)). The first gives \(k \le 6\) and the second \(k \ge \tfrac{2}{7}\text{.}\) So \(\tfrac{2}{7} \le k \le 6\text{.}\) At either end two vertices tie.
