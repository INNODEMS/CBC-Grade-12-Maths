Learner Experience 4.2.27 Corners of the Fundraiser Region.

Use your graph of the fundraiser region R from Learner Experience 4.2.2, or Figure 4.2.28.
🔗
  1. Read the coordinates of the four corners of R from your graph.
    🔗
    Solution.
    About \((0, 30)\text{,}\) \((70, 30)\text{,}\) \((50, 50)\) and \((0, 72)\text{;}\) readings of the third corner vary from group to group.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The corner near \((50, 50)\) is where \(x + y = 100\) meets \(15x + 35y = 2520\text{.}\) Solve these two equations together.
    🔗
    Solution.
    From the first, \(x = 100 - y\text{.}\) Then \(15(100 - y) + 35y = 2520\text{,}\) so \(1500 + 20y = 2520\text{,}\) \(y = 51\) and \(x = 49\text{.}\) The corner is \((49, 51)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Find the other three corners exactly. Which two lines meet at each?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(x = 0\) and \(y = 30\) meet at \((0, 30)\text{.}\) \(y = 30\) and \(x + y = 100\) meet at \((70, 30)\text{.}\) \(x = 0\) and \(15x + 35y = 2520\) meet at \((0, 72)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Is each corner in R?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Yes: every boundary is solid, so points on the lines, and the corners where they meet, are allowed.
