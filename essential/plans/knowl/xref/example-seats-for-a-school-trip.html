Example 3.2.7 Seats for a School Trip.

A school takes \(196\) people on a trip. A bus has \(49\) seats and a matatu has \(14\) seats. Write an inequality for \(b\) buses and \(m\) matatus to carry everyone, and simplify it. Are \(2\) buses and \(7\) matatus enough? Are \(3\) buses and \(3\) matatus?
🔗

Solution.

The seats are \(49b + 14m\text{,}\) and there must be at least \(196\text{:}\) \(49b + 14m \ge 196\text{.}\) Every number is a multiple of \(7\text{,}\) so divide by \(7\text{:}\) \(7b + 2m \ge 28\text{.}\)
🔗
\((2, 7)\text{:}\) \(7(2) + 2(7) = 28\text{,}\) that is \(196\) seats: exactly enough.
🔗
\((3, 3)\text{:}\) \(7(3) + 2(3) = 27 \lt 28\text{,}\) that is \(189\) seats: not enough.
