Example 4.4.24 Feeding Learners Without Waste.

The kitchen of a boarding school in Kitui cooks lunch for \(620\) learners. A pot of githeri serves \(70\) learners and a pot of rice serves \(45\text{.}\) A pot of githeri needs \(7\) bundles of firewood and a pot of rice \(2\) bundles; the kitchen has \(50\) bundles. Any food beyond \(620\) servings is wasted. How many pots of each should the cooks prepare?
🔗

Solution.

Let \(x\) be the number of pots of githeri and \(y\) the number of pots of rice. Enough food: \(70x + 45y \ge 620\text{.}\) Firewood: \(7x + 2y \le 50\text{.}\) Also \(x \ge 0\text{,}\) \(y \ge 0\text{,}\) whole numbers. The servings left over are \(W = 70x + 45y - 620\text{;}\) minimise \(W\text{.}\)
🔗
Every plan on the line \(70x + 45y = 620\) wastes nothing, so look for a whole-number point on it. Dividing by \(5\text{,}\) \(14x + 9y = 124\text{.}\) Try \(x = 0, 1, 2, \ldots\text{:}\) \(124 - 14x\) must be a multiple of \(9\text{.}\) Only \(x = 5\) works (\(124 - 70 = 54\)), giving \(y = 6\text{.}\) Firewood: \(35 + 12 = 47 \le 50\text{,}\) so \((5, 6)\) is in R.
🔗
The cooks should prepare \(5\) pots of githeri and \(6\) pots of rice: exactly \(350 + 270 = 620\) servings, with nothing wasted. By comparison, rice alone needs \(14\) pots (\(630\) servings, \(10\) wasted), and \((2, 11)\) wastes \(15\text{.}\)
