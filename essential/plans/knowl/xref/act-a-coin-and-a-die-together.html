Learner Experience 10.1.22 A Coin and a Die Together.

Toss a coin and roll a die at the same time.
🔗
  1. List all the possible outcomes, such as H1 (head and 1). How many are there?
    🔗
    Solution.
    H1, H2, H3, H4, H5, H6, T1, T2, T3, T4, T5, T6: \(12\) outcomes, \(2 \times 6\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Find the probability of a head and a 6.
    🔗
    Solution.
    One outcome, H6: \(\frac{1}{12}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Find the probability of a tail and an even number.
    🔗
    Solution.
    T2, T4, T6: \(\frac{3}{12} = \frac{1}{4}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Now roll two dice and add the scores. Draw a grid of all the totals. How many outcomes are there?
    🔗
    Solution.
    A \(6 \times 6\) grid: \(36\) outcomes (see Figure 10.1.25).
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Otieno says: “There are 11 possible totals, from 2 to 12, so the probability of a total of 7 is \(\frac{1}{11}\text{.}\)” Is he right?
    🔗
    Solution.
    No. The totals are not equally likely: six of the \(36\) cells have total 7, so \(P(7) = \frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}\text{,}\) while only one cell has total 2.
