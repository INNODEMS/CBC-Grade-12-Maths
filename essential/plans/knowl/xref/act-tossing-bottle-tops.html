Learner Experience 10.2.2 Tossing Bottle Tops.

Work in groups of four with one soda bottle top. A toss lands either printed side up or printed side down.
🔗
  1. Toss the bottle top \(50\) times. Tally how often it lands printed side up and printed side down.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Results vary. One group recorded \(31\) printed side up and \(19\) down.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Work out the fraction of your tosses that landed printed side up, as a decimal.
    🔗
    Solution.
    For that group, \(\frac{31}{50} = 0.62\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Add up the results of the whole class and work out the fraction again. Which estimate do you trust more, and why?
    🔗
    Solution.
    One class recorded \(236\) printed side up in \(400\) tosses: \(\frac{236}{400} = 0.59\text{.}\) The class figure is more trustworthy because it uses more tosses.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Is the probability of landing printed side up equal to \(\frac{1}{2}\text{?}\) Why can we not find it by counting outcomes, as we did for a coin?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Probably not: the two sides are different shapes, so the outcomes are not equally likely. Counting outcomes only works when they are equally likely, so we must estimate by experiment.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Use the class estimate to predict how many times the bottle top would land printed side up in \(1000\) tosses.
    🔗
    Solution.
    About \(1000 \times 0.59 = 590\) times.
    🔗
