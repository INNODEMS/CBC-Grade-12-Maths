🔗
  1. Sort the cards into three piles: events that cannot both happen; events where one does not affect the other; and neither.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Cannot both happen (mutually exclusive): 1 and 4. Do not affect each other (independent): 2, 6 and 8. Neither: 3, 5 and 7. For card 5, the score 2 is both even and less than 4, and of the scores 1, 2, 3 only one in three is even, compared with one in two overall.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. For each card in the first pile, explain why the two events cannot be independent.
    🔗
    Solution.
    If one happens, the other cannot: knowing one has happened changes the probability of the other to 0.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Achieng travels to school by exactly one of: walking, matatu or boda boda, with probabilities \(0.5\text{,}\) \(0.35\) and \(0.15\text{.}\) Find the probability that she uses a motor vehicle. Which choice would a road-safety campaign most want her to avoid, and why?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The ways are mutually exclusive: \(0.35 + 0.15 = 0.5\text{.}\) Open answer: many learners will say the boda boda, as riders and passengers are exposed and often travel without helmets; accept any reasoned answer.
