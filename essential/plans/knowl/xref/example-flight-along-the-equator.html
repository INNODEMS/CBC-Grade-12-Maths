Example 7.3.6 A Flight Along the Equator.

An aircraft flies due west along the equator from longitude \(37^\circ\,\text{E}\) to longitude \(9^\circ\,\text{E}\text{.}\) The flight takes \(3\,\text{h}\ 30\,\text{min}\text{.}\) Find its speed in knots and in kilometres per hour.
🔗

Solution.

Both longitudes are east, so the difference in longitude is \(37^\circ - 9^\circ = 28^\circ\text{.}\) The equator is a great circle, so the distance is \(28 \times 60 = 1680\,\text{nm}\text{.}\) The time is \(3.5\,\text{h}\text{.}\)
\begin{equation*} \text{speed} = \frac{1680}{3.5} = 480\,\text{knots} = 480 \times 1.853 = 889.4\,\text{km/h} \end{equation*}
