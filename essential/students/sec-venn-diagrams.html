    Subsection 1.5.1 Venn Diagrams with Two Sets

    Learner Experience 1.5.1 Multiples of Two and Three.

    \(\xi = \{1, 2, \ldots, 15\}\text{,}\) \(T\) is the set of multiples of \(2\) in \(\xi\) and \(H\) is the set of multiples of \(3\) in \(\xi\text{.}\)
    🔗
    1. Copy the diagram and write each number from \(1\) to \(15\) in the region where it belongs.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find \(n(T)\text{,}\) \(n(H)\text{,}\) \(n(T \cap H)\) and \(n(T \cup H)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Find a rule that links the four numbers in part (b).
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 1.5.3 Two-Set Venn Diagrams.

    In a Venn diagram the universal set \(\xi\) is a rectangle and each set is a circle, labelled outside the circle. Elements, or counts of elements, are written in the regions. In this book the four regions of a two-set diagram are numbered as in Figure 1.5.4: region \(1\) is in \(A\) only, region \(2\) is in both, region \(3\) is in \(B\) only and region \(4\) is outside both. So \(A\) is regions \(\{1, 2\}\text{,}\) \(B\) is \(\{2, 3\}\) and \(A \cup B\) is \(\{1, 2, 3\}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    For counting, fill the region that belongs to the most sets first, then work outwards. Adding \(n(A)\) and \(n(B)\) counts region \(2\) twice, so
    \begin{equation*} n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.5.5 Complements by Region.

    Give the region numbers of \(A'\text{,}\) \(B'\text{,}\) \((A \cup B)'\) and \(A' \cap B'\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(A' = \{3, 4\}\) and \(B' = \{1, 4\}\text{.}\) \(A \cup B = \{1, 2, 3\}\text{,}\) so \((A \cup B)' = \{4\}\text{.}\) \(A' \cap B' = \{3, 4\} \cap \{1, 4\} = \{4\}\text{.}\) These are two ways to describe the outside of both circles.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.5.6 Agriculture and Computer Studies.

    In a class of \(45\) learners, \(28\) take Agriculture, \(22\) take Computer Studies and \(9\) take both. How many take Agriculture only, Computer Studies only, and neither? Find \(n(A \cup C)\) by the formula.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Fill the overlap first: \(9\text{.}\) Agriculture only \(= 28 - 9 = 19\text{;}\) Computer Studies only \(= 22 - 9 = 13\text{;}\) neither \(= 45 - (19 + 9 + 13) = 4\text{.}\) By the formula, \(n(A \cup C) = 28 + 22 - 9 = 41 = 45 - 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.5.8 Finding the Overlap.

    In a youth group of \(38\text{,}\) \(25\) have a smartphone, \(21\) have a bicycle and \(5\) have neither. How many have both?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(n(S \cup B) = 38 - 5 = 33\text{.}\) Then \(n(S \cap B) = n(S) + n(B) - n(S \cup B) = 25 + 21 - 33 = 13\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 1.5.12 Three-Set Venn Diagrams.

    A three-set diagram has eight regions, numbered as in Figure 1.5.13: \(1\) in \(A\) only, \(2\) in \(A\) and \(B\) only, \(3\) in \(B\) only, \(4\) in \(A\) and \(C\) only, \(5\) in all three, \(6\) in \(B\) and \(C\) only, \(7\) in \(C\) only, \(8\) outside all three.
    🔗
    When a survey gives the set totals, the pair totals and the number in all three, fill the region that belongs to the most sets first, then work outwards:
    1. Write the number in all three in the centre.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Each “exactly two” region is its pair total minus the centre.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Each “only” region is its set total minus its two “exactly two” regions minus the centre.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. The number in none is the total minus the seven inner regions.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Check every set total.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.5.15 Placing Numbers.

    \(\xi = \{1, 2, \ldots, 15\}\text{,}\) \(A\) is the set of odd numbers, \(B\) the set of prime numbers and \(C\) the set of factors of \(70\) in \(\xi\text{.}\) List the elements in each of the eight regions.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(A = \{1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15\}\text{,}\) \(B = \{2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13\}\text{,}\) \(C = \{1, 2, 5, 7, 10, 14\}\text{.}\) Start with the centre.
    🔗
    Region \(5\) (all three): \(\{5, 7\}\text{.}\) Region \(2\text{:}\) \(\{3, 11, 13\}\text{.}\) Region \(4\text{:}\) \(\{1\}\text{.}\) Region \(6\text{:}\) \(\{2\}\text{.}\) Region \(1\text{:}\) \(\{9, 15\}\text{.}\) Region \(3\text{:}\) \(\varnothing\text{.}\) Region \(7\text{:}\) \(\{10, 14\}\text{.}\) Region \(8\text{:}\) \(\{4, 6, 8, 12\}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Region \(3\) is empty: every prime in \(\xi\) is odd or a factor of \(70\text{.}\) An empty region is still drawn.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.5.16 Crops in Bungoma.

    An agricultural officer asked \(65\) farmers in Bungoma which of maize, beans and bananas they grow. \(35\) grow maize, \(29\) beans and \(24\) bananas; \(15\) grow maize and beans, \(11\) maize and bananas, and \(10\) beans and bananas; \(6\) grow all three. How many grow maize only? How many grow exactly two of the crops? How many grow none of them?
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Centre: \(6\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Exactly two: maize and beans only \(15 - 6 = 9\text{;}\) maize and bananas only \(11 - 6 = 5\text{;}\) beans and bananas only \(10 - 6 = 4\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Only one: maize \(35 - 9 - 5 - 6 = 15\text{;}\) beans \(29 - 9 - 4 - 6 = 10\text{;}\) bananas \(24 - 5 - 4 - 6 = 9\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. None: the seven inner regions add up to \(58\text{,}\) so none \(= 65 - 58 = 7\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Check: maize \(15 + 9 + 5 + 6 = 35\text{,}\) beans \(9 + 10 + 4 + 6 = 29\text{,}\) bananas \(5 + 4 + 9 + 6 = 24\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    So \(15\) grow maize only, \(9 + 5 + 4 = 18\) grow exactly two crops and \(7\) grow none.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 1.5.18 Reading the Diagram.

    Use Figure 1.5.17. How many farmers grow at least two of the crops? Exactly one? Beans but not bananas?
    🔗
    Solution.
    At least two: \(9 + 5 + 4 + 6 = 24\text{.}\) Exactly one: \(15 + 10 + 9 = 34\text{.}\) Beans but not bananas: beans only and maize and beans only, \(10 + 9 = 19\text{.}\)
    🔗
