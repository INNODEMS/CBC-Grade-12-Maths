    Learner Experience 3.2.1 Shopping for School.

    Exercise books cost Ksh \(60\) each and pens cost Ksh \(25\) each. Achieng has Ksh \(500\) to spend.
    🔗
    1. What do \(3\) books and \(4\) pens cost? Can she afford them?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Write an expression for the cost of \(x\) books and \(y\) pens.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Write an inequality that says she can afford them.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Test the pairs \((5, 8)\text{,}\) \((6, 6)\) and \((4, 10)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    5. She needs at least \(3\) books. Write this as an inequality.
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Give two more pairs that meet both conditions.
      🔗
      🔗
    7. Why must \(x\) and \(y\) be whole numbers that are not negative?
      🔗
      🔗

    Key Takeaway 3.2.2 Inequalities in Two Unknowns.

    An inequality such as \(60x + 25y \le 500\) is a linear inequality in two unknowns. The sign can be any of \(\lt\text{,}\) \(\le\text{,}\) \(\gt\) or \(\ge\text{.}\)
    🔗
    A solution is a pair of values \((x, y)\) that makes the inequality true. To test a pair, substitute both values and see whether the statement is true. There are usually many solutions.
    🔗
    When \(x\) and \(y\) count things, they are whole numbers and cannot be negative, so we also write \(x \ge 0\) and \(y \ge 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    We write inequalities with whole-number coefficients in their simplest form: if every number has a common factor, divide by it. Dividing both sides by a positive number does not change which pairs make the inequality true.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.2.3 Mandazi and Chapati.

    Wanjiru sells mandazi and chapati. She makes a profit of Ksh \(4\) on each mandazi and Ksh \(9\) on each chapati. She wants a profit of at least Ksh \(360\) a day. Write an inequality for \(m\) mandazi and \(c\) chapati. Does selling \(45\) mandazi and \(20\) chapati meet her target? Does selling \(30\) mandazi and \(25\) chapati?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The profit is \(4m + 9c\) shillings, and “at least” gives \(\ge\text{:}\) \(4m + 9c \ge 360\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \((45, 20)\text{:}\) \(4(45) + 9(20) = 180 + 180 = 360\text{,}\) and \(360 \ge 360\) is true: she meets the target exactly.
    🔗
    \((30, 25)\text{:}\) \(4(30) + 9(25) = 120 + 225 = 345\text{,}\) and \(345 \ge 360\) is false: she does not.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.2.4 Seats for a School Trip.

    A school takes \(196\) people on a trip. A bus has \(49\) seats and a matatu has \(14\) seats. Write an inequality for \(b\) buses and \(m\) matatus to carry everyone, and simplify it. Are \(2\) buses and \(7\) matatus enough? Are \(3\) buses and \(3\) matatus?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The seats are \(49b + 14m\text{,}\) and there must be at least \(196\text{:}\) \(49b + 14m \ge 196\text{.}\) Every number is a multiple of \(7\text{,}\) so divide by \(7\text{:}\) \(7b + 2m \ge 28\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \((2, 7)\text{:}\) \(7(2) + 2(7) = 28\text{,}\) that is \(196\) seats: exactly enough.
    🔗
    \((3, 3)\text{:}\) \(7(3) + 2(3) = 27 \lt 28\text{,}\) that is \(189\) seats: not enough.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.2.5 Three Times as Many.

    A juice stall sells \(m\) cups of mango juice and \(p\) cups of passion juice a day. It always sells at least three times as many cups of mango as of passion. Write an inequality. Do \(20\) mango and \(6\) passion fit? Do \(15\) mango and \(6\) passion?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Three times the passion cups is \(3p\text{,}\) and the mango cups are at least that: \(m \ge 3p\text{.}\)
    🔗
    A common mistake is to write \(3m \ge p\text{.}\) Check with a pair that fits the words, \(m = 9\text{,}\) \(p = 3\text{:}\) \(9 \ge 3(3)\) is true, as it should be. Now try \(m = 4\text{,}\) \(p = 3\text{,}\) which does not fit the words: \(4 \ge 9\) is false for \(m \ge 3p\text{,}\) but \(12 \ge 3\) is true for \(3m \ge p\text{,}\) so \(3m \ge p\) is wrong.
    🔗
    \((20, 6)\text{:}\) \(20 \ge 18\text{,}\) yes. \((15, 6)\text{:}\) \(15 \ge 18\) is false, no.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Type \(\le\) as <= and \(\ge\) as >=, for example 3x+10y>=30. Use the letters x and y as defined in the question. Type fractions, not decimals, for example y<=2x/3+1.

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    Adult tickets for a school play cost Ksh \(200\) and learner tickets cost Ksh \(70\text{.}\) The drama club needs takings of at least Ksh \(7000\text{.}\) Write an inequality for \(a\) adult and \(l\) learner tickets, and simplify it.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(200a + 70l \ge 7000\text{,}\) which simplifies to \(20a + 7l \ge 700\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Takings are \(200a\) from adults and \(70l\) from learners. “At least” gives \(\ge\text{:}\) \(200a + 70l \ge 7000\text{.}\) Divide every term by \(10\text{:}\) \(20a + 7l \ge 700\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Daily data bundles cost Ksh \(20\) and weekly bundles cost Ksh \(99\text{.}\) Otieno spends at most Ksh \(500\) a month on \(x\) daily and \(y\) weekly bundles. Write the inequality.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(20x + 99y \le 500\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    For the school play in Exercise 3.2.1, which of these pairs \((a, l)\) meet the target: \((30, 20)\text{,}\) \((25, 25)\text{,}\) \((28, 20)\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \((30, 20)\text{:}\) Ksh \(7400\text{,}\) yes. \((25, 25)\text{:}\) Ksh \(6750\text{,}\) no. \((28, 20)\text{:}\) Ksh \(7000\text{,}\) yes.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Substitute into \(200a + 70l\text{.}\) \((30, 20)\text{:}\) \(6000 + 1400 = 7400 \ge 7000\text{,}\) yes. \((25, 25)\text{:}\) \(5000 + 1750 = 6750\text{,}\) less than \(7000\text{,}\) no. \((28, 20)\text{:}\) \(5600 + 1400 = 7000\text{;}\) the target is “at least”, so exactly \(7000\) meets it, yes.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    A choir has at least \(3\) girls for every \(2\) boys. Write an inequality for \(g\) girls and \(b\) boys.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(2g \ge 3b\) (or \(g \ge \frac{3}{2}b\))
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    A lorry driver travels for \(p\) hours at \(80\,\text{km/h}\) on the highway and \(q\) hours at \(30\,\text{km/h}\) through towns. The driver covers at most \(360\,\text{km}\) in a day. Write the inequality and simplify it.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(80p + 30q \le 360\text{,}\) which simplifies to \(8p + 3q \le 36\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Distance is speed times time, so the distance is \(80p + 30q\,\text{km}\text{.}\) Divide every number by \(10\text{.}\) Keeping to the lower limit in towns protects pedestrians and other road users.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Challenge: The Same Inequality.

    Show that \(6x + 14y \le 42\) and \(3x + 7y \le 21\) have exactly the same solutions.
    🔗
    Hint.
    What happens when you divide by \(2\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    Dividing both sides of the first by the positive number \(2\) gives the second, so a pair makes one true exactly when it makes the other true.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    For any pair, \(6x + 14y = 2(3x + 7y)\) and \(42 = 2 \times 21\text{.}\) Dividing both sides of an inequality by a positive number keeps it true or keeps it false, so the two inequalities are true for exactly the same pairs.
