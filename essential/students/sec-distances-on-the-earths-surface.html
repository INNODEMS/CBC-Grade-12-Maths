    Subsection 7.2.1 Distance Along a Great Circle

    Learner Experience 7.2.1 How Wide Is Kenya at the Equator?

    The equator enters Kenya from Uganda at about \(34^\circ\,\text{E}\) and leaves it into Somalia at about \(41^\circ\,\text{E}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    1. Through what angle of longitude does the equator cross Kenya?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. The equator is a great circle of radius \(6370\,\text{km}\text{.}\) Find its length, then the length of an arc of \(7^\circ\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Sailors and pilots measure distance in minutes of arc. How many minutes are there in \(7^\circ\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. An arc of one minute on a great circle is called a nautical mile, and is about \(1.853\,\text{km}\text{.}\) Change \(420\) nautical miles to kilometres and compare with your answer to (b).
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 7.2.2 Distance Along a Great Circle.

    A nautical mile (nm) is the length of an arc of a great circle that subtends one minute (\(1'\)) at the centre of the earth. So along a great circle the distance in nautical miles is the angle at the centre in minutes, and \(1^\circ = 60\,\text{nm}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    For an angle \(\theta^\circ\) at the centre, the distance along a great circle is
    \begin{equation*} \text{distance} = 60\theta\,\text{nm} \qquad\text{or}\qquad \text{distance} = \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi R\,\text{km}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    With \(R = 6370\,\text{km}\) and \(\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}\text{,}\) \(1^\circ = 60\,\text{nm} = 111.2\,\text{km}\) and \(1\,\text{nm} = 40\,040 \div 21\,600 = 1.8537\ldots\,\text{km}\text{,}\) which this chapter takes as \(1.853\,\text{km}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Along a meridian, \(\theta\) is the difference in latitude. Along the equator, \(\theta\) is the difference in longitude, taking the smaller of the two ways round.
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 7.2.3 The International Nautical Mile.

    Since 1929 the international nautical mile has been defined as exactly \(1.852\,\text{km}\text{.}\) With the values of this chapter we get \(1.8537\,\text{km}\text{,}\) which we use as \(1.853\,\text{km}\text{.}\) That agrees with \(1.852\,\text{km}\) to about \(1\) part in \(2000\text{,}\) so either gives the same answers to the accuracy we need.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.2.4 Garissa to Mombasa.

    Take Garissa \((0^\circ 27'\,\text{S})\) and Mombasa \((4^\circ 03'\,\text{S})\) to be on the same meridian, \(39^\circ 40'\,\text{E}\text{.}\) Find the distance between them along the meridian in nautical miles and in kilometres.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Both are south of the equator, so subtract: \(4^\circ 03' - 0^\circ 27' = 3^\circ 63' - 0^\circ 27' = 3^\circ 36'\text{.}\) In minutes, \(3 \times 60 + 36 = 216'\text{,}\) so the distance is \(216\,\text{nm}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    In kilometres, \(216 \times 1.853 = 400.2\,\text{km}\text{.}\) By arc length, \(3^\circ 36' = 3.6^\circ\) and \(\tfrac{3.6}{360} \times 40\,040 = 400.4\,\text{km}\text{.}\) Both are fine: the first uses the rounded value \(1.853\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.2.5 Across the Equator.

    A survey aircraft flies along the meridian \(38^\circ 45'\,\text{E}\) from Addis Ababa \((9^\circ 02'\,\text{N})\) to latitude \(6^\circ 48'\,\text{S}\text{.}\) How far does it fly, in nautical miles and in kilometres?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The equator lies between the two points, so add: \(9^\circ 02' + 6^\circ 48' = 15^\circ 50' = 950'\text{.}\) The distance is \(950\,\text{nm}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    In kilometres, \(15^\circ 50' = 15\tfrac{5}{6}^\circ\) and \(15\tfrac{5}{6} \div 360 \times 40\,040 = 1761.0\,\text{km}\) (or \(950 \times 1.853 = 1760.4\,\text{km}\)).
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.2.6 Along the Equator, the Short Way.

    Two points on the equator are at \(37^\circ\,\text{E}\) and \(150^\circ\,\text{W}\text{.}\) Find the shorter distance between them along the equator.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The longitudes are on opposite sides of Greenwich, so add: \(37^\circ + 150^\circ = 187^\circ\text{.}\) This is more than \(180^\circ\text{,}\) so the other way round, across the \(180^\circ\) meridian, is shorter: \(360^\circ - 187^\circ = 173^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Distance \(= 173 \times 60 = 10\,380\,\text{nm}\text{,}\) or \(\tfrac{173}{360} \times 40\,040 = 19\,241.4\,\text{km}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.2.7 Sailing Due South.

    A ship leaves Mombasa \((4^\circ 03'\,\text{S},\ 39^\circ 40'\,\text{E})\) and sails \(300\,\text{nm}\) due south. Find its new position.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Sailing due south keeps the ship on its meridian. \(300\,\text{nm}\) is \(300' = 5^\circ\) of latitude, and the ship moves further from the equator: \(4^\circ 03' + 5^\circ = 9^\circ 03'\text{.}\) The new position is \((9^\circ 03'\,\text{S},\ 39^\circ 40'\,\text{E})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Type decimals with a point, for example 36.8. Type angles in degrees as numbers, without the degree sign. Choose N, S, E or W from each list. Take \(\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}\) and \(R = 6370\,\text{km}\text{.}\) Distances in km within 0.1% of the exact value are accepted.

    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(120^\circ + 100^\circ = 220^\circ\text{,}\) more than \(180^\circ\text{,}\) so the shorter arc is \(360^\circ - 220^\circ = 140^\circ = 8400'\text{:}\) \(8400\,\text{nm}\text{.}\) \(\tfrac{140}{360} \times 40\,040 = 15\,571.1\,\text{km}\text{.}\)

    5.

    A dhow leaves Lamu \((2^\circ 16'\,\text{S},\ 40^\circ 54'\,\text{E})\) and sails \(450\,\text{nm}\) due north. Find its new position.

    Answer.

    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(450' = 7^\circ 30'\) of latitude northwards. The first \(2^\circ 16'\) take the dhow to the equator, and the remaining \(7^\circ 30' - 2^\circ 16' = 5^\circ 14'\) take it north of it. The new position is \((5^\circ 14'\,\text{N},\ 40^\circ 54'\,\text{E})\text{.}\)

    6. Challenge: Latitude from a Distance.

    A research ship leaves Mogadishu, taken to be at \((2^\circ 03'\,\text{N},\ 45^\circ 19'\,\text{E})\text{,}\) and sails \(900\,\text{km}\) due south. Find its new position, to the nearest minute.
    🔗
    Hint.
    \(900\,\text{km}\) is \(900 \div 40\,040\) of a full turn round a great circle.
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \((6^\circ 03'\,\text{S},\ 45^\circ 19'\,\text{E})\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The ship moves along its meridian through \(\theta = \tfrac{900}{40\,040} \times 360 = 8.092^\circ\text{,}\) and \(0.092 \times 60 = 5.5\text{,}\) so \(8^\circ 06'\) to the nearest minute. The first \(2^\circ 03'\) take it to the equator; the remaining \(8^\circ 06' - 2^\circ 03' = 6^\circ 03'\) take it south of the equator. New position \((6^\circ 03'\,\text{S},\ 45^\circ 19'\,\text{E})\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 7.2.12 Distance Along a Parallel.

    The parallel of latitude \(\phi\) is a small circle of radius
    \begin{equation*} r = R\cos\phi \end{equation*}
    (Figure 7.2.13). It shrinks to a point at the pole.
    🔗
    Two points on this parallel whose difference in longitude is \(\theta^\circ\) are the same angle apart as on the equator, but on a smaller circle, so multiply by \(\cos\phi\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \text{distance} = \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi R\cos\phi\,\text{km} \qquad\text{or}\qquad \text{distance} = 60\theta\cos\phi\,\text{nm}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    In the second form, \(60\theta\) is the difference in longitude in minutes.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.2.15 Using the Radius of a Parallel.

    Find the radius of the parallel of latitude \(45^\circ\,\text{S}\) and the length of the whole parallel.
    🔗
    Solution.
    From Figure 7.2.13, the radius is \(r = R\cos\phi = 6370 \cos 45^\circ = 4504\,\text{km}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The length of the parallel is \(2\pi r = 2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 4504 = 28\,311\,\text{km}\text{.}\) (Working without rounding the radius, \(40\,040 \cos 45^\circ = 28\,313\,\text{km}\text{.}\))
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.2.16 Mombasa to the Seychelles.

    Take Mombasa \((39^\circ 40'\,\text{E})\) and Victoria in the Seychelles \((55^\circ 27'\,\text{E})\) both to be on the parallel \(4^\circ\,\text{S}\text{.}\) Find the distance between them along the parallel in nautical miles and in kilometres.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Difference in longitude: \(55^\circ 27' - 39^\circ 40' = 54^\circ 87' - 39^\circ 40' = 15^\circ 47' = 947'\text{.}\)
    🔗
    In nautical miles: \(947 \cos 4^\circ = 944.7\,\text{nm}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    In kilometres: \(15\tfrac{47}{60} \div 360 \times 40\,040 \times \cos 4^\circ = 1751.2\,\text{km}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.2.17 A Research Ship in the Southern Ocean.

    A research ship sails along the parallel \(45^\circ\,\text{S}\) from \(30^\circ\,\text{E}\) to \(75^\circ\,\text{E}\text{.}\) How far does it sail?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Difference in longitude \(45^\circ = 2700'\text{.}\) Distance \(= 2700 \cos 45^\circ = 1909.2\,\text{nm}\text{,}\) or \(\tfrac{45}{360} \times 40\,040 \times \cos 45^\circ = 3539.1\,\text{km}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.2.18 Over the Pole or Along the Parallel?

    P is at \((60^\circ\,\text{N},\ 40^\circ\,\text{E})\) and Q at \((60^\circ\,\text{N},\ 140^\circ\,\text{W})\text{.}\) Compare the distance from P to Q along the parallel with the distance over the North Pole.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(40 + 140 = 180\text{,}\) so the meridians of P and Q are opposite each other. Together they form a great circle that passes through the North Pole (Figure 7.2.19).
    🔗
    Along the parallel: the difference in longitude is \(180^\circ = 10\,800'\text{,}\) so the distance is \(10\,800 \cos 60^\circ = 5400\,\text{nm}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Over the pole: P is \(90^\circ - 60^\circ = 30^\circ\) from the pole and so is Q, so the angle at the centre is \(60^\circ = 3600'\text{:}\) \(3600\,\text{nm}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The route over the pole is shorter, by \(1800\,\text{nm}\text{:}\) it follows a great circle.
    🔗
    🔗

    🔗
    Solution.
    Opposite sides of Greenwich: \(20^\circ + 35^\circ = 55^\circ = 3300'\text{.}\) \(3300 \cos 52^\circ = 2031.7\,\text{nm}\text{;}\) \(\tfrac{55}{360} \times 40\,040 \times \cos 52^\circ = 3766.1\,\text{km}\text{.}\)

    3.

    Take Lodwar \((35^\circ 36'\,\text{E})\) and Mandera \((41^\circ 52'\,\text{E})\) both to be on the parallel \(3^\circ\,\text{N}\text{.}\) Find the distance between them along the parallel in nautical miles and in kilometres.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(375.5\,\text{nm}\text{;}\) \(696.0\,\text{km}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(41^\circ 52' - 35^\circ 36' = 6^\circ 16' = 376'\text{.}\) \(376 \cos 3^\circ = 375.5\,\text{nm}\text{;}\) \(6\tfrac{16}{60} \div 360 \times 40\,040 \times \cos 3^\circ = 696.0\,\text{km}\text{.}\)

    4.

    A ship leaves Port Louis in Mauritius, taken to be at \((20^\circ\,\text{S},\ 57^\circ 30'\,\text{E})\text{,}\) and sails \(600\,\text{nm}\) due west along the parallel. Find its new longitude, to the nearest minute.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(46^\circ 51'\,\text{E}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Each degree of longitude on \(20^\circ\,\text{S}\) is \(60\cos 20^\circ = 56.38\,\text{nm}\text{.}\) Change in longitude \(= 600 \div (60 \cos 20^\circ) = 10.642^\circ = 10^\circ 39'\text{.}\) Sailing west from an east longitude decreases it: \(57^\circ 30' - 10^\circ 39' = 46^\circ 51'\,\text{E}\text{.}\)

    5.

    P is at \((70^\circ\,\text{S},\ 25^\circ\,\text{E})\) and Q at \((70^\circ\,\text{S},\ 155^\circ\,\text{W})\text{.}\) Compare the distance from P to Q along the parallel with the distance over the South Pole.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Parallel \(3693.8\,\text{nm}\text{;}\) over the pole \(2400\,\text{nm}\text{;}\) the route over the pole is shorter.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(25 + 155 = 180\text{:}\) opposite meridians. Along the parallel: \(10\,800 \cos 70^\circ = 3693.8\,\text{nm}\text{.}\) Over the pole: \(2 \times (90^\circ - 70^\circ) = 40^\circ = 2400'\text{,}\) so \(2400\,\text{nm}\text{.}\)

    6. Challenge: Which Parallel?

    Along which parallel of latitude is the distance between the meridians \(0^\circ\) and \(60^\circ\,\text{E}\) equal to \(1800\,\text{nm}\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    On the equator the distance is \(3600\,\text{nm}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(60^\circ\,\text{N}\) or \(60^\circ\,\text{S}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(60^\circ = 3600'\text{,}\) so \(3600\cos\phi = 1800\text{,}\) \(\cos\phi = \tfrac{1}{2}\) and \(\phi = 60^\circ\text{.}\) The parallel is \(60^\circ\,\text{N}\) or \(60^\circ\,\text{S}\text{.}\)
