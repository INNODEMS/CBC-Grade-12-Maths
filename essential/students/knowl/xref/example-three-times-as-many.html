Example 3.2.5 Three Times as Many.

A juice stall sells \(m\) cups of mango juice and \(p\) cups of passion juice a day. It always sells at least three times as many cups of mango as of passion. Write an inequality. Do \(20\) mango and \(6\) passion fit? Do \(15\) mango and \(6\) passion?
🔗
Solution.
Three times the passion cups is \(3p\text{,}\) and the mango cups are at least that: \(m \ge 3p\text{.}\)
🔗
A common mistake is to write \(3m \ge p\text{.}\) Check with a pair that fits the words, \(m = 9\text{,}\) \(p = 3\text{:}\) \(9 \ge 3(3)\) is true, as it should be. Now try \(m = 4\text{,}\) \(p = 3\text{,}\) which does not fit the words: \(4 \ge 9\) is false for \(m \ge 3p\text{,}\) but \(12 \ge 3\) is true for \(3m \ge p\text{,}\) so \(3m \ge p\) is wrong.
🔗
\((20, 6)\text{:}\) \(20 \ge 18\text{,}\) yes. \((15, 6)\text{:}\) \(15 \ge 18\) is false, no.
