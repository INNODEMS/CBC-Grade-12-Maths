    Key Takeaway 8.6.3 Income Tax and PAYE.

    Your gross pay is all you earn: basic pay plus allowances. Your taxable pay is the part that is taxed; it can be less than gross pay when a deduction, such as a pension contribution, is allowed before tax. Taxable pay is cut into tax bands, and each slice of pay is taxed at its own band’s rate, so a rise never lowers take-home pay. Every resident employee then takes off a personal relief of Ksh \(2400\) a month. What is left is PAYE (Pay As You Earn): the employer deducts it from your pay and sends it to the Kenya Revenue Authority.
    🔗
    A shortcut: the tax on the whole of the first bands is
    \begin{align*} \text{up to } 24\,000\amp: \quad 2400\\ \text{up to } 32\,333\amp: \quad 2400 + 2083.25 = 4483.25\\ \text{up to } 500\,000\amp: \quad 4483.25 + 467\,667 \times 0.3 = 144\,783.35\\ \text{up to } 800\,000\amp: \quad 144\,783.35 + 300\,000 \times 0.325 = 242\,283.35 \end{align*}
    so only the slice in the highest band needs working out.
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 8.6.5 These Figures Change.

    The tax bands and the personal relief are set by Parliament in a Finance Act and can change. The figures in this chapter are those in force from 1 July 2023 (checked in November 2026). Find the current table on the Kenya Revenue Authority website.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.6.6 Just into the Second Band.

    Find the PAYE on a monthly taxable pay of Ksh \(28\,000\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The first \(24\,000\) is taxed at \(10\%\text{:}\) \(2400\text{.}\) The remaining \(4000\) is in the \(25\%\) band: \(4000 \times 0.25 = 1000\text{.}\) The tax is \(2400 + 1000 = 3400\text{,}\) and the PAYE is \(3400 - 2400 = 1000\text{,}\) Ksh \(1000\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.6.7 A Bigger Salary.

    Find the PAYE on a monthly taxable pay of Ksh \(120\,000\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The slices are \(24\,000\) at \(10\%\text{,}\) \(8333\) at \(25\%\) and \(120\,000 - 32\,333 = 87\,667\) at \(30\%\text{.}\)
    \begin{equation*} \text{tax} = 2400 + 2083.25 + 87\,667 \times 0.3 = 2400 + 2083.25 + 26\,300.10 = 30\,783.35 \end{equation*}
    The PAYE is \(30\,783.35 - 2400 = 28\,383.35\text{,}\) Ksh \(28\,383.35\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.6.8 No PAYE to Pay.

    Find the PAYE on a monthly taxable pay of Ksh \(20\,000\text{,}\) and on Ksh \(24\,000\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    On \(20\,000\) the tax is \(2000\text{,}\) less than the relief of \(2400\text{.}\) Relief cannot make tax negative, so the PAYE is \(0\text{.}\) On \(24\,000\) the tax is exactly \(2400\text{,}\) so the PAYE is again \(0\text{.}\)

    6. Challenge: Monthly and Yearly Bands.

    Show how the yearly bands and relief in Table 8.6.1 follow from the monthly ones. Why is the \(25\%\) band \(100\,000\) a year and not \(12 \times 8333 = 99\,996\text{,}\) and the \(30\%\) band \(5\,612\,000\) and not \(12 \times 467\,667 = 5\,612\,004\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Divide the yearly band by \(12\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    Multiply by \(12\text{:}\) \(12 \times 24\,000 = 288\,000\text{,}\) \(12 \times 300\,000 = 3\,600\,000\text{,}\) \(12 \times 800\,000 = 9\,600\,000\) and relief \(12 \times 2400 = 28\,800\text{.}\) The \(25\%\) band is set at \(100\,000\) a year, and \(100\,000 \div 12 = 8333.33\) is rounded to \(8333\) a month. The \(30\%\) band ends at \(12 \times 500\,000 = 6\,000\,000\) a year, so it is \(6\,000\,000 - 288\,000 - 100\,000 = 5\,612\,000\text{;}\) the monthly \(467\,667 = 500\,000 - 32\,333\) carries the rounding of \(8333\text{.}\)

    Key Takeaway 8.6.12 Net Pay.

    A deduction is money taken off your pay before you receive it. Your net pay, or take-home pay, is what is left:
    \begin{equation*} \text{net pay} = \text{gross pay} - \text{pension contribution} - \text{PAYE} - \text{other deductions} \end{equation*}
    When the pension contribution is taken off before tax, the PAYE is worked out on the gross pay minus the pension contribution.
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 8.6.13 Other Deductions on a Payslip.

    A real payslip also shows statutory deductions such as NSSF (a pension fund), SHIF (health insurance) and the Affordable Housing Levy, and may show a SACCO or loan repayment. Some of these reduce taxable pay, and insurance premiums can earn insurance relief. Their rates and rules are set by their own laws and change from time to time, so in this book they are given as one total, “other deductions”.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.6.14 A Nurse’s Pay.

    A nurse in Mombasa has a gross pay of Ksh \(92\,000\) a month. Her pension contribution of Ksh \(4500\) is taken off before tax, and her other deductions total Ksh \(9800\text{.}\) Find her taxable pay, PAYE and net pay.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Taxable pay \(= 92\,000 - 4500 = 87\,500\text{.}\)
    \begin{equation*} \text{tax} = 4483.25 + (87\,500 - 32\,333) \times 0.3 = 4483.25 + 16\,550.10 = 21\,033.35 \end{equation*}
    PAYE \(= 21\,033.35 - 2400 = 18\,633.35\text{.}\) Net pay \(= 92\,000 - 4500 - 18\,633.35 - 9800 = 59\,066.65\text{,}\) Ksh \(59\,066.65\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.6.15 A Pay Rise into the 30% Band.

    A clerk’s taxable pay rises from Ksh \(30\,000\) to Ksh \(36\,000\) a month; the other deductions do not change. By how much does take-home pay change?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Before: tax \(2400 + 6000 \times 0.25 = 3900\text{,}\) PAYE \(1500\text{.}\) After: tax \(4483.25 + (36\,000 - 32\,333) \times 0.3 = 4483.25 + 1100.10 = 5583.35\text{,}\) PAYE \(3183.35\text{.}\)
    🔗
    PAYE rises by \(3183.35 - 1500 = 1683.35\text{,}\) so take-home pay rises by \(6000 - 1683.35 = 4316.65\text{,}\) Ksh \(4316.65\text{.}\) Only the slice above \(32\,333\) pays \(30\%\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 8.6.16 Money Decisions.

    What this chapter means for everyday money decisions:
    • Borrowing costs money: compare yearly rates before you borrow.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Saving grows money, and compound interest grows it faster the longer you leave it.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Things you own gain or lose value: land often appreciates; motorbikes, phones and machines depreciate.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Tax pays for schools, roads and hospitals. Every worker’s PAYE, paid honestly, is a share in the public services we all use.
      🔗
      🔗

    5.

    Explain why nobody with a monthly taxable pay of Ksh \(24\,000\) or less pays PAYE.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The tax on \(24\,000\) is exactly \(2400\text{,}\) equal to the personal relief; on less pay the tax is smaller still, and PAYE is never negative.

    6. Challenge: A Rise Never Hurts.

    Explain why a pay rise of Ksh \(1000\) a month always raises take-home pay, whatever the band, if the other deductions do not change.
    🔗
    Hint.
    What is the highest rate at which any of the extra Ksh \(1000\) can be taxed?
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    At most \(35\%\) of the rise is taxed, so at least Ksh \(650\) of it is kept.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Each slice is taxed at its own band’s rate, and the highest rate is \(35\%\text{.}\) So the extra \(1000\) adds at most \(350\) to the tax, and the relief does not change. Take-home pay rises by at least \(1000 - 350 = 650\text{.}\)
