    Subsection 7.4.1 Local Time and Longitude

    Learner Experience 7.4.1 Noon in Nairobi.

    Take Nairobi to be on longitude \(37^\circ\,\text{E}\) and Kisumu on \(35^\circ\,\text{E}\text{.}\) Local time at a place is the time by the sun there: it is 12:00 local time when the sun is highest in the sky.
    🔗
    1. The earth turns once (\(360^\circ\)) in \(24\) hours. Through how many degrees does it turn in \(1\) hour? In \(4\) minutes?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. When the sun is highest over Nairobi, how long is it until the sun is highest over Kisumu?
      🔗
      🔗
    3. When it is 12:00 local time on \(37^\circ\,\text{E}\text{,}\) find the local times in London (\(0^\circ\)), Dubai (\(55^\circ\,\text{E}\)) and New York (\(74^\circ\,\text{W}\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    4. In January, clocks in Nairobi show East Africa Time (EAT), which is GMT \(+ 3\) hours. Clocks in London show GMT, in Dubai GMT \(+ 4\) hours and in New York GMT \(- 5\) hours. When Nairobi clocks show 12:00, what do the clocks in the other three cities show? Why are the answers to (c) and (d) different?
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 7.4.2 Longitude and Local Time.

    The local time at a place is the time by the sun there: 12:00 when the sun is highest. The earth turns eastwards through \(15^\circ\) every hour, so the sun reaches places to the east first.
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 360^\circ \amp = 24\ \text{hours}, \amp 15^\circ \amp = 1\ \text{hour}, \amp 1^\circ \amp = 4\ \text{minutes}, \amp 15' \amp = 1\ \text{minute} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Time difference between two places \(=\) difference in longitude (in degrees) \(\times 4\) minutes. A place east of another is ahead in local time; a place west of it is behind. Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) is the local time on the Greenwich meridian, \(0^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 7.4.4 Time Zones and East Africa Time.

    If every town kept its own local time, clocks in Nairobi and Mombasa would differ by about \(11\) minutes. Instead, most countries use time zones: every clock in a zone shows the same time, usually a whole number of hours ahead of or behind GMT. Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania use East Africa Time (EAT), which is GMT \(+ 3\) hours: the local time of the \(45^\circ\,\text{E}\) meridian. Nairobi is about \(8^\circ\) west of that meridian, so the sun is highest over Nairobi at about 12:33 EAT. In this chapter, “local time” always means the time by longitude, not the time zone.
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 7.4.5 The International Date Line.

    Travel far enough east or west and the time difference reaches \(12\) hours, at the \(180^\circ\) meridian. The International Date Line runs through the Pacific Ocean, roughly along \(180^\circ\text{.}\) Crossing it travelling westwards, you add a day; travelling eastwards, you take a day away. The line bends round some island groups so that each country keeps one date.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.4.6 Mombasa and Kampala.

    Take Mombasa to be on longitude \(40^\circ\,\text{E}\) and Kampala on \(33^\circ\,\text{E}\text{.}\) Find the local time in Kampala when it is 12:00 local time in Mombasa.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Both are east of Greenwich, so the difference in longitude is \(40^\circ - 33^\circ = 7^\circ\text{,}\) which is \(7 \times 4 = 28\) minutes. Kampala is west of Mombasa, so its local time is behind: \(12{:}00 - 28\,\text{min} = 11{:}32\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.4.7 Accra and Nairobi.

    It is 15:20 local time in Nairobi (take \(37^\circ\,\text{E}\)). Take Accra to be on the Greenwich meridian, \(0^\circ\text{.}\) What is the local time in Accra?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Difference in longitude \(37^\circ\text{,}\) so \(37 \times 4 = 148\,\text{min} = 2\,\text{h}\ 28\,\text{min}\text{.}\) Accra is west, so behind: \(15{:}20 - 2{:}28 = 12{:}52\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.4.8 Finding a Ship’s Longitude.

    A ship’s navigator sees that the local time on board is 05:24 when it is 08:00 GMT. Find the ship’s longitude.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The ship’s time is behind GMT by \(8{:}00 - 5{:}24 = 2\,\text{h}\ 36\,\text{min} = 156\,\text{min}\text{.}\) Each degree is \(4\) minutes, so the ship is \(156 \div 4 = 39^\circ\) from Greenwich. Behind means west: the ship is on longitude \(39^\circ\,\text{W}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.4.9 Across the Pacific.

    It is 05:00 on Wednesday, local time, in Nairobi (\(37^\circ\,\text{E}\)). Take Honolulu to be on longitude \(158^\circ\,\text{W}\text{.}\) What are the day and local time in Honolulu?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Nairobi and Honolulu are on opposite sides of Greenwich, so add: \(37^\circ + 158^\circ = 195^\circ\text{.}\) \(195 \times 4 = 780\,\text{min} = 13\,\text{h}\text{.}\) Honolulu is west of Nairobi, so it is behind: \(13\) hours before 05:00 on Wednesday is 16:00 on Tuesday.
    🔗
    Counting the other way, eastwards across the \(180^\circ\) meridian, Honolulu is \(165^\circ\) away and \(11\) hours ahead, which also gives 16:00. Crossing the International Date Line (Note 7.4.5) eastwards takes a day away, so the day is Tuesday either way.
    🔗
    🔗

    🔗

    08:00; 21:00
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(75 \times 4 = 300\,\text{min} = 5\,\text{h}\) behind: 08:00. \(120 \times 4 = 480\,\text{min} = 8\,\text{h}\) ahead: 21:00.

    When it is 12:00 GMT, the local time on a ship is 15:48. Find the ship’s longitude.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(57^\circ\,\text{E}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The ship is \(3\,\text{h}\ 48\,\text{min} = 228\,\text{min}\) ahead, so \(228 \div 4 = 57^\circ\) east.

    4.

    It is 10:00 on Wednesday, local time, in Nairobi (\(37^\circ\,\text{E}\)). Find the day and local time in Wellington (take \(175^\circ\,\text{E}\)) and in Lima (take \(77^\circ\,\text{W}\)).

    Answer.
    Wellington: Wednesday 19:12. Lima: Wednesday 02:24.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Wellington is \(175 - 37 = 138^\circ\) east: \(552\,\text{min} = 9\,\text{h}\ 12\,\text{min}\) ahead, 19:12 on Wednesday. Lima is \(37 + 77 = 114^\circ\) west: \(456\,\text{min} = 7\,\text{h}\ 36\,\text{min}\) behind, 02:24 on Wednesday.

    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    It is 21:30 on Friday, local time, in Nairobi (\(37^\circ\,\text{E}\)). Find the day and local time on longitude \(165^\circ\,\text{E}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Saturday 06:02
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(165 - 37 = 128^\circ\) east: \(512\,\text{min} = 8\,\text{h}\ 32\,\text{min}\) ahead. \(21{:}30 + 8{:}32 = 30{:}02\text{,}\) which is past midnight: 06:02 on Saturday.

    6. Challenge: Two Possible Longitudes.

    The local time at a place P differs by \(3\,\text{h}\ 20\,\text{min}\) from the local time on longitude \(25^\circ\,\text{E}\text{.}\) Find the two possible longitudes of P.
    🔗
    Hint.
    P could be east or west of \(25^\circ\,\text{E}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(75^\circ\,\text{E}\) or \(25^\circ\,\text{W}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(3\,\text{h}\ 20\,\text{min} = 200\,\text{min}\text{,}\) and \(200 \div 4 = 50^\circ\text{.}\) East: \(25^\circ + 50^\circ = 75^\circ\,\text{E}\text{.}\) West: \(50^\circ - 25^\circ = 25^\circ\) past Greenwich, so \(25^\circ\,\text{W}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Subsection 7.4.2 Journeys Across Longitudes

    Learner Experience 7.4.13 Five Days on the Equator.

    1. How far does the ship sail, in nautical miles?
      🔗

    2. How long does the voyage take?
      🔗
      🔗
    3. What is the time of arrival by the clock of the starting port?
      🔗
      🔗
    4. What is the local time on \(73^\circ\,\text{E}\) when the ship arrives?
      🔗
      🔗

      🔗
      🔗

    Key Takeaway 7.4.14 Arrival Local Time.

    For a journey from one longitude to another:
    🔗
    1. Find the journey time from the distance and the speed.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Add it to the departure time: this is the arrival time by the clock of the starting place.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Change to local time at the end: add \(4\) minutes for each degree travelled east, or subtract \(4\) minutes for each degree travelled west.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \text{arrival local time} = \text{departure local time} + \text{journey time} \pm 4\ \text{min} \times (\text{degrees of longitude travelled}) \end{equation*}
    with \(+\) for a journey east and \(-\) for a journey west.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.4.15 A Westward Flight.

    An aircraft flies due west along the equator from longitude \(37^\circ\,\text{E}\) to longitude \(13^\circ\,\text{E}\) at \(450\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) It leaves at 07:30 local time. Find the local time when it arrives.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Step 1: journey time. Distance \((37 - 13) \times 60 = 1440\,\text{nm}\text{.}\) Time \(1440 \div 450 = 3.2\,\text{h} = 3\,\text{h}\ 12\,\text{min}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 2: starting clock. \(07{:}30 + 3{:}12 = 10{:}42\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 3: local time. The aircraft travelled \(24^\circ\) west, so local time is \(24 \times 4 = 96\,\text{min} = 1\,\text{h}\ 36\,\text{min}\) behind: \(10{:}42 - 1{:}36 = 09{:}06\text{.}\) It arrives at 09:06 local time.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.4.16 Finding the Speed from Local Times.

    A plane leaves longitude \(0^\circ\) on the equator at 09:00 local time and flies due east along the equator to longitude \(37^\circ\,\text{E}\text{,}\) arriving at 17:58 local time. Find its speed in knots.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The local times differ by \(17{:}58 - 9{:}00 = 8\,\text{h}\ 58\,\text{min}\text{.}\) Part of this is the change of local time: \(37 \times 4 = 148\,\text{min} = 2\,\text{h}\ 28\,\text{min}\text{,}\) gained by flying east. So the flight time is \(8\,\text{h}\ 58\,\text{min} - 2\,\text{h}\ 28\,\text{min} = 6\,\text{h}\ 30\,\text{min} = 6.5\,\text{h}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Distance \(37 \times 60 = 2220\,\text{nm}\text{.}\) Speed \(2220 \div 6.5 = 341.5\,\text{knots}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 7.4.17 Navigation Around Us.

    Navigation is finding where you are and how to reach where you want to go. In Kenya it is used every day:
    🔗
    • ships entering the ports of Mombasa and Lamu, and dhows along the coast;
      🔗
      🔗
    • Kenya Airways and other flights planning routes, fuel and arrival times across time zones;
      🔗
      🔗
    • fishermen on Lake Victoria and in the Indian Ocean finding their way back to shore;
      🔗
      🔗
    • phone maps and GPS used by matatu, boda boda and delivery riders;
      🔗
      🔗
    • search and rescue teams giving positions in latitude and longitude;
      🔗
      🔗
    • businesses arranging calls and deliveries with partners in other time zones.
      🔗
      🔗

    4. Challenge: The Ship’s Clock.

    A ship sails due west along the equator, covering \(300\,\text{nm}\) each day. Its clock is set to local time once a day. By how much is the clock changed each day, and is it put forward or back?
    🔗
    Hint.
    How many degrees of longitude is \(300\,\text{nm}\) along the equator?
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    Back \(20\) minutes each day
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(300\,\text{nm} = 300' = 5^\circ\) a day. \(5 \times 4 = 20\) minutes. The ship moves west, where local time is behind, so the clock is put back \(20\) minutes each day.
