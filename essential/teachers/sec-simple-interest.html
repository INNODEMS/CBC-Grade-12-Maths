    Key Inquiry Questions 8.1.1.

    Why should every adult understand interest, value and tax?
    🔗
    🔗
    Money decisions come early. A chama saves together and lends to its members; a parent borrows from a SACCO to pay school fees; a family buys a plot for the future; a boda boda rider buys a motorbike that is worth less every year; and one day you will open your first payslip and see how much has been taken off in tax.
    🔗
    This chapter gives you the arithmetic behind those decisions: simple and compound interest, appreciation and depreciation, hire purchase, and income tax. As you work, ask who gains and who pays in each case, and keep the key inquiry question in mind.
    🔗

    Note 8.1.2 Money in This Chapter.

    • Amounts are in Kenya shillings, written “Ksh” before the number. Prices, values, rates and salaries in this chapter are invented, at about 2026 levels; the tax bands are the real ones from the Kenya Revenue Authority.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Rates of interest are per cent per annum (p.a.), that is, per year. \(R\) in the simple interest formula and \(r\) in the compound interest formula are the same thing, the rate per cent per annum; we keep the letters in common use.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Round only at the end. Give money to the nearest shilling unless the work is exact; tax is worked to the cent, as the Kenya Revenue Authority does. Give rates and percentages to 1 decimal place.
      🔗
      🔗

    Subsection 8.1.1 Simple Interest and the Amount

    Learner Experience 8.1.3 Interest Year by Year.

    Kamau saves Ksh \(20\,000\) in a SACCO account that pays \(8\%\) per annum simple interest. The interest is paid to his phone each year and is not added to the account.
    🔗
    1. How much interest does Kamau receive in the first year?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the total interest he has received after \(1\text{,}\) \(2\text{,}\) \(3\) and \(4\) years.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Write a rule for the total interest after \(T\) years.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. How much has Kamau received altogether after \(4\) years, savings plus interest?
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 8.1.4 Simple Interest.

    The sum saved or borrowed is the principal, \(P\text{.}\) The rate of interest, \(R\text{,}\) is a percentage per year (per annum, “p.a.”), and the time, \(T\text{,}\) is in years. Simple interest is interest on the principal only, the same every year:
    \begin{equation*} I = \frac{PRT}{100} \end{equation*}
    The amount is the principal plus the interest, \(A = P + I\text{.}\) For a time in months, divide the number of months by \(12\) to get \(T\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.1.6 A SACCO Loan.

    Achieng borrows Ksh \(45\,000\) from her SACCO at \(12\%\) per annum simple interest for \(2\) years. She repays the amount in \(24\) equal monthly instalments. Find the interest, the amount and each instalment.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} I = \frac{PRT}{100} = \frac{45\,000 \times 12 \times 2}{100} = 10\,800 \end{equation*}
    The interest is Ksh \(10\,800\) and the amount is \(45\,000 + 10\,800 = 55\,800\text{,}\) so Ksh \(55\,800\text{.}\) Each instalment is \(55\,800 \div 24 = 2325\text{,}\) so Ksh \(2325\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.1.7 Interest for Part of a Year.

    Find the simple interest and the amount on Ksh \(18\,000\) at \(9.5\%\) per annum for \(10\) months.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The time in years is \(T = \dfrac{10}{12}\text{.}\)
    \begin{equation*} I = \frac{18\,000 \times 9.5 \times 10}{100 \times 12} = 1425 \end{equation*}
    The interest is Ksh \(1425\) and the amount is Ksh \(19\,425\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.1.8 A Fixed Deposit.

    A farmer puts Ksh \(7500\) in a fixed deposit at \(6.4\%\) per annum simple interest for \(5\) years. Find the interest and the amount.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} I = \frac{7500 \times 6.4 \times 5}{100} = 2400 \end{equation*}
    The interest is Ksh \(2400\) and the amount is \(7500 + 2400 = 9900\text{,}\) so Ksh \(9900\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Type amounts as plain numbers, without Ksh, spaces or commas, for example 45326. Give money to the nearest shilling and percentages to 1 decimal place unless the question says otherwise; exact answers in cents are also accepted.

    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.

    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(T = \dfrac{18}{12} = 1.5\text{,}\) so \(I = \dfrac{30\,000 \times 15 \times 1.5}{100} = 6750\text{.}\)

    6. Challenge: Double the Time.

    Ksh \(10\,000\) is invested at \(10\%\) per annum simple interest. Show that doubling the time from \(2\) years to \(4\) years doubles the interest but does not double the amount.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Work out the interest and the amount for each time. Which part of the amount does not change?
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    Interest Ksh \(2000\) then Ksh \(4000\text{;}\) amount Ksh \(12\,000\) then Ksh \(14\,000\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    For \(2\) years, \(I = \dfrac{10\,000 \times 10 \times 2}{100} = 2000\) and \(A = 12\,000\text{.}\) For \(4\) years, \(I = 4000\) and \(A = 14\,000\text{.}\) The interest is proportional to the time, so it doubles; the amount includes the principal, which stays at \(10\,000\text{,}\) so the amount does not double.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 8.1.2 Finding the Rate, the Time or the Principal

    Learner Experience 8.1.10 SACCO, Bank or Phone?

    Wafula needs Ksh \(10\,000\) for \(6\) months.
    🔗
    1. His SACCO charges \(12\%\) per annum simple interest. How much interest would he pay?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. A bank charges \(16\%\) per annum simple interest. How much interest would he pay?
      🔗
      🔗
    3. A mobile lender charges \(7.5\%\) of the loan for each month. How much would he pay for \(6\) months?
      🔗
      🔗
    4. What yearly rate is the mobile lender’s charge?
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Which would you choose, and why might someone still use the mobile lender?
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 8.1.11 Rearranging the Formula.

    From \(I = \dfrac{PRT}{100}\text{,}\) multiply both sides by \(100\) and divide by the other two letters:
    \begin{equation*} R = \frac{100I}{PT}, \qquad T = \frac{100I}{PR}, \qquad P = \frac{100I}{RT} \end{equation*}
    When the amount is given instead of the interest, find the interest first: \(I = A - P\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 8.1.12 Mobile Loans.

    Loans on a mobile phone are quick, but they usually charge a fee for each month, often several per cent. Multiply a monthly rate by \(12\) to compare it with a yearly rate. Borrow only what you can repay on time.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.1.13 Finding the Rate.

    Ksh \(60\,000\) grew to Ksh \(74\,400\) in \(3\) years at simple interest. Find the rate.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The interest is \(I = 74\,400 - 60\,000 = 14\,400\text{.}\)
    \begin{equation*} R = \frac{100I}{PT} = \frac{100 \times 14\,400}{60\,000 \times 3} = 8 \end{equation*}
    The rate is \(8\%\) per annum.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.1.14 How Long?

    Ksh \(25\,000\) is invested at \(9\%\) per annum simple interest and earns Ksh \(6750\text{.}\) For how long was it invested?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} T = \frac{100I}{PR} = \frac{100 \times 6750}{25\,000 \times 9} = 3 \end{equation*}
    It was invested for \(3\) years.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.1.15 Finding the Principal.

    How much must be saved at \(7.5\%\) per annum simple interest to earn Ksh \(12\,000\) in \(4\) years?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} P = \frac{100I}{RT} = \frac{100 \times 12\,000}{7.5 \times 4} = 40\,000 \end{equation*}
    Ksh \(40\,000\) must be saved.
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Challenge: Doubling Your Money.

    At what simple rate of interest does money double in \(8\) years? Explain why the principal does not matter.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Doubling means the interest equals the principal: \(I = P\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(12.5\%\) per annum
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Money doubles when \(I = P\text{,}\) that is \(\dfrac{PRT}{100} = P\text{.}\) Dividing by \(P\) gives \(RT = 100\text{,}\) whatever \(P\) is. With \(T = 8\text{,}\) \(R = 100 \div 8 = 12.5\text{.}\)
