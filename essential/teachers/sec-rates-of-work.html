    Section 2.3 Rates of Work

    Curriculum Alignment

    Strand
    🔗
    1.0 Numbers and Algebra
    🔗
    Sub-Strand
    🔗
    1.2 Compound Proportions, Rates of Work and Mixtures
    🔗
    Specific Learning Outcomes
    🔗
    Compute rates of work from real life situations
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    A rate of work is a compound proportion: the work done depends on how many work, how fast each works, and for how long. In this section we measure work as a fraction of the whole job, so that people and machines working at different speeds can be combined.
    🔗

    Key Inquiry Questions 2.3.1.

    Why does it matter how fast people and machines work?
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 2.3.1 Working Together

    Learner Experience 2.3.2 Filling the Bucket.

    Learners fill a bucket from a drum, one cup at a time. Each learner has a cup of the same size and works at the same speed.
    🔗
    1. One learner takes \(120\) seconds, two take \(60\) seconds and three take \(40\) seconds. Predict the time for four learners, then check it.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Asha alone fills the bucket in \(90\) seconds and Brian alone in \(180\) seconds. What fraction of the bucket does each fill in one second?
      🔗
      🔗
    3. What fraction do they fill together in one second? How long do they take together?
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Why is the answer not \(135\) seconds, the mean of their times?
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 2.3.3 Rate of Work.

    A job that takes \(t\) days is done at a rate of work of \(\dfrac{1}{t}\) of the job a day. When people or machines work together, their rates add. The time together is \(1 \div\) (the combined rate), and the fraction done in \(k\) days is \(k \times\) (the rate). Add the fractions done in one day, not the times.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 2.3.5 Two Welders.

    Mutua can make a gate in \(6\) days and Njeri can make one in \(3\) days. How long do they take working together?
    🔗
    Solution.
    In one day Mutua makes \(\tfrac{1}{6}\) of the gate and Njeri \(\tfrac{1}{3}\text{.}\) Together: \(\tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{2}{6} = \tfrac{1}{2}\) of the gate a day.
    🔗
    The time is \(1 \div \tfrac{1}{2} = 2\) days.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 2.3.6 Two Tractors.

    Tractor A ploughs a farm in \(10\) hours and tractor B in \(15\) hours. How long do they take together, and what fraction of the farm do they plough in \(4\) hours?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Together: \(\tfrac{1}{10} + \tfrac{1}{15} = \tfrac{3}{30} + \tfrac{2}{30} = \tfrac{1}{6}\) of the farm an hour, so \(6\) hours.
    🔗
    In \(4\) hours they plough \(4 \times \tfrac{1}{6} = \tfrac{2}{3}\) of the farm.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 2.3.7 One Starts Early.

    Kiprop can paint a hall in \(12\) days and Auma in \(8\) days. Kiprop works alone for \(2\) days, then Auma joins and they finish together. How many days does the whole job take?
    🔗
    Solution.
    In \(2\) days Kiprop paints \(\tfrac{2}{12} = \tfrac{1}{6}\) of the hall, so \(\tfrac{5}{6}\) is left.
    🔗
    Together they paint \(\tfrac{1}{12} + \tfrac{1}{8} = \tfrac{2}{24} + \tfrac{3}{24} = \tfrac{5}{24}\) a day, so the rest takes \(\tfrac{5}{6} \div \tfrac{5}{24} = 4\) days. The whole job takes \(2 + 4 = 6\) days.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

Give times, masses and volumes exactly or correct to 1 decimal place, and money to the nearest shilling, unless the question says otherwise.

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    A can do a job in \(4\) hours and B in \(12\) hours. How long do they take together?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(3\) hours
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\tfrac{1}{4} + \tfrac{1}{12} = \tfrac{4}{12} = \tfrac{1}{3}\) an hour, so \(3\) hours.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Two road crews take \(20\) days and \(30\) days to do a job alone. How long do they take together, and what fraction of the job do they do in \(5\) days?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(12\) days; \(\tfrac{5}{12}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Three workers take \(6\text{,}\) \(10\) and \(15\) days to do a job alone. How long do they take together?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(3\) days
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{1}{10} + \tfrac{1}{15} = \tfrac{5 + 3 + 2}{30} = \tfrac{1}{3}\) a day, so \(3\) days.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Together A and B take \(8\) days to do a job. A alone takes \(12\) days. How long does B take alone?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(24\) days
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    B’s rate is \(\tfrac{1}{8} - \tfrac{1}{12} = \tfrac{1}{24}\) of the job a day.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    A can do a job in \(9\) days and B in \(18\) days. A works alone for \(3\) days, then they finish together. How many days does the job take in all?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(7\) days
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    A does \(\tfrac{3}{9} = \tfrac{1}{3}\text{,}\) leaving \(\tfrac{2}{3}\text{.}\) Together they do \(\tfrac{1}{9} + \tfrac{1}{18} = \tfrac{1}{6}\) a day, so \(\tfrac{2}{3} \div \tfrac{1}{6} = 4\) more days: \(7\) in all.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Challenge: Twice as Fast.

    Two workers together take \(6\) days to do a job. One works twice as fast as the other. How long would each take alone?
    🔗
    Hint.
    If the slower worker does \(r\) of the job a day, the faster does \(2r\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(9\) days and \(18\) days
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(r + 2r = \tfrac{1}{6}\text{,}\) so \(r = \tfrac{1}{18}\text{.}\) The slower takes \(18\) days and the faster \(9\) days.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 2.3.2 Filling, Emptying and Safe Working Hours

    Learner Experience 2.3.11 Safe Hours on a Long Journey.

    A bus company runs buses from Nairobi to Mombasa, about \(480\,\text{km}\text{,}\) at an average speed of \(60\,\text{km/h}\text{.}\) The company’s own rule is that a driver drives at most \(4\) hours without a \(30\)-minute rest, at most \(8\) hours a day, and on at most \(5\) days a week.
    🔗
    1. How long is the driving time from Nairobi to Mombasa?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. With one rest after \(4\) hours, how long does the journey take?
      🔗
      🔗
    3. The company runs \(12\) one-way trips a day. How many hours of driving is that a day, and how many drivers are needed each day?
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Drivers work \(5\) days a week, but the buses run every day. How many drivers does the company need?
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Why do companies and the law limit how long drivers may drive?
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 2.3.12 Filling and Emptying.

    An inlet that fills a tank in \(a\) hours adds \(\dfrac{1}{a}\) of the tank each hour; an outlet that empties it in \(c\) hours removes \(\dfrac{1}{c}\text{.}\) With both open the net rate is \(\dfrac{1}{a} - \dfrac{1}{c}\) of the tank an hour, and the time to fill is \(1 \div\) (net rate). If the outlet is faster, the net rate is negative and the tank empties; if they are equally fast, the level never changes. The outlet takes away its fraction each hour.
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 2.3.13 Safe Working Hours.

    Tired drivers cause many road crashes. In Kenya the law, and the National Transport and Safety Authority (NTSA), set rules on how long drivers of public service vehicles may drive; look up the current rules. The hours in this chapter are a company’s own rules, invented for practice. Keeping to safe hours is part of a driver’s responsibility to passengers and other road users.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 2.3.14 A School Water Tank.

    A pump fills the school’s water tank in \(5\) hours. The kitchen taps empty the full tank in \(20\) hours. The tank is empty and both are turned on. How long does the tank take to fill?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The net rate is \(\tfrac{1}{5} - \tfrac{1}{20} = \tfrac{4}{20} - \tfrac{1}{20} = \tfrac{3}{20}\) of the tank an hour.
    🔗
    The time is \(1 \div \tfrac{3}{20} = \tfrac{20}{3} = 6\tfrac{2}{3}\) hours, that is \(6\) h \(40\) min. Check with Figure 2.3.4: set \(a = 5\text{,}\) clear tap B, open the outlet and set \(c = 20\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 2.3.15 Two Inlets and a Drain.

    Two inlets fill a tank in \(6\) hours and \(4\) hours, and a drain empties it in \(8\) hours. With all three open, how long does the empty tank take to fill?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{1}{4} - \tfrac{1}{8} = \tfrac{4}{24} + \tfrac{6}{24} - \tfrac{3}{24} = \tfrac{7}{24}\) of the tank an hour.
    🔗
    The time is \(\tfrac{24}{7} \approx 3.4\) hours (about \(3\) h \(26\) min).
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 2.3.16 How Many Drivers?

    A transport company needs \(250\) hours of driving a week. Its rule is at most \(7\) hours a day on at most \(5\) days a week. How many drivers does it need? How many if the daily limit falls to \(6\) hours?
    🔗
    Solution.
    One driver may drive \(7 \times 5 = 35\) hours a week. \(250 \div 35 = 7.14\ldots\text{;}\) \(7\) drivers give only \(245\) hours, so \(8\) drivers are needed.
    🔗
    At \(6\) hours a day one driver gives \(30\) hours. \(250 \div 30 = 8.33\ldots\text{,}\) so \(9\) drivers.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    An inlet fills a tank in \(3\) hours and an outlet empties it in \(12\) hours. Both are opened when the tank is empty. How long does it take to fill?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(4\) hours
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\tfrac{1}{3} - \tfrac{1}{12} = \tfrac{3}{12} = \tfrac{1}{4}\) an hour.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    A tank is full. Its inlet fills it in \(10\) hours and its outlet empties it in \(6\) hours. With both open, how long does it take to empty?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(15\) hours
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    It loses \(\tfrac{1}{6} - \tfrac{1}{10} = \tfrac{1}{15}\) of the tank an hour.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    A tank is half full. The inlet fills an empty tank in \(8\) hours and the outlet empties a full one in \(12\) hours. With both open, how long does it take to fill?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(12\) hours
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Net rate \(\tfrac{1}{8} - \tfrac{1}{12} = \tfrac{1}{24}\text{,}\) and \(\tfrac{1}{2} \div \tfrac{1}{24} = 12\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    A company needs \(330\) hours of driving a week. Its drivers may drive at most \(8\) hours a day on \(5\) days a week. How many drivers does it need?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(9\) drivers
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(330 \div 40 = 8.25\text{;}\) \(8\) drivers give only \(320\) hours, so \(9\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    A lorry trip needs \(14\) hours of driving, and one driver may drive at most \(8\) hours a day. (a) How many days does the trip take with one driver? (b) How many hours does each drive if two drivers share the driving equally?
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(2\) days (b) \(7\) hours each, so the trip can be done in one day
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Challenge: A Formula for the Time.

    Show that an inlet that fills a tank in \(a\) hours and an outlet that empties it in \(c\) hours fill the empty tank in \(\dfrac{ac}{c - a}\) hours, when \(c \gt a\text{.}\) What happens when \(c \le a\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    The net rate is \(\dfrac{c - a}{ac}\text{;}\) if \(c \le a\) the tank never fills (the net rate is \(0\) or negative).
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\dfrac{1}{a} - \dfrac{1}{c} = \dfrac{c - a}{ac}\text{,}\) so the time is \(\dfrac{ac}{c - a}\text{.}\) If \(c = a\) the net rate is \(0\) and the level stays where it is; if \(c \lt a\) the outlet is faster and the net rate is negative: the tank never fills.
