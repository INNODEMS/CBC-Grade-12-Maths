    Subsection 13.3.1 Maxima and Minima in Context

    Learner Experience 13.3.1 The Best Box.

    Squares of side \(x\) cm are cut from the four corners of a square sheet of manila paper \(24\,\text{cm}\) by \(24\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) and the sides are folded up to make an open box (Figure 13.3.2).
    🔗
    1. Build your group’s box. Measure it and work out its volume.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Which cut gives the largest volume so far?
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Show that \(V = x(24 - 2x)^2 = 4x^3 - 96x^2 + 576x\text{.}\) Why must \(0 \lt x \lt 12\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. \(\dfrac{dV}{dx} = 12(x - 4)(x - 12)\text{.}\) Which solution of \(\dfrac{dV}{dx} = 0\) gives a box?
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Use \(\dfrac{d^2V}{dx^2}\) to show that \(x = 4\) gives the greatest volume. Give the dimensions and volume of the best box.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 13.3.3 Solving a Maximum or Minimum Problem.

    The best value of a quantity is at a stationary point or at an end of its domain.
    1. Name the variable and draw a diagram.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Write the quantity in terms of that one variable, using the constraint.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. State the domain: the values of the variable that make sense.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Differentiate and solve \(\text{derivative} = 0\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Confirm whether it is a maximum or a minimum (by the second derivative or the gradient either side).
      🔗
      🔗
    6. On a closed domain, compare with the values at the ends.
      🔗
      🔗
    7. Answer the question in context, with units.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.3.5 A Chicken Run Against a Wall.

    Wambui has \(40\,\text{m}\) of wire fencing to make a rectangular chicken run against the wall of her house in Nyeri. The wall forms one side. Find the dimensions that give the greatest area.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Let each side that meets the wall be \(x\) m (Figure 13.3.6). The fence makes the other three sides, so the side opposite the wall is \((40 - 2x)\) m and
    \begin{equation*} A = x(40 - 2x) = 40x - 2x^2, \qquad 0 \lt x \lt 20 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \(\dfrac{dA}{dx} = 40 - 4x = 0\) when \(x = 10\text{.}\) \(\dfrac{d^2A}{dx^2} = -4 \lt 0\text{,}\) so this is a maximum.
    🔗
    The run is \(10\,\text{m}\) by \(20\,\text{m}\) (the \(20\,\text{m}\) side along the wall), with area \(200\,\text{m}^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.3.7 The Best Price for Juice.

    Mwanajuma sells cups of sugarcane juice at her stall in Mombasa. Each cup costs her Ksh \(20\) to make. At a price of Ksh \(p\) a cup she sells \(300 - 2p\) cups a day. Find the price that gives the greatest daily profit.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The profit on each cup is \((p - 20)\) shillings, so the daily profit is
    \begin{equation*} P = (p - 20)(300 - 2p) = -2p^2 + 340p - 6000 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \(\dfrac{dP}{dp} = -4p + 340 = 0\) when \(p = 85\text{,}\) and \(\dfrac{d^2P}{dp^2} = -4 \lt 0\text{,}\) so this is a maximum.
    🔗
    At Ksh \(85\) a cup she sells \(130\) cups, and her profit is \(65 \times 130 = 8450\text{:}\) Ksh \(8450\) a day.
    🔗
    Notice that her takings alone, \(p(300 - 2p)\text{,}\) are greatest at Ksh \(75\text{,}\) a different price. The cost of each cup moves the best price up.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.3.8 The Cheapest Speed for a Lorry.

    The running cost of a lorry on the Nairobi to Mombasa road, \(C\) shillings per km, at a steady speed of \(v\) km/h, is modelled by \(C = 0.004v^2 - 0.64v + 70\) for \(40 \le v \le 100\text{.}\) Find the cheapest speed and the cost per km at that speed.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\dfrac{dC}{dv} = 0.008v - 0.64 = 0\) when \(v = 80\text{,}\) which is inside the domain. \(\dfrac{d^2C}{dv^2} = 0.008 \gt 0\text{,}\) so this is a minimum.
    🔗
    \(C = 0.004 \times 6400 - 0.64 \times 80 + 70 = 25.6 - 51.2 + 70 = 44.4\text{.}\) The cheapest speed is \(80\,\text{km/h}\text{,}\) costing Ksh \(44.40\) per km.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Type whole numbers or fractions, for example -3 or 5/2. Type powers with ^, for example 2x^3-5x+1.

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    Two positive numbers \(x\) and \(y\) have \(x + y = 12\text{.}\) Find the greatest value of \(xy^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(256\text{,}\) when \(x = 4\) and \(y = 8\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(xy^2 = (12 - y)y^2 = 12y^2 - y^3\text{,}\) whose derivative \(24y - 3y^2 = 3y(8 - y)\) is \(0\) at \(y = 8\) (the second derivative \(24 - 6y = -24 \lt 0\)). Then \(x = 4\) and \(xy^2 = 256\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Achieng has \(48\,\text{m}\) of fencing to make a rectangular goat pen divided into two equal parts by a fence parallel to one side. This uses three lengths \(x\) m and two lengths \(y\) m. Find the greatest total area.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(96\,\text{m}^2\text{,}\) with \(x = 8\) and \(y = 12\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(3x + 2y = 48\text{,}\) so \(y = 24 - 1.5x\) and \(A = xy = 24x - 1.5x^2\text{.}\) \(\dfrac{dA}{dx} = 24 - 3x = 0\) at \(x = 8\text{;}\) \(y = 12\text{;}\) \(A = 96\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    An open box is made from a sheet of card \(21\,\text{cm}\) by \(16\,\text{cm}\) by cutting squares of side \(x\) cm from the corners. Find the dimensions of the box with the greatest volume.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(15\,\text{cm}\) by \(10\,\text{cm}\) by \(3\,\text{cm}\text{;}\) volume \(450\,\text{cm}^3\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(V = x(21 - 2x)(16 - 2x) = 4x^3 - 74x^2 + 336x\) for \(0 \lt x \lt 8\text{.}\) \(\dfrac{dV}{dx} = 12x^2 - 148x + 336 = 4(3x - 28)(x - 3)\text{,}\) zero at \(x = 3\) or \(x = \dfrac{28}{3}\text{;}\) but \(\dfrac{28}{3} \gt 8\) is outside the domain. \(\dfrac{d^2V}{dx^2} = 24x - 148 = -76\) at \(x = 3\text{:}\) a maximum.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    A bus company charges Ksh \(p\) for a ticket from Nairobi to Nakuru, and carries \(1600 - 2p\) passengers a day. Find the fare that gives the greatest daily takings, and the takings at that fare.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Ksh \(400\text{;}\) \(800\) passengers; Ksh \(320\,000\) a day
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    A jua kali workshop in Kamukunji makes \(x\) stools a week. Its weekly profit, \(P\) hundred shillings, is modelled by \(P = -x^3 + 18x^2 - 60x - 40\) for \(0 \le x \le 12\text{.}\) Find and classify the stationary points. How many stools give the greatest profit?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \((2, -96)\) minimum point; \((10, 160)\) maximum point. \(P(0) = -40\) and \(P(12) = 104\text{,}\) so \(10\) stools give the greatest profit, Ksh \(16\,000\) a week.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\dfrac{dP}{dx} = -3x^2 + 36x - 60 = -3(x - 2)(x - 10)\text{.}\) \(\dfrac{d^2P}{dx^2} = -6x + 36\) is \(24\) at \(x = 2\) and \(-24\) at \(x = 10\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    The best cut for an open box made from a square sheet of side \(s\) cm is \(x = 5\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Find \(s\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(s = 30\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(V = x(s - 2x)^2\) gives \(\dfrac{dV}{dx} = (s - 2x)(s - 6x)\text{,}\) so the best cut is \(x = \dfrac{s}{6}\text{,}\) and \(\dfrac{s}{6} = 5\) gives \(s = 30\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    7. Challenge: The Square Is Best.

    Show that of all rectangles with perimeter \(4k\text{,}\) the square has the greatest area.
    🔗
    Hint.
    If one side is \(x\text{,}\) the other is \(2k - x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(A = x(2k - x)\text{;}\) \(\dfrac{dA}{dx} = 2k - 2x = 0\) at \(x = k\text{,}\) and \(\dfrac{d^2A}{dx^2} = -2 \lt 0\text{.}\) Both sides are then \(k\text{:}\) a square.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 13.3.2 Greatest and Least Values on an Interval

    Learner Experience 13.3.12 Peak or Edge?

    Let \(f(x) = x^3 - 12x^2 + 36x + 10\text{.}\)
    🔗
    1. Find and classify the stationary points of \(y = f(x)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find \(f(1)\) and \(f(9)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Find the greatest and least values of \(f(x)\) for \(1 \le x \le 9\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. What are the greatest and least values for \(1 \le x \le 5\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 13.3.13 Greatest and Least Values.

    The greatest value of \(f(x)\) on an interval \(a \le x \le b\) is the largest value it takes there; the least value is the smallest. Each occurs either at a stationary point inside the interval or at one of the ends, \(x = a\) or \(x = b\text{.}\) So work out \(f\) at each of these candidates and compare. Stationary points outside the interval are ignored.
    🔗
    The best value of a quantity is at a stationary point or at an end of its domain.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.3.14 Checking the Ends.

    Find the greatest and least values of \(f(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 12x + 5\) for \(-2 \le x \le 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(f'(x) = 6x^2 - 6x - 12 = 6(x + 1)(x - 2)\text{,}\) so the stationary points are at \(x = -1\) and \(x = 2\text{,}\) both inside the interval.
    🔗
    The greatest value is \(37\text{,}\) at the end \(x = 4\text{.}\) The least value is \(-15\text{,}\) at the minimum point \(x = 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.3.17 The Least Metal for a Water Tank (Extension).

    A closed cylindrical water tank for a school in Kitui must hold \(16\pi\,\text{m}^3\text{.}\) Find the radius and height that use the least metal.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Let the radius be \(r\) m and the height \(h\) m (Figure 13.3.18). The volume gives the constraint \(\pi r^2 h = 16\pi\text{,}\) so \(h = \dfrac{16}{r^2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The metal is the surface area: two circles and the curved side.
    \begin{equation*} A = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h = 2\pi r^2 + \frac{32\pi}{r} = 2\pi r^2 + 32\pi r^{-1}, \qquad r \gt 0 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Using the Grade 11 power rule for negative powers, \(\dfrac{dA}{dr} = 4\pi r - 32\pi r^{-2} = 4\pi r - \dfrac{32\pi}{r^2}\text{.}\) This is \(0\) when \(r^3 = 8\text{,}\) so \(r = 2\) and \(h = \dfrac{16}{4} = 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \(\dfrac{d^2A}{dr^2} = 4\pi + \dfrac{64\pi}{r^3} \gt 0\text{,}\) so this is a minimum. The tank should have radius \(2\,\text{m}\) and height \(4\,\text{m}\text{,}\) using \(8\pi + 16\pi = 24\pi \approx 75.4\,\text{m}^2\) of metal.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.3.19 A Workshop at Full Capacity.

    The Kamukunji workshop of Exercise 13.3.1.5 can make at most \(8\) stools a week, so its profit \(P = -x^3 + 18x^2 - 60x - 40\) (hundred shillings) applies for \(0 \le x \le 8\text{.}\) How many stools give the greatest profit?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\dfrac{dP}{dx} = -3(x - 2)(x - 10)\text{.}\) The stationary point at \(x = 2\) is inside the interval (\(P = -96\text{,}\) a minimum point); the one at \(x = 10\) is outside it and is ignored.
    🔗
    The candidates are \(P(0) = -40\text{,}\) \(P(2) = -96\) and \(P(8) = -512 + 1152 - 480 - 40 = 120\text{.}\) The greatest profit is \(120\) hundred shillings, Ksh \(12\,000\) a week, at \(x = 8\text{:}\) at full capacity, an end of the interval.

    Subsection 13.3.3 Kinematics: Distance, Speed and Greatest Velocity

    Learner Experience 13.3.22 A Drone over the Tea.

    Key Takeaway 13.3.23 Displacement, Distance and Speed.

    As in Grade 11, a particle moves along a straight line; \(s\) metres is its displacement from a fixed point \(O\) at time \(t\) seconds (\(t \ge 0\)), \(v = \dfrac{ds}{dt}\) is its velocity in \(\text{m/s}\) and \(a = \dfrac{dv}{dt} = \dfrac{d^2s}{dt^2}\) its acceleration in \(\text{m/s}^2\text{.}\) When \(v = 0\) the particle is instantaneously at rest; it may change direction there.
    🔗
    Displacement is where you are; distance travelled is how far you went. To find the distance travelled, split the motion at the times when the particle is at rest, and add the distances moved in each stage:
    \begin{equation*} \text{distance} = |s(t_1) - s(0)| + |s(t_2) - s(t_1)| + \cdots \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The speed is the size of the velocity, \(|v|\text{.}\) The velocity is greatest or least where \(a = \dfrac{dv}{dt} = 0\text{,}\) or at an end of the time interval. The particle is speeding up when \(v\) and \(a\) have the same sign (pulling the same way), and slowing down when their signs differ.
    🔗
    🔗

    Warning 13.3.24 Displacement Is Not Distance.

    After \(12\) seconds the drone is \(36\,\text{m}\) from the post, but it has flown \(44\,\text{m}\text{,}\) because it turned back between \(t = 4\) and \(t = 8\text{.}\) Whenever a particle is at rest during the motion, check whether it changes direction there before you find the distance.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.3.26 Distance Travelled.

    A particle moves so that \(s = t^3 - 12t^2 + 36t\) metres after \(t\) seconds. Find its displacement at \(t = 8\) and the distance it travels in the first \(8\) seconds.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(v = 3t^2 - 24t + 36 = 3(t - 2)(t - 6)\text{,}\) so the particle is at rest at \(t = 2\) and \(t = 6\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \(s(0) = 0\text{,}\) \(s(2) = 8 - 48 + 72 = 32\text{,}\) \(s(6) = 216 - 432 + 216 = 0\text{,}\) \(s(8) = 512 - 768 + 288 = 32\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The displacement at \(t = 8\) is \(32\,\text{m}\text{.}\) The distance is \(|32 - 0| + |0 - 32| + |32 - 0| = 32 + 32 + 32 = 96\,\text{m}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.3.27 A Sprinter’s Greatest Velocity.

    During the first \(10\) seconds of a race at Kasarani, a sprinter’s velocity is modelled by \(v = 2.4t - 0.12t^2\,\text{m/s}\text{.}\) Find the sprinter’s greatest velocity, and the acceleration at the start.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(a = \dfrac{dv}{dt} = 2.4 - 0.24t\text{,}\) which is \(0\) at \(t = 10\text{.}\) Before that \(a \gt 0\text{,}\) so \(v\) increases up to \(t = 10\text{:}\) \(v(10) = 24 - 12 = 12\,\text{m/s}\) is the greatest velocity.
    🔗
    At the start, \(a(0) = 2.4\,\text{m/s}^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.3.28 Speeding Up or Slowing Down?

    A particle moves so that \(s = 2t^3 - 15t^2 + 24t\) metres after \(t\) seconds. Describe its motion at \(t = 2\text{,}\) \(t = 3\) and \(t = 5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(v = 6t^2 - 30t + 24 = 6(t - 1)(t - 4)\) and \(a = 12t - 30\text{.}\)
    🔗
    At \(t = 2\text{:}\) \(v = -12\text{,}\) \(a = -6\text{.}\) Same signs: the particle is moving in the negative direction and speeding up.
    🔗
    At \(t = 3\text{:}\) \(v = -12\text{,}\) \(a = 6\text{.}\) Opposite signs: moving in the negative direction and slowing down.
    🔗
    At \(t = 5\text{:}\) \(v = 24\text{,}\) \(a = 30\text{.}\) Same signs: moving in the positive direction and speeding up.

    Where Differentiation Is Used.

    Every example in this chapter is a real kind of decision. Engineers find the least material for a part, as for the water tank in Example 13.3.17. Traders choose the price that gives the greatest profit. Doctors plan doses so that the concentration of a medicine reaches its greatest value safely. Coaches study when an athlete reaches top speed. In each case the answer comes from where a derivative is zero.
