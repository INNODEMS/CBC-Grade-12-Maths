    Learner Experience 8.2.1 Sliding a Pencil Along a Box.

    A shoebox \(ABCDEFGH\) has \(AB = 24\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) \(BC = 14\,\text{cm}\) and \(AE = 10\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) \(CH\) is a diagonal of the back face \(DCGH\text{.}\) The lines \(AB\) and \(CH\) never meet and are not parallel: they are skew.
    🔗
    1. Hold the pencil along \(AB\text{.}\) Which edges of the box are parallel to \(AB\) and touch the line \(CH\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Slide the pencil, keeping it parallel to \(AB\text{,}\) until it lies along \(DC\text{.}\) It now meets \(CH\) at \(C\text{.}\) Measure angle \(DCH\) with the protractor.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Triangle \(DCH\) has a right angle at \(D\text{.}\) Use tan to calculate angle \(DCH\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Slide the pencil to \(HG\) instead and find angle \(GHC\text{.}\) Compare it with angle \(DCH\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Find the angle between \(AB\) and \(CG\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Report your measured angle and your calculated angle side by side. If they differ, keep both: the difference tells you how accurate the measuring was.
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 8.2.3 Angle Between Skew Lines.

    The angle between two skew lines is found by moving one of them, without turning it, to a parallel line that meets the other. The angle between these two meeting lines is the angle between the skew lines. In short: slide one line, without turning it, until it meets the other.
    🔗
    1. Find a line parallel to the first line that passes through a vertex of the second line.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Name the angle between this parallel line and the second line, with three letters.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Find a triangle that contains the angle, draw it flat, and calculate the angle.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    The angle between two lines is never more than \(90^{\circ}\text{:}\) if the triangle gives an obtuse angle, the angle between the lines is \(180^{\circ}\) minus it. Skew lines at \(90^{\circ}\) to each other are called perpendicular, even though they do not meet.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.2.5 Skew Lines in a Cuboid.

    A packing crate in a shop is a cuboid \(ABCDEFGH\) with \(AB = 16\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) \(BC = 10\,\text{cm}\) and \(AE = 12\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Find the angle between (a) \(AB\) and \(FH\text{;}\) (b) \(BC\) and \(DH\text{;}\) (c) \(AE\) and \(BG\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    (a) \(AB\) is parallel to \(EF\text{,}\) which meets \(FH\) at \(F\text{.}\) The angle is angle \(EFH\text{,}\) in the top face, where triangle \(EFH\) has a right angle at \(E\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \tan EFH = \frac{EH}{EF} = \frac{10}{16}, \quad \text{angle } EFH = 32.0^{\circ} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    (b) \(BC\) is parallel to \(AD\text{,}\) which meets \(DH\) at \(D\text{.}\) \(DH\) is vertical and \(AD\) is horizontal, so the angle is \(90^{\circ}\text{:}\) \(BC\) and \(DH\) are perpendicular.
    🔗
    (c) \(AE\) is parallel to \(BF\text{,}\) which meets \(BG\) at \(B\text{.}\) The angle is angle \(FBG\text{,}\) in the face \(BCGF\text{,}\) where triangle \(FBG\) has a right angle at \(F\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \tan FBG = \frac{FG}{BF} = \frac{10}{12}, \quad \text{angle } FBG = 39.8^{\circ} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.2.6 Skew Lines in a Pyramid.

    A model of a right pyramid \(VABCD\) stands on a square base \(ABCD\) of side \(12\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) and each sloping edge is \(15\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Find the angle between the skew lines \(VA\) and \(BC\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(BC\) is parallel to \(AD\text{,}\) which meets \(VA\) at \(A\text{,}\) so the angle is angle \(VAD\text{,}\) in the face \(VAD\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The face \(VAD\) is isosceles, with \(VA = VD = 15\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) The perpendicular from \(V\) to \(AD\) meets it at its midpoint, \(6\,\text{cm}\) from \(A\text{.}\) In the right-angled triangle this makes,
    \begin{equation*} \cos VAD = \frac{6}{15}, \quad \text{angle } VAD = 66.4^{\circ} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.2.8 The Ridge of a Tent.

    The tent \(ABCDEF\) in Figure 8.1.11 has \(AB = 2.4\,\text{m}\text{,}\) \(CA = CB = 1.5\,\text{m}\) and \(AD = 3\,\text{m}\text{.}\) Find the angle between (a) \(AC\) and \(EF\text{;}\) (b) \(AB\) and the ridge \(CF\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    (a) \(EF\) is parallel to \(BC\text{,}\) which meets \(AC\) at \(C\text{,}\) so we need angle \(ACB\) in the end of the tent, drawn flat in Figure 8.2.9. The height \(CN\) cuts the isosceles triangle into two right-angled triangles with \(AN = 1.2\,\text{m}\) and \(CA = 1.5\,\text{m}\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \sin ACN = \frac{1.2}{1.5}, \quad \text{angle } ACN = 53.13^{\circ}, \quad \text{angle } ACB = 2 \times 53.13^{\circ} = 106.3^{\circ} \end{equation*}
    This angle is obtuse. The angle between two lines is never more than \(90^{\circ}\text{,}\) so the angle between \(AC\) and \(EF\) is \(180^{\circ} - 106.3^{\circ} = 73.7^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) \(CF\) is parallel to \(AD\text{,}\) which meets \(AB\) at \(A\text{.}\) The ground \(ABED\) is a rectangle, so angle \(BAD = 90^{\circ}\text{:}\) \(AB\) and the ridge are perpendicular.
    🔗
    🔗

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    A cuboid \(ABCDEFGH\) has \(AB = 20\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) \(BC = 15\,\text{cm}\) and \(AE = 8\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Find the angle between (a) \(AB\) and \(CG\text{;}\) (b) \(AB\) and \(DG\text{;}\) (c) \(EH\) and \(BG\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(90^{\circ}\) (b) \(21.8^{\circ}\) (c) \(28.1^{\circ}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    (a) Slide \(AB\) to \(DC\text{,}\) which meets \(CG\) at a right angle: \(90^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) Slide \(AB\) to \(DC\text{,}\) which meets \(DG\) at \(D\text{.}\) Angle \(CDG\text{:}\) \(\tan CDG = \frac{CG}{DC} = \frac{8}{20}\text{,}\) so \(21.8^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (c) \(EH\) is parallel to \(FG\text{,}\) which meets \(BG\) at \(G\text{.}\) Angle \(BGF\text{:}\) \(\tan BGF = \frac{BF}{FG} = \frac{8}{15}\text{,}\) so \(28.1^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    \(ABCDEFGH\) is a cube. Find the angle between \(AB\) and \(CH\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(45^{\circ}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(AB\) is parallel to \(DC\text{,}\) which meets \(CH\) at \(C\text{.}\) Angle \(DCH\) is between a side and a diagonal of the square face \(DCGH\text{,}\) so it is \(45^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    A right pyramid \(VABCD\) stands on a rectangular base with \(AB = 16\,\text{cm}\) and \(BC = 12\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Every sloping edge is \(14\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Find the angle between (a) \(VA\) and \(BC\text{;}\) (b) \(VA\) and \(CD\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(64.6^{\circ}\) (b) \(55.2^{\circ}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    (a) \(BC\) is parallel to \(AD\) (\(12\,\text{cm}\)), which meets \(VA\) at \(A\text{.}\) In the isosceles face \(VAD\text{,}\) \(\cos VAD = \frac{6}{14}\text{,}\) so angle \(VAD = 64.6^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) \(CD\) is parallel to \(AB\) (\(16\,\text{cm}\)). In the isosceles face \(VAB\text{,}\) \(\cos VAB = \frac{8}{14}\text{,}\) so angle \(VAB = 55.2^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    A classroom is a cuboid \(ABCDEFGH\) with \(AB = 9\,\text{m}\text{,}\) \(BC = 7\,\text{m}\) and \(AE = 3\,\text{m}\text{.}\) Find the angle between the floor edge \(AD\) and the diagonal \(CF\) of the end wall \(BCGF\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(23.2^{\circ}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(AD\) is parallel to \(BC\text{,}\) which meets \(CF\) at \(C\text{.}\) In the end wall, triangle \(BCF\) has a right angle at \(B\text{:}\) \(\tan BCF = \frac{BF}{BC} = \frac{3}{7}\text{,}\) so angle \(BCF = 23.2^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Explain why, in any cuboid \(ABCDEFGH\text{,}\) the angle between the vertical edge \(AE\) and any line in the base that does not pass through \(A\) is \(90^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Slide \(AE\) to the vertical line through a point of the base line; a vertical line is perpendicular to every line in the horizontal base through its foot.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Challenge: Perpendicular Face Diagonals.

    \(ABCDEFGH\) is a cube. Show that the face diagonals \(AF\) and \(CH\) are perpendicular.
    🔗
    Hint.
    \(AF\) is parallel to \(DG\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    The angle between them is \(90^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    Slide \(AF\text{,}\) without turning it, to \(DG\text{,}\) the diagonal of the opposite face that slopes the same way. \(DG\) and \(CH\) are the two diagonals of the square \(DCGH\text{,}\) and the diagonals of a square cross at right angles. So the angle between \(AF\) and \(CH\) is \(90^{\circ}\text{.}\)
