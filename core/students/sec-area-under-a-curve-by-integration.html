    Subsection 14.3.1 Area and the Definite Integral

    Learner Experience 14.3.1 Area Under a Straight Line.

    A plan of a shamba drawn on squared paper shows a region bounded by the line \(y = 2x + 1\text{,}\) the \(x\)-axis, the \(y\)-axis and the line \(x = X\text{.}\) Let \(A(X)\) be its area.
    🔗
    1. Each region is a trapezium. Find \(A(1)\text{,}\) \(A(2)\) and \(A(3)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Guess a formula for \(A(X)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Differentiate \(A(X)\text{.}\) What do you notice?
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Find the area under the line from \(x = 1\) to \(x = 3\) as \(A(3) - A(1)\text{.}\) Check it with the area of a trapezium.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_{1}^{3} (2x + 1)\,dx\) and compare.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 14.3.3 Area Under a Curve.

    If \(f(x) \ge 0\) for \(a \le x \le b\text{,}\) the area of the region between the curve \(y = f(x)\text{,}\) the \(x\)-axis and the lines \(x = a\) and \(x = b\) is
    \begin{equation*} \text{area} = \int_{a}^{b} f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)\text{,} \end{equation*}
    where \(F\) is any integral of \(f\text{.}\) The region is shaded in Figure 14.3.7. Areas are in square units, or in \(\text{m}^2\) when the lengths are in metres.
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 14.3.4 Why Integration Gives Area.

    Let \(A(x)\) be the area under the curve from \(a\) up to \(x\text{.}\) Moving \(x\) on by a small amount \(\delta x\) adds a thin strip of width \(\delta x\) and height about \(y\text{,}\) so \(\delta A \approx y\,\delta x\text{.}\) Dividing by \(\delta x\) and letting the strip get thinner gives \(\dfrac{dA}{dx} = y\text{:}\) the area function is an integral of \(y\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 14.3.6 Area Under a Parabola.

    Find the area of the region between the curve \(y = x^2 + 2\text{,}\) the \(x\)-axis and the lines \(x = 1\) and \(x = 3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The curve is above the \(x\)-axis, so
    \begin{equation*} \text{area} = \int_{1}^{3} (x^2 + 2)\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} + 2x \right]_{1}^{3} = (9 + 6) - \left(\frac{1}{3} + 2\right) = \frac{38}{3} \approx 12.67 \end{equation*}
    square units.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 14.3.8 Area Under a Cubic.

    Find the area between the curve \(y = x^3 + 1\text{,}\) the \(x\)-axis and the lines \(x = 0\) and \(x = 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    For \(0 \le x \le 2\text{,}\) \(y \ge 1\text{,}\) so the curve is above the axis.
    \begin{equation*} \int_{0}^{2} (x^3 + 1)\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^4}{4} + x \right]_{0}^{2} = (4 + 2) - 0 = 6 \end{equation*}
    The area is \(6\) square units.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 14.3.9 A Shamba With a Curved Boundary.

    A shamba in Nyeri is drawn on a plan with axes in units of \(10\,\text{m}\text{.}\) It is bounded by the \(x\)-axis (a road), the lines \(x = 0\) and \(x = 4\) (two fences) and a stream along the curve \(y = 6 + 2x - \frac{x^2}{2}\text{.}\) Find the area of the shamba in square metres.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Check the curve is above the axis: \(y = 6\) at \(x = 0\text{,}\) \(8\) at \(x = 2\) and \(6\) at \(x = 4\text{,}\) and a parabola that bends down stays above its two end heights between them. So
    \begin{equation*} \text{area} = \int_{0}^{4} \left(6 + 2x - \frac{x^2}{2}\right)dx = \left[ 6x + x^2 - \frac{x^3}{6} \right]_{0}^{4} = 24 + 16 - \frac{64}{6} = \frac{88}{3} \approx 29.33 \end{equation*}
    square units. One square unit is \(10\,\text{m} \times 10\,\text{m} = 100\,\text{m}^2\text{,}\) so the shamba is about \(2933\,\text{m}^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Type exact values as whole numbers, fractions or decimals, for example 27/4 or 6.75.
    🔗

    7. Challenge: Equal Areas.

    The area under \(y = 3x^2 + k\) from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 2\) equals the area under the same curve from \(x = 2\) to \(x = 3\text{,}\) where \(k\) is a positive constant. Find \(k\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Write both areas in terms of \(k\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(k = 11\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\int_{0}^{2} (3x^2 + k)\,dx = 8 + 2k\) and \(\int_{2}^{3} (3x^2 + k)\,dx = 19 + k\text{.}\) So \(8 + 2k = 19 + k\) and \(k = 11\text{.}\)

    Subsection 14.3.2 Areas Above and Below the \(x\)-axis

    Key Takeaway 14.3.13 Regions Below the Axis.

    The definite integral adds up thin strips of height \(y\text{.}\) Below the \(x\)-axis \(y\) is negative, so a region below the axis gives a negative integral; its area is the size of the integral.
    🔗
    When the curve crosses the axis between the limits, find the \(x\)-intercepts, integrate each part separately, and add the sizes of the answers. Always sketch the curve first.
    🔗
    🔗

    Warning 14.3.14 Integrating Across the Axis.

    One integral across a crossing point gives the area above the axis minus the area below it, not their sum. In Learner Experience 14.3.11 it gives \(0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 14.3.15 Area Enclosed by a Parabola.

    Find the area enclosed by the curve \(y = (x + 1)(3 - x)\) and the \(x\)-axis.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The curve crosses the axis at \(x = -1\) and \(x = 3\text{,}\) and is above it between them (at \(x = 0\text{,}\) \(y = 3\)). Expand: \(y = 3 + 2x - x^2\text{.}\)
    \begin{equation*} \int_{-1}^{3} (3 + 2x - x^2)\,dx = \left[ 3x + x^2 - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{-1}^{3} = 9 - \left(-\frac{5}{3}\right) = \frac{32}{3} \approx 10.67 \end{equation*}
    The area is \(\frac{32}{3}\) square units.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 14.3.16 A Region Below the Axis.

    Find the area between the curve \(y = x^2 - 5x + 4\) and the \(x\)-axis.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(y = (x - 1)(x - 4)\text{,}\) so the curve crosses the axis at \(x = 1\) and \(x = 4\) and is below it between them.
    \begin{equation*} \int_{1}^{4} (x^2 - 5x + 4)\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{5x^2}{2} + 4x \right]_{1}^{4} = \left(-\frac{8}{3}\right) - \frac{11}{6} = -\frac{9}{2} \end{equation*}
    The integral is negative because the region is below the axis. The area is \(\frac{9}{2} = 4.5\) square units.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 14.3.17 A Region Above and Below.

    Find the total area between the curve \(y = x^3 - 4x\text{,}\) the \(x\)-axis and the lines \(x = 0\) and \(x = 3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(y = x(x - 2)(x + 2)\text{,}\) so between \(0\) and \(3\) the curve crosses the axis at \(x = 2\text{.}\) Integrate each part, with \(F(x) = \frac{x^4}{4} - 2x^2\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} \int_{0}^{2} (x^3 - 4x)\,dx \amp = F(2) - F(0) = (4 - 8) - 0 = -4\\ \int_{2}^{3} (x^3 - 4x)\,dx \amp = F(3) - F(2) = \left(\frac{81}{4} - 18\right) - (-4) = \frac{25}{4} \end{align*}
    The total area is \(4 + \frac{25}{4} = \frac{41}{4} = 10.25\) square units. The single integral \(\int_{0}^{3} (x^3 - 4x)\,dx = \frac{9}{4}\) is not the area: the part below the axis has cancelled part of the area above.
    🔗

    1.

    Find the area enclosed by the curve \(y = 16 - x^2\) and the \(x\)-axis.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\frac{256}{3} \approx 85.33\) square units
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The curve crosses the axis at \(x = \pm 4\text{.}\) \(\left[ 16x - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{-4}^{4} = \frac{128}{3} - \left(-\frac{128}{3}\right) = \frac{256}{3}\text{.}\)

    2.

    Find the area between the curve \(y = x^2 - 6x\) and the \(x\)-axis.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(36\) square units
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(y = x(x - 6)\text{,}\) below the axis for \(0 \lt x \lt 6\text{.}\) \(\left[ \frac{x^3}{3} - 3x^2 \right]_{0}^{6} = 72 - 108 = -36\text{,}\) so the area is \(36\text{.}\)

    3.

    Find the total area enclosed by the curve \(y = x^3 - x^2 - 2x\) and the \(x\)-axis.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\frac{37}{12} \approx 3.08\) square units
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(y = x(x - 2)(x + 1)\text{,}\) crossing at \(x = -1\text{,}\) \(0\text{,}\) \(2\text{.}\) With \(F(x) = \frac{x^4}{4} - \frac{x^3}{3} - x^2\text{:}\) \(\int_{-1}^{0} y\,dx = \frac{5}{12}\) (above) and \(\int_{0}^{2} y\,dx = -\frac{8}{3}\) (below). Total area \(\frac{5}{12} + \frac{8}{3} = \frac{37}{12}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    A pipe joins two tanks. Water flows through it at \(r = 6 - 2t\) litres per second for \(0 \le t \le 5\text{,}\) into the first tank when \(r\) is positive and out of it when \(r\) is negative. Find the net change in the amount of water in the first tank, and the total amount of water that passes through the pipe.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Net change \(5\,\text{litres}\text{;}\) total \(13\,\text{litres}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(r = 0\) at \(t = 3\text{.}\) \(\int_{0}^{3} (6 - 2t)\,dt = 9\,\text{litres}\) in; \(\int_{3}^{5} (6 - 2t)\,dt = -4\text{,}\) so \(4\,\text{litres}\) out. Net change \(9 - 4 = 5\,\text{litres}\) (which is \(\int_{0}^{5} r\,dt\)); total through the pipe \(9 + 4 = 13\,\text{litres}\text{.}\)

    5.

    Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2} x^3\,dx\text{.}\) Find the area between \(y = x^3\text{,}\) the \(x\)-axis and the lines \(x = -2\) and \(x = 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(0\text{;}\) \(8\) square units
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\left[ \frac{x^4}{4} \right]_{-2}^{2} = 4 - 4 = 0\text{.}\) Split at \(x = 0\text{:}\) each part has size \(4\text{,}\) so the area is \(8\text{.}\)

    6. Challenge: An Unknown Root.

    The curve \(y = x(k - x)\text{,}\) where \(k \gt 0\text{,}\) encloses an area of \(36\) square units with the \(x\)-axis. Find \(k\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Show that the area is \(\frac{k^3}{6}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(k = 6\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The curve crosses the axis at \(0\) and \(k\text{.}\) \(\left[ \frac{kx^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{0}^{k} = \frac{k^3}{2} - \frac{k^3}{3} = \frac{k^3}{6}\text{.}\) So \(k^3 = 216\) and \(k = 6\text{.}\)
