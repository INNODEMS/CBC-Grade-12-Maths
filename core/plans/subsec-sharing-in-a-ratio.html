    Subsection 3.1.1 Sharing in a Ratio

    Upon completion of this lesson, learners will be able to:
    1. Know: a ratio, its parts, and a share.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Do: share a quantity in a two-part or three-part ratio; find the total or the other shares from one share or from the difference between two shares.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Apply: sharing money, stationery and farm inputs.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Teacher Resource 3.1.3 Problem-Solving and Discovery.

    Suggested Time
    🔗
    14 minutes
    🔗
    Objective
    🔗
    Learners share real counters in given ratios and discover that the key step is to find one part first.
    🔗
    Teacher’s Role
    🔗
    Give each group of three \(60\) bottle tops (or beans, or sweets). Before any rule, ask: “How many bottle tops is one part?”
    🔗
    🔗

    Learner Experience 3.1.4 Sharing Bottle Tops.

    Work in groups of three. Your group has \(60\) bottle tops.
    🔗
    1. Share the bottle tops in the ratio \(1 : 4 : 5\text{.}\) How many does each of you get?
      🔗
      Solution.
      There are \(1 + 4 + 5 = 10\) parts, so one part is \(60 \div 10 = 6\text{.}\) The shares are \(6\text{,}\) \(24\) and \(30\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Share them again in the ratio \(1 : 5 : 6\text{.}\)
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(12\) parts of \(5\text{:}\) \(5\text{,}\) \(25\) and \(30\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Share them in the ratio \(5 : 7 : 8\text{.}\)
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(20\) parts of \(3\text{:}\) \(15\text{,}\) \(21\) and \(24\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Try to share them in the ratio \(2 : 6 : 9\text{.}\) What goes wrong?
      🔗
      Solution.
      There are \(17\) parts, and \(60 \div 17\) is not a whole number, so some bottle tops are left over (or would have to be cut).
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Is sharing equally always fair? When would sharing in a ratio be fairer?
      🔗
      Solution.
      Open. Sharing in a ratio is fairer when people did different amounts of work or put in different amounts of money.
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Teacher Resource 3.1.5 Structured Instruction.

    Suggested Time
    🔗
    8 minutes
    🔗
    Objective
    🔗
    To formalise the sharing method from the activity.
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaways & Teacher Connection.

    1. Connecting to Learner Work: in every part of the activity the groups found one part first: \(60\) divided by the total number of parts.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Each share is its number of parts times one part.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. The shares must add back to the total; this is the check.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 3.1.6 Sharing in a Ratio.

    A ratio such as \(5 : 7 : 8\) compares quantities. Its numbers are parts, and the amount each person gets is a share. To share a total in the ratio \(a : b : c\text{:}\)
    1. Total number of parts: \(a + b + c\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. One part: total \(\div (a + b + c)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Each share: its number of parts \(\times\) one part.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Check: the shares add up to the total.
      🔗
      🔗
    So the first person gets \(\dfrac{a}{a + b + c}\) of the total. Find one part first, then each share is a number of parts.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.1.9 Stationery for Three Classes.

    A school shares \(840\) exercise books among classes 12A, 12B and 12C in the ratio \(7 : 8 : 9\text{.}\) How many does each class get?
    🔗

    Solution.

    Total parts: \(7 + 8 + 9 = 24\text{.}\) One part: \(840 \div 24 = 35\) books.
    🔗
    12A gets \(7 \times 35 = 245\text{,}\) 12B gets \(8 \times 35 = 280\) and 12C gets \(9 \times 35 = 315\) exercise books.
    🔗
    Check: \(245 + 280 + 315 = 840\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.1.10 Shares from One Share.

    Achieng, Baraka and Chebet share the profit from their poultry project in the ratio \(3 : 4 : 6\text{.}\) Chebet gets Ksh \(9000\text{.}\) Find the total profit and the other two shares.
    🔗

    Solution.

    Chebet’s \(6\) parts are Ksh \(9000\text{,}\) so one part is \(9000 \div 6 = 1500\text{.}\)
    🔗
    There are \(3 + 4 + 6 = 13\) parts, so the total profit is \(13 \times 1500 = 19\,500\text{,}\) Ksh \(19\,500\text{.}\) Achieng gets \(3 \times 1500 = 4500\) and Baraka \(4 \times 1500 = 6000\text{:}\) Ksh \(4500\) and Ksh \(6000\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.1.11 Shares from a Difference.

    Two dairy co-operatives share their income from a milk cooler in the ratio \(5 : 2\text{.}\) The first gets Ksh \(36\,000\) more than the second. Find each share and the total.
    🔗

    Solution.

    The difference is \(5 - 2 = 3\) parts, so \(3\) parts are Ksh \(36\,000\) and one part is \(12\,000\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The first gets \(5 \times 12\,000 = 60\,000\) and the second \(2 \times 12\,000 = 24\,000\text{:}\) Ksh \(60\,000\) and Ksh \(24\,000\text{,}\) Ksh \(84\,000\) in all.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    Share Ksh \(4800\) in the ratio \(7 : 9\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Ksh \(2100\) and Ksh \(2700\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Divide \(1260\,\text{kg}\) of fertiliser among three farmers in the ratio \(4 : 5 : 12\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(240\,\text{kg}\text{,}\) \(300\,\text{kg}\) and \(720\,\text{kg}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(4 + 5 + 12 = 21\) parts, and one part is \(1260 \div 21 = 60\,\text{kg}\text{.}\) The shares are \(4 \times 60 = 240\text{,}\) \(5 \times 60 = 300\) and \(12 \times 60 = 720\,\text{kg}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    A matatu SACCO shares Ksh \(96\,000\) among three drivers, Otieno, Kamau and Wekesa, in the ratio of the days they worked, \(6 : 5 : 5\text{.}\) How much does each driver get?
    🔗
    Answer.
    Otieno Ksh \(36\,000\text{;}\) Kamau and Wekesa Ksh \(30\,000\) each
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Mangoes are sorted into grades A, B and C in the ratio \(9 : 5 : 2\text{.}\) There are \(180\) grade A mangoes. How many mangoes are there altogether, and how many are grade C?
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(320\) mangoes; \(40\) grade C
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(9\) parts are \(180\text{,}\) so one part is \(20\text{.}\) There are \(16\) parts, \(16 \times 20 = 320\) mangoes, and grade C is \(2 \times 20 = 40\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Money is shared in the ratio \(7 : 3\text{.}\) The larger share is Ksh \(2800\) more than the smaller. Find the total.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Ksh \(7000\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The difference is \(4\) parts, so one part is \(2800 \div 4 = 700\text{.}\) The total is \(10 \times 700 = 7000\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6. Challenge: Which Totals Share Exactly?

    Can \(100\) bottle tops be shared exactly in the ratio \(2 : 6 : 9\text{?}\) Which totals from \(100\) to \(120\) can be?
    🔗
    Hint.
    How many parts are there? Which totals divide exactly by that number?
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    No; \(102\) and \(119\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    There are \(2 + 6 + 9 = 17\) parts, and \(100\) is not a multiple of \(17\text{.}\) The multiples of \(17\) from \(100\) to \(120\) are \(102 = 6 \times 17\) and \(119 = 7 \times 17\text{.}\)
