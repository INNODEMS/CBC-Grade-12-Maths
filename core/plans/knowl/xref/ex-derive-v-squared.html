Exercise 6. Challenge: Another Constant-Acceleration Equation.

Starting from \(v = u + at\) and \(s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2\text{,}\) show that \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Make \(t\) the subject of the first equation and substitute into the second.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Shown.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\(t = \dfrac{v - u}{a}\text{,}\) so \(s = \dfrac{u(v - u)}{a} + \dfrac{(v - u)^2}{2a}\text{.}\) Multiply by \(2a\text{:}\) \(2as = 2uv - 2u^2 + v^2 - 2uv + u^2 = v^2 - u^2\text{.}\)
