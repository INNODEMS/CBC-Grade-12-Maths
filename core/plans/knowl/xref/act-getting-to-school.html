Learner Experience 12.2.2 Getting to School.

The table shows the journey times to school, in minutes (to the nearest minute), of \(40\) learners in Nakuru.
🔗
  1. Add a row of running totals: the number of learners in each class and all the classes before it.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(4, 13, 26, 34, 38, 40\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The first running total, \(4\text{,}\) belongs at a time on the horizontal axis. Why should it go at \(14.5\) minutes, and not at \(10\) or \(14\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Every learner in 5–14 took less than \(14.5\) minutes (a time of \(14.4\) minutes rounds to \(14\)), so by \(14.5\) minutes all \(4\) have certainly arrived. At \(10\) or \(14\) we cannot be sure all \(4\) have.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Plot the points \((4.5, 0)\text{,}\) \((14.5, 4)\text{,}\) \((24.5, 13)\text{,}\) \((34.5, 26)\text{,}\) \((44.5, 34)\text{,}\) \((54.5, 38)\) and \((64.5, 40)\) on graph paper.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use \(2\,\text{cm}\) for \(10\) minutes and \(2\,\text{cm}\) for \(10\) learners. Check that the first point is on the axis and the last at \(40\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Join the points. What shape do you get?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The graph never falls; it rises steeply where the frequencies are large (the class 25–34) and levels off at \(40\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Use your graph to estimate how many learners took less than \(30\) minutes.
    🔗
    Solution.
    About \(20\) learners. By calculation along the straight segment: \(13 + 13 \times \dfrac{30 - 24.5}{10} = 20.15\text{.}\)
