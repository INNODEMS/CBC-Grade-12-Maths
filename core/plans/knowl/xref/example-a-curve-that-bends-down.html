Example 14.4.8 A Curve That Bends Down.

For \(y = 4x - x^2\) from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 4\text{,}\) use \(4\) strips to find the trapezoidal and mid-ordinate estimates of the area, and the exact area. Say which estimate is too big.
🔗

Solution.

Step 1: Ordinates. With \(h = 1\text{,}\) the heights at \(x = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\) are \(0, 3, 4, 3, 0\text{,}\) and the mid-ordinates at \(x = 0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5\) are \(1.75, 3.75, 3.75, 1.75\text{.}\)
🔗
Step 2: Estimates.
\begin{align*} T \amp = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1 \times \left[0 + 0 + 2(3 + 4 + 3)\right] = 10\\ M \amp = 1 \times (1.75 + 3.75 + 3.75 + 1.75) = 11 \end{align*}
🔗
Step 3: Exact area.
\begin{equation*} \int_0^4 (4x - x^2)\,dx = \left[2x^2 - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^4 = 32 - \frac{64}{3} = \frac{32}{3} \approx 10.67 \end{equation*}
🔗
Here \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -2\text{,}\) so the curve bends downwards: the trapezoidal estimate \(10\) is too small and the mid-ordinate estimate \(11\) is too big.
