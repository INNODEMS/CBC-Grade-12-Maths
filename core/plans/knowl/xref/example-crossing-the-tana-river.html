Example 11.5.8 Crossing the Tana River.

At Garsen a boat is steered straight across the Tana River at \(2.4\,\text{m/s}\) through the water. The river flows at \(0.7\,\text{m/s}\) and is \(120\,\text{m}\) wide. Find the ground speed, the drift angle, the time to cross and how far downstream the boat lands.
🔗

Solution.

The current is at right angles to the course, so the velocity triangle is right-angled with the ground speed as its hypotenuse.
\begin{align*} \text{ground speed} \amp = \sqrt{2.4^2 + 0.7^2} = \sqrt{6.25} = 2.5\,\text{m/s}\\ \tan d \amp = \frac{0.7}{2.4}, \quad d = 16.3^\circ \end{align*}
The speed across the river is still \(2.4\,\text{m/s}\text{,}\) so the crossing takes \(120 \div 2.4 = 50\,\text{s}\text{.}\) In that time the current carries the boat \(50 \times 0.7 = 35\,\text{m}\) downstream.
