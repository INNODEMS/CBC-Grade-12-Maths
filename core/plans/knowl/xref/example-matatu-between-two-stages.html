Example 14.6.29 A Matatu Between Two Stages.

A matatu leaves a stage from rest and speeds up steadily to \(12\,\text{m/s}\) in \(10\) seconds. It travels at \(12\,\text{m/s}\) for \(30\) seconds, then slows steadily to rest at the next stage in \(8\) seconds. How far apart are the stages?
🔗

Solution.

The velocity is never negative, so the distance is the whole area under the graph in Figure 14.6.30:
\begin{equation*} \tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 12 + 30 \times 12 + \tfrac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 12 = 60 + 360 + 48 = 468\,\text{m} \end{equation*}
Integrating each straight-line part of the graph gives the same three areas.
