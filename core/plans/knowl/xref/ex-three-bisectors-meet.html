Exercise 6. Challenge: Three Bisectors Meet.

Explain why the perpendicular bisectors of the three sides of a triangle \(ABC\) meet at one point.
🔗
Hint.
Start with the point where two of the bisectors meet.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
The point \(O\) on two bisectors has \(OA = OB = OC\text{,}\) so it is on the third.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Let the perpendicular bisectors of \(AB\) and \(BC\) meet at \(O\text{.}\) Since \(O\) is on the first, \(OA = OB\text{;}\) since it is on the second, \(OB = OC\text{.}\) So \(OA = OC\text{,}\) and \(O\) is on the locus of points equidistant from \(A\) and \(C\text{:}\) the perpendicular bisector of \(AC\text{.}\) All three bisectors pass through \(O\text{.}\)
