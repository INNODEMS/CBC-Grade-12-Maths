Exercise 6. Challenge: Only Some Heights.

The heights of a curve are known only at \(x = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, 8\text{:}\) \(5, 7, 8, 8, 7, 6, 5, 3, 2\text{.}\) What are the narrowest strips that let you use the mid-ordinate rule? Estimate the area under the curve from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 8\) with the mid-ordinate rule, and with the trapezoidal rule using all the heights.
🔗
Hint.
Each mid-ordinate must be one of the known heights.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Strips \(2\) wide; \(M = 48\text{;}\) \(T = 47.5\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
With strips \(1\) wide the middles \(0.5, 1.5, \ldots\) have no known heights. Strips \(2\) wide have middles \(1, 3, 5, 7\text{:}\) \(M = 2 \times (7 + 8 + 6 + 3) = 48\text{.}\) With \(8\) strips of width \(1\text{:}\) \(T = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1 \times [5 + 2 + 2(7 + 8 + 8 + 7 + 6 + 5 + 3)] = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 95 = 47.5\text{.}\)
