Exercise 7. Challenge: When \(x\) Is Not Small.

Use the first four terms of \((1 + x)^6\) to estimate \((1.2)^6\text{,}\) and compare with a calculator. Why is this estimate poorer than the one for \((1.02)^6\text{?}\)
🔗
Hint.
Work out the fifth term.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(2.96\text{;}\) the calculator gives \(2.985984\text{.}\) With \(x = 0.2\) the fifth term is \(0.024\text{,}\) far from negligible.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\(1 + 6(0.2) + 15(0.04) + 20(0.008) = 1 + 1.2 + 0.6 + 0.16 = 2.96\text{.}\) The fifth term is \(15(0.2)^4 = 0.024\text{,}\) so the later terms still matter: \(x = 0.2\) is not small. For \((1.02)^6\) the fifth term is only \(0.0000024\text{.}\)
