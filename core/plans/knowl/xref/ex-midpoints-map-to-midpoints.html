Exercise 6. Challenge: Midpoints.

The points \(A(-1, 3)\) and \(B(5, 1)\) are mapped by \(W = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \amp 1 \\ -1 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix}\) onto \(A'\) and \(B'\text{.}\) Show that the image of the midpoint of \(AB\) is the midpoint of \(A'B'\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Work out both points and compare them.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Both are \((10, 2)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The midpoint of \(AB\) is \(\left(\frac{-1 + 5}{2}, \frac{3 + 1}{2}\right) = (2, 2)\text{,}\) and \(W \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
🔗
\(A' = W \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(B' = W \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 21 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) The midpoint of \(A'B'\) is \(\left(\frac{-1 + 21}{2}, \frac{7 - 3}{2}\right) = (10, 2)\text{,}\) the same point.
