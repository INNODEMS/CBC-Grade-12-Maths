Example 10.6.10 Three Points in Space.

\(A(1, -2, 3)\text{,}\) \(B(3, 1, 2)\) and \(C(7, 7, 0)\) are three points.
🔗
  1. Show that \(A\text{,}\) \(B\) and \(C\) are collinear.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Is \(D(5, 4, 2)\) on the same line?
    🔗
    🔗
🔗

Solution.

(a) \(\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\overrightarrow{BC} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 6 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix} = 2\overrightarrow{AB}\text{.}\) The vectors are parallel and share \(B\text{,}\) so the points are collinear.
🔗
(b) \(\overrightarrow{BD} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) The first two components equal those of \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) (\(k = 1\)), but the third would need \(0 = 1 \times (-1)\text{,}\) which is false. \(\overrightarrow{BD}\) is not a multiple of \(\overrightarrow{AB}\text{,}\) so \(D\) is not on the line.
