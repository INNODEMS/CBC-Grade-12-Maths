Vectors Around Us.

At the start of this chapter we asked: how do we use vectors to describe positions and movements in space? You can now answer with three numbers for every position and every movement, and the rules for adding, scaling, measuring and dividing them.
🔗
  • A drone spraying a shamba, or an aircraft approaching an airport, has a position and a velocity in three dimensions. Its flight computer adds displacement vectors many times a second.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Structural engineers treat the forces in a roof truss or a mast cable as vectors; a roof stands when the force vectors at each joint add to the zero vector.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Computer games and 3D animation move every point of a character by adding a vector, and make it larger or smaller with a scalar multiple.
    🔗
    🔗
  • A GPS receiver works out its position in three dimensions from the distances to several satellites, using the magnitude formula of Section 10.4.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Navigation by bearings and distances, in Chapter 11, uses the same ideas in the plane.
    🔗
    🔗
