Example 7.3.9 Constructing Lines at a Fixed Distance.

Draw a straight line \(l\text{.}\) Construct the locus of points \(2.5\,\text{cm}\) from \(l\text{.}\)
🔗

Solution.

Step 1: Mark two points \(P\) and \(Q\) on \(l\text{,}\) well apart.
🔗
Step 2: Construct the perpendicular to \(l\) at \(P\text{.}\) With the compass point at \(P\text{,}\) draw two arcs that cut \(l\) on each side of \(P\text{.}\) Open the compasses wider and, from each of those two points, draw arcs above \(l\) that cross. Join \(P\) to the crossing point, and extend the line below \(l\text{.}\) Do the same at \(Q\text{.}\)
🔗
Step 3: Set the compasses to \(2.5\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) With the point at \(P\text{,}\) mark the perpendicular above and below \(l\text{;}\) do the same at \(Q\text{.}\)
🔗
Step 4: Join the two marks above \(l\text{,}\) and the two marks below. These two lines, parallel to \(l\text{,}\) are the locus (Figure 7.3.6). Keep all the construction arcs.
🔗
