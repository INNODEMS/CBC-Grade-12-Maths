Exercise 6. Challenge: Two Space Diagonals of a Cube.

Show that the angle between two space diagonals of a cube, \(AG\) and \(CE\text{,}\) is about \(70.5^{\circ}\text{,}\) whatever the size of the cube.
🔗
Hint.
The diagonals cross at the centre \(X\) of the cube. Use the rectangle \(ACGE\) and the triangle \(AXC\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Angle \(AXC \approx 109.5^{\circ}\text{,}\) so the angle between the lines is \(180^{\circ} - 109.5^{\circ} = 70.5^{\circ}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Let the edge be \(a\text{.}\) Then \(AC = a\sqrt{2}\) and \(AG = CE = a\sqrt{3}\text{.}\) The diagonals bisect each other at \(X\text{,}\) so \(XA = XC = \frac{a\sqrt{3}}{2}\text{.}\) In triangle \(AXC\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} \cos AXC = \frac{\frac{3a^2}{4} + \frac{3a^2}{4} - 2a^2}{2 \times \frac{3a^2}{4}} = \frac{-\frac{a^2}{2}}{\frac{3a^2}{2}} = -\frac{1}{3} \end{equation*}
The \(a^2\) cancels, so the angle does not depend on the size of the cube. Angle \(AXC \approx 109.5^{\circ}\) is obtuse, so the angle between the two lines is \(180^{\circ} - 109.5^{\circ} = 70.5^{\circ}\text{.}\)
