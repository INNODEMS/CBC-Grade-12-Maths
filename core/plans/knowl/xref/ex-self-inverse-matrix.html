Exercise 6. Challenge: A Matrix That Undoes Itself.

Show that \(\begin{bmatrix} 7 \amp -12 \\ 4 \amp -7 \end{bmatrix}\) is its own inverse. What happens to a point that is transformed by this matrix twice?
🔗
Hint.
Work out the square of the matrix.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Its square is \(I\text{;}\) the point returns to where it started.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\(\begin{bmatrix} 7 \amp -12 \\ 4 \amp -7 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 7 \amp -12 \\ 4 \amp -7 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 49 - 48 \amp -84 + 84 \\ 28 - 28 \amp -48 + 49 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) So the matrix times itself is \(I\text{,}\) which means it is its own inverse: the second transformation undoes the first and the point returns to its start.
