Exercise 7. Challenge: A Term Without \(x\).

Expand \(\left(x + \dfrac{1}{x}\right)^4\) and write down the term that does not contain \(x\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Put \(a = x\) and \(b = \dfrac{1}{x}\text{,}\) and use \(x \times \dfrac{1}{x} = 1\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(x^4 + 4x^2 + 6 + \dfrac{4}{x^2} + \dfrac{1}{x^4}\text{;}\) the term is \(6\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\(x^4 + 4x^3\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right) + 6x^2\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)^2 + 4x\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)^3 + \left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)^4 = x^4 + 4x^2 + 6 + \dfrac{4}{x^2} + \dfrac{1}{x^4}\text{.}\) The middle term, \(6\text{,}\) does not contain \(x\text{.}\)
