Three-Dimensional Geometry Around Us.

At the start of this chapter we asked: where do we use the lengths and angles of solid shapes in everyday life? You can now find any length or angle in a solid by finding the right triangle and flattening it.
🔗
  • A fundi building a roof needs the length of each rafter and the slope of each face. County building plans state the roof pitch, which is the angle between a roof face and the horizontal.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Solar panels near the equator are tilted only a little, about \(10^{\circ}\) to \(15^{\circ}\text{,}\) so that they face the sun well and rain still washes off the dust.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Ramps for wheelchair users must not be too steep: the guideline is about the steepest line on the ramp, as in Example 8.5.12.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Tents and greenhouses are prisms; the tent maker needs the slope of the sides and the length of each guy rope.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Surveyors and drone pilots measure angles of elevation to a line in space, and 3D design and printing programs work out the lengths and angles of every edge of a model.
