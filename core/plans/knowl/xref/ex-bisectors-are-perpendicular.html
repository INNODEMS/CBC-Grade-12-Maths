Exercise 6. Challenge: Why the Bisectors Meet at Right Angles.

Two straight lines cross, making angles of \(x^{\circ}\) and \((180 - x)^{\circ}\text{.}\) Show that the bisectors of these two angles are perpendicular.
🔗
Hint.
The two angles are next to each other on a straight line. Add their halves.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
The angle between the bisectors is \(\frac{x}{2} + \frac{180 - x}{2} = 90\) degrees.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The bisectors lie inside two adjacent angles, so the angle between them is half of one plus half of the other: \(\frac{x}{2} + \frac{180 - x}{2} = \frac{180}{2} = 90\text{.}\) So the bisectors are perpendicular, whatever \(x\) is.
