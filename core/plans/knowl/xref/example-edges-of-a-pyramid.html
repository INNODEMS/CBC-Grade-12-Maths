Example 8.3.11 Edges of a Pyramid.

A model of a right pyramid \(VABCD\) has a square base of side \(10\,\text{cm}\) and height \(VO = 12\,\text{cm}\) (Figure 8.3.7). \(M\) is the midpoint of \(BC\text{.}\) Find the length of (a) \(OA\text{,}\) the projection of \(VA\) on the base; (b) the sloping edge \(VA\text{;}\) (c) the slant height \(VM\text{.}\)
🔗

Solution.

(a) \(AC^2 = 10^2 + 10^2 = 200\text{,}\) and \(O\) is the midpoint of \(AC\text{,}\) so \(OA = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{200} = \sqrt{50} \approx 7.07\,\text{cm}\text{.}\)
🔗
(b) Triangle \(VOA\) has a right angle at \(O\text{:}\) \(VA^2 = 50 + 12^2 = 194\text{,}\) so \(VA = \sqrt{194} \approx 13.93\,\text{cm}\text{.}\)
🔗
(c) \(OM\) is half of \(AB\text{,}\) so \(OM = 5\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Triangle \(VOM\) has a right angle at \(O\text{:}\) \(VM = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13\,\text{cm}\text{.}\)
🔗
The sloping edge \(VA\) and the slant height \(VM\) are different lengths: \(VA\) runs to a corner, \(VM\) to the middle of a side.
