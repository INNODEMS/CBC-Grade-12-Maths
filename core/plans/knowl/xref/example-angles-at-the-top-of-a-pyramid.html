Example 8.4.11 Angles at the Top of a Pyramid.

A model of a roof is a right pyramid \(VABCD\) on a square base of side \(10\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) with height \(VO = 12\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Find (a) angle \(AVC\text{,}\) between opposite sloping edges; (b) angle \(AVB\text{,}\) between neighbouring sloping edges.
🔗

Solution.

(a) Triangle \(AVC\) is isosceles, and \(VO\) cuts it into two right-angled triangles. From Example 8.3.11, \(OA = \sqrt{50} \approx 7.07\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) In triangle \(VOA\text{,}\) with the right angle at \(O\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} \tan AVO = \frac{OA}{VO} = \frac{\sqrt{50}}{12} \end{equation*}
so angle \(AVO \approx 30.5^{\circ}\) and angle \(AVC = 2 \times AVO \approx 61.0^{\circ}\) (double before rounding).
🔗
(b) Triangle \(AVB\) has \(VA = VB = \sqrt{50 + 144} = \sqrt{194}\) and \(AB = 10\text{.}\) \(VO\) is not in this face, and triangle \(AVB\) has no right angle, so use the cosine rule (or split the isosceles triangle at the midpoint of \(AB\text{,}\) as in Section 8.2):
\begin{equation*} \cos AVB = \frac{194 + 194 - 100}{2 \times 194} = \frac{288}{388} \end{equation*}
so angle \(AVB \approx 42.1^{\circ}\text{.}\)
