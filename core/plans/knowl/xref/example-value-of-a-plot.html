Example 6.3.31 The Value of a Plot.

The width of a plot along a road \(60\,\text{m}\) long was measured every \(10\,\text{m}\text{:}\) \(30, 34, 37, 38, 36, 33, 28\) metres. Land here is valued at Ksh \(1500\) per square metre. Estimate the area with the trapezoidal rule (strips \(10\,\text{m}\) wide) and with the mid-ordinate rule (strips \(20\,\text{m}\) wide). By how much do the two values of the plot differ?
🔗

Solution.

Trapezoidal rule, \(h = 10\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} T = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times [30 + 28 + 2(34 + 37 + 38 + 36 + 33)] = 5 \times 414 = 2070 \end{equation*}
Mid-ordinate rule, \(h = 20\text{:}\) the strips are \(0\) to \(20\text{,}\) \(20\) to \(40\) and \(40\) to \(60\text{,}\) with middles at \(10, 30, 50\,\text{m}\text{,}\) where the widths are \(34, 38, 33\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} M = 20 \times (34 + 38 + 33) = 20 \times 105 = 2100 \end{equation*}
Values: \(2070 \times 1500 = 3\,105\,000\) and \(2100 \times 1500 = 3\,150\,000\text{,}\) that is Ksh \(3\,105\,000\) and Ksh \(3\,150\,000\text{.}\) They differ by Ksh \(45\,000\text{,}\) enough to matter to a buyer.
