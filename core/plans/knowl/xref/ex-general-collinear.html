Exercise 6. Challenge: Collinear for Any a and b.

The points \(A\text{,}\) \(B\) and \(C\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{a}\text{,}\) \(\mathbf{b}\) and \(3\mathbf{b} - 2\mathbf{a}\text{.}\) Show that \(A\text{,}\) \(B\) and \(C\) are collinear for any \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Find \(\overrightarrow{BC}\) in terms of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(\overrightarrow{BC} = 2\overrightarrow{AB}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\(\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}\) and \(\overrightarrow{BC} = (3\mathbf{b} - 2\mathbf{a}) - \mathbf{b} = 2\mathbf{b} - 2\mathbf{a} = 2(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}) = 2\overrightarrow{AB}\text{.}\) The vectors are parallel and share \(B\text{,}\) so the points are collinear, whatever \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) are.
