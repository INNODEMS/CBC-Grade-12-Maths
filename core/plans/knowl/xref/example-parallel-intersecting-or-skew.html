Example 8.1.11 Parallel, Intersecting or Skew.

In the cuboid \(ABCDEFGH\) of Figure 8.1.7, decide whether each pair of lines is parallel, intersecting or skew. (a) \(AB\) and \(HG\) (b) \(AG\) and \(CE\) (c) \(BC\) and \(AE\) (d) \(AF\) and \(DG\) (e) \(AF\) and \(CH\)
🔗

Solution.

(a) \(HG\) is the top edge directly above and behind \(AB\text{,}\) pointing the same way: parallel.
🔗
(b) \(A\text{,}\) \(C\text{,}\) \(G\) and \(E\) are the corners of the rectangle \(ACGE\text{,}\) which stands upright inside the cuboid. \(AG\) and \(CE\) are its diagonals, so they cross at its centre, which is the centre of the cuboid: intersecting.
🔗
(c) \(AE\) is the vertical edge through \(A\text{.}\) \(BC\) lies in the base and does not pass through \(A\text{,}\) so the lines never meet; one is vertical and the other horizontal, so they are not parallel: skew.
🔗
(d) \(AF\) and \(DG\) are diagonals of the opposite faces \(ABFE\) and \(DCGH\text{,}\) both rising from left to right in the same way: parallel.
🔗
(e) \(AF\) and \(CH\) lie in the parallel faces \(ABFE\) and \(DCGH\text{,}\) so they never meet, but they slope in opposite ways, so they are not parallel: skew.
🔗
