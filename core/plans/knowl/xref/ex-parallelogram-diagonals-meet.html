Exercise 6. Challenge: Where the Diagonals Meet.

\(ABCD\) is a parallelogram with \(A(2, -1, 3)\text{,}\) \(B(5, 1, 4)\) and \(D(0, 2, 6)\text{.}\) Find \(C\text{.}\) Then show that the diagonals \(AC\) and \(BD\) have the same midpoint.
🔗
Hint.
\(\overrightarrow{BC} = \overrightarrow{AD}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(C(3, 4, 7)\text{;}\) both midpoints are \((2.5, 1.5, 5)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\(\overrightarrow{AD} = \mathbf{d} - \mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 3 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) so \(\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{b} + \overrightarrow{AD} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \\ 7 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) The midpoint of \(AC\) is \(\left(\frac{2 + 3}{2}, \frac{-1 + 4}{2}, \frac{3 + 7}{2}\right) = (2.5, 1.5, 5)\text{,}\) and the midpoint of \(BD\) is \(\left(\frac{5 + 0}{2}, \frac{1 + 2}{2}, \frac{4 + 6}{2}\right) = (2.5, 1.5, 5)\text{:}\) the same point.
