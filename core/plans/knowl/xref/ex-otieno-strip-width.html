Exercise 5.

Otieno measured the depth of a river \(12\,\text{m}\) wide every \(2\,\text{m}\text{:}\) \(0, 0.8, 1.4, 1.7, 1.5, 0.9, 0\) metres. He used the trapezoidal rule with \(h = 12 \div 7\text{.}\) What did he get, and what is wrong? Find the correct estimate.
🔗
Answer.
He got \(10.8\,\text{m}^2\text{;}\) he divided by the number of depths, not the number of strips; \(12.6\,\text{m}^2\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The inner depths add to \(6.3\text{.}\) Otieno: \(\tfrac{1}{2} \times \tfrac{12}{7} \times 2 \times 6.3 = 10.8\text{.}\) But \(7\) depths make \(6\) strips, so \(h = 12 \div 6 = 2\text{,}\) the gap between measurements: \(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 2 \times 6.3 = 12.6\,\text{m}^2\text{.}\)
