Example 7.1.12 Constructing the Perpendicular Bisector.

Draw a line segment \(AB = 7\,\text{cm}\) and construct its perpendicular bisector, with the compasses opened to \(5\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) How far from the midpoint \(M\) are the points where the arcs cross? Would a radius of \(3\,\text{cm}\) work?
🔗

Solution.

Step 1: Draw \(AB = 7\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Open the compasses to \(5\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) which is more than half of \(AB\) (\(3.5\,\text{cm}\)).
🔗
Step 2: With the point at \(A\text{,}\) draw an arc above \(AB\) and an arc below. Without changing the compasses, do the same from \(B\text{.}\) Join the two crossing points (Figure 7.1.9).
🔗
Check: each crossing point is \(5\,\text{cm}\) from \(A\) and \(3.5\,\text{cm}\) along from \(A\) to \(M\text{,}\) so by Pythagoras its distance from \(M\) is
\begin{equation*} \sqrt{5^2 - 3.5^2} = \sqrt{12.75} \approx 3.6\,\text{cm}. \end{equation*}
Measure it on your drawing. Any radius more than \(3.5\,\text{cm}\) works. A radius of \(3\,\text{cm}\) does not: the arcs from \(A\) and \(B\) would not meet.
