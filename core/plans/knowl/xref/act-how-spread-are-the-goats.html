Learner Experience 12.4.24 How Spread Are the Goats?

Use the masses of the \(50\) goats at Garissa market from Example 12.2.9: frequencies \(3, 7, 12, 14, 9, 5\) for the classes 15–19 to 40–44 kg.
🔗
  1. Write down the midpoint of each class.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(17, 22, 27, 32, 37, 42\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Multiply each midpoint by its frequency and add. Estimate the mean mass.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(fx\text{:}\) \(51, 154, 324, 448, 333, 210\text{;}\) \(\Sigma fx = 1520\text{;}\) \(\bar{x} = 1520 \div 50 = 30.4\,\text{kg}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Is this the true mean mass of the \(50\) goats? Explain.
    🔗
    Solution.
    No, it is an estimate: we do not know the actual masses, so each goat is treated as if it weighed the midpoint of its class.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Work out \(\Sigma fx^2\) and use \(\sigma^2 = \dfrac{\Sigma fx^2}{\Sigma f} - \bar{x}^2\) to estimate the standard deviation.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(fx^2\text{:}\) \(867, 3388, 8748, 14\,336, 12\,321, 8820\text{;}\) \(\Sigma fx^2 = 48\,480\text{;}\) \(\sigma^2 = 969.6 - 924.16 = 45.44\text{;}\) \(\sigma \approx 6.74\,\text{kg}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
