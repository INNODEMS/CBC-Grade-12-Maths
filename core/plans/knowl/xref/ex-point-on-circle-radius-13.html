Exercise 6. Challenge: A Point on a Circle.

The point \(P(-5, 12)\) lies on a circle of radius 13 with centre \(O\text{.}\) Show that \(\left(-\dfrac{5}{13}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{12}{13}\right)^2 = 1\text{,}\) and find the angle \(x\) measured from the positive \(x\)-axis to \(OP\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
\(\cos x = -\dfrac{5}{13}\) and \(\sin x = \dfrac{12}{13}\text{.}\) Which quadrant is \(P\) in?
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(\dfrac{25}{169} + \dfrac{144}{169} = 1\text{;}\) \(x = 112.6^\circ\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\(P\) is in the second quadrant. \(\cos^{-1}\left(-\dfrac{5}{13}\right) = 112.6^\circ\text{,}\) and \(\sin 112.6^\circ = 0.923 = \dfrac{12}{13}\text{,}\) as it should be.
