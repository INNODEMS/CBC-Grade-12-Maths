Example 7.4.9 Bisecting an Angle of \(64^{\circ}\).

Draw an angle \(XOY\) of \(64^{\circ}\) and construct its bisector.
🔗

Solution.

Step 1: With the compass point at \(O\text{,}\) draw an arc that cuts \(OX\) at \(A\) and \(OY\) at \(B\text{.}\)
🔗
Step 2: With the compasses open to a suitable radius, the same for both, draw an arc from \(A\) and an arc from \(B\) inside the angle so that they cross at \(C\text{.}\)
🔗
Step 3: Join \(O\) to \(C\) and extend the line. This is the bisector (Figure 7.4.6).
🔗
Check with a protractor: each half should measure \(64^{\circ} \div 2 = 32^{\circ}\text{.}\) It works because \(OA = OB\) and \(AC = BC\text{,}\) so triangles \(OAC\) and \(OBC\) are congruent and the angles at \(O\) are equal.
