Exercise 7. Challenge: Powers of 11 and 101.

Use \((10 + 1)^5\) to find \(11^5\text{,}\) and explain why its digits are not 1 5 10 10 5 1. Then find \(101^4\) using \((100 + 1)^4\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Think about carrying.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(11^5 = 161\,051\text{;}\) \(101^4 = 104\,060\,401\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\((10 + 1)^5 = 100\,000 + 5(10\,000) + 10(1000) + 10(100) + 5(10) + 1 = 161\,051\text{.}\) The coefficients 10 are two-digit numbers, so they carry into the next place. With \((100 + 1)^4\) each coefficient has two places to itself: \(100\,000\,000 + 4\,000\,000 + 60\,000 + 400 + 1 = 104\,060\,401\text{,}\) the row 1, 4, 6, 4, 1 with zeros between.
