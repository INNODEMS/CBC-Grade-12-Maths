Learner Experience 14.2.2 Term by Term.

  1. Differentiate \(x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x + 7\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(3x^2 + 4x - 5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Hence write down \(\displaystyle\int (3x^2 + 4x - 5)\,dx\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x + c\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Now integrate each term of \(3x^2 + 4x - 5\) separately and add the results. Do you get the same answer?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(x^3\text{,}\) \(2x^2\) and \(-5x\text{:}\) yes, the same, with one \(+\,c\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Is \(\displaystyle\int 4x^2\,dx\) equal to \(4\displaystyle\int x^2\,dx\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Yes: both are \(\frac{4x^3}{3} + c\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Wanjiru says \(\displaystyle\int (x + 1)(x + 2)\,dx = \left(\frac{x^2}{2} + x\right)\left(\frac{x^2}{2} + 2x\right) + c\text{.}\) Expand her answer and differentiate it. Is she right?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Her answer is \(\frac{x^4}{4} + \frac{3x^3}{2} + 2x^2 + c\text{,}\) whose derivative is \(x^3 + \frac{9x^2}{2} + 4x\text{,}\) not \((x + 1)(x + 2) = x^2 + 3x + 2\text{.}\) She is wrong. Expanding first gives \(\frac{x^3}{3} + \frac{3x^2}{2} + 2x + c\text{.}\)
