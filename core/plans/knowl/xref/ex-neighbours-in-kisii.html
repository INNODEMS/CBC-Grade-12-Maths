Exercise 6. Challenge: Neighbours in Kisii.

Two neighbours in Kisii disagree about the area of a plot. One counted squares on a map of scale \(1 : 1000\) with \(1\,\text{cm}\) squares: \(28\) full and \(18\) part squares. The other measured the width of the plot every \(10\,\text{m}\) along the \(60\,\text{m}\) boundary road: \(55, 60, 64, 66, 63, 58, 52\) metres. Find both estimates and suggest how they can agree.
🔗
Hint.
Which estimate used measurements made on the ground?
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(3700\,\text{m}^2\) and \(3645\,\text{m}^2\text{,}\) about \(1.5\%\) apart
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Map: \(1\,\text{cm}\) stands for \(10\,\text{m}\text{,}\) so one square is \(100\,\text{m}^2\text{;}\) \(28 + 9 = 37\) squares give \(3700\,\text{m}^2\text{.}\) Offsets: \(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times [55 + 52 + 2(60 + 64 + 66 + 63 + 58)] = 5 \times 729 = 3645\,\text{m}^2\text{.}\) The difference, \(55\,\text{m}^2\text{,}\) is about \(1.5\%\text{.}\) The offsets were measured on the ground, so they are more trustworthy than a count on a small map; the neighbours could also measure more often, together, and agree to use the result.
