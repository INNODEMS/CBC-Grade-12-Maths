Exercise 6. Challenge: Why a Constant?

The functions \(F\) and \(G\) both have derivative \(f\text{.}\) Explain why \(F(x) - G(x)\) is a constant.
🔗
Hint.
Differentiate \(F - G\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Answer.
The derivative of \(F - G\) is \(f - f = 0\text{,}\) so its graph is horizontal everywhere: \(F - G\) is a constant.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\bigl(F(x) - G(x)\bigr) = F'(x) - G'(x) = f(x) - f(x) = 0\text{.}\) A function whose gradient is \(0\) at every point has a horizontal graph, so it is a constant. So any two integrals of \(f\) differ by a constant, which is why one \(+\,c\) covers them all.
