Exercise 6. Challenge: The Determinant of a Product.

\(P = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(Q = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp 5 \\ 1 \amp 4 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Show that \(\det(PQ) = \det P \times \det Q\text{,}\) and explain this result using area scale factors.
🔗
Hint.
\(PQ\) is the single matrix for two transformations. Each one has its own area scale factor.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(PQ = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \amp 19 \\ 3 \amp 9 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) \(\det(PQ) = 6 = 2 \times 3\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\(PQ = \begin{bmatrix} 6 + 1 \amp 15 + 4 \\ 2 + 1 \amp 5 + 4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \amp 19 \\ 3 \amp 9 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) so \(\det(PQ) = 63 - 57 = 6\text{.}\) Also \(\det P = 3 - 1 = 2\) and \(\det Q = 8 - 5 = 3\text{,}\) and \(2 \times 3 = 6\text{.}\) \(PQ\) means \(Q\) followed by \(P\text{:}\) \(Q\) multiplies every area by \(3\text{,}\) and then \(P\) multiplies every area by \(2\text{,}\) so together every area is multiplied by \(6\text{.}\)
