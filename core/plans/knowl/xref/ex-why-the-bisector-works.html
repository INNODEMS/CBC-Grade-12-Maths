Exercise 6. Challenge: Why the Bisector Works.

\(P\) is equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\text{,}\) and \(M\) is the midpoint of \(AB\text{.}\) Show that \(PM\) is at right angles to \(AB\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Compare triangles \(PMA\) and \(PMB\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Answer.
The triangles are congruent (SSS), so the angles at \(M\) are equal, and each is \(90^{\circ}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
In triangles \(PMA\) and \(PMB\text{:}\) \(PA = PB\) (given), \(AM = MB\) (\(M\) is the midpoint) and \(PM\) is common. So the triangles are congruent (SSS), and angle \(PMA\) equals angle \(PMB\text{.}\) These two angles lie on the straight line \(AB\text{,}\) so they add to \(180^{\circ}\text{,}\) and each is \(90^{\circ}\text{.}\)
