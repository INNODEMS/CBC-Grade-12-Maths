Learner Experience 4.3.2 Which Plan Earns Most?

The Grade 12 class in Machakos is planning its fundraiser again (Learner Experience 4.1.3). They make \(x\) mandazi and \(y\) samosas, with
\begin{equation*} x + y \le 100, \quad 15x + 35y \le 2520, \quad y \ge 30, \quad x \ge 0\text{.} \end{equation*}
The profit is Ksh \(15\) on a mandazi and Ksh \(25\) on a samosa, so the profit in shillings is \(P = 15x + 25y\text{.}\) The corners of the region R are \((0, 30)\text{,}\) \((70, 30)\text{,}\) \((49, 51)\) and \((0, 72)\) (Learner Experience 4.2.27).
🔗
  1. Check that the plans \((20, 40)\text{,}\) \((50, 40)\) and \((30, 50)\) are inside R, and work out the profit for each.
    🔗
    Solution.
    All three satisfy every constraint. \(P = 1300\text{,}\) \(1750\) and \(1700\) shillings.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Work out the profit at each corner of R.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \((0, 30)\text{:}\) Ksh \(750\text{;}\) \((70, 30)\text{:}\) Ksh \(1800\text{;}\) \((49, 51)\text{:}\) Ksh \(2010\text{;}\) \((0, 72)\text{:}\) Ksh \(1800\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Which plan earns most? How much is the profit?
    🔗
    Solution.
    49 mandazi and 51 samosas, a profit of Ksh \(2010\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Start at the plan \((50, 40)\text{.}\) What happens to the profit if the class makes one more samosa? Can they keep doing this? Use your answer to explain why the best plan is on the edge of R, and then at a corner.
    🔗
    Solution.
    One more samosa adds Ksh \(25\text{.}\) From any point inside R you can keep adding samosas (or mandazi) and the profit keeps rising until you reach a boundary. Along that boundary you can still move in a direction that raises the profit until you reach a corner, where two limits stop you at once.
