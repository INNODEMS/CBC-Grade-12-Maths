Exercise 6. Challenge: Midpoints of a Quadrilateral.

Prove that the midpoints of the four sides of any quadrilateral \(ABCD\) are the corners of a parallelogram.
🔗
Hint.
Compare each side of the inner shape with a diagonal of \(ABCD\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Answer.
Each side of the inner shape is half of a diagonal: two sides are \(\frac{1}{2}\overrightarrow{AC}\) and two are \(\frac{1}{2}\overrightarrow{BD}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Let the midpoints of \(AB\text{,}\) \(BC\text{,}\) \(CD\text{,}\) \(DA\) be \(E\text{,}\) \(F\text{,}\) \(G\text{,}\) \(H\text{,}\) with position vectors \(\mathbf{e} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})\) and so on. Then \(\overrightarrow{EF} = \mathbf{f} - \mathbf{e} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}) = \frac{1}{2}\overrightarrow{AC}\) and \(\overrightarrow{HG} = \mathbf{g} - \mathbf{h} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a})\text{.}\) So \(\overrightarrow{EF} = \overrightarrow{HG}\text{:}\) one pair of opposite sides is equal and parallel, and \(EFGH\) is a parallelogram. (In the same way \(\overrightarrow{FG} = \overrightarrow{EH} = \frac{1}{2}\overrightarrow{BD}\text{.}\))
