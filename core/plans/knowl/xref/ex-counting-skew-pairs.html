Exercise 6. Challenge: Counting Skew Pairs.

How many pairs of edges of a cube are skew? How many pairs of edges of a square-based pyramid are skew?
🔗
Hint.
Count the edges skew to one edge, then share out, remembering that each pair is counted twice.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(24\text{;}\) \(8\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Each of the \(12\) edges of a cube is skew to \(4\) others (as \(AB\) is skew to \(CG\text{,}\) \(DH\text{,}\) \(FG\) and \(HE\)). That counts each pair twice, so there are \(12 \times 4 \div 2 = 24\) skew pairs.
🔗
In the pyramid \(VABCD\text{,}\) a base edge meets every other base edge or is parallel to it, and two sloping edges always meet at \(V\text{.}\) Each sloping edge is skew to the two base edges that do not touch its foot (for \(VA\text{:}\) \(BC\) and \(CD\)). So there are \(4 \times 2 = 8\) skew pairs.
