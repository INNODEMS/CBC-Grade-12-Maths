Example 4.2.34 Goats and Sheep.

A farmer in Kajiado has Ksh \(35\,000\) to buy goats at Ksh \(6000\) each and sheep at Ksh \(5000\) each. Her pen has \(26\) square metres of floor; a goat needs \(2\) square metres and a sheep \(7\text{.}\) She wants at least one of each. List every plan she could choose.
🔗

Solution.

Let \(x\) be the number of goats and \(y\) the number of sheep she buys. Money: \(6000x + 5000y \le 35\,000\text{,}\) that is \(6x + 5y \le 35\text{.}\) Space: \(2x + 7y \le 26\text{.}\) Also \(x \ge 1\) and \(y \ge 1\text{,}\) whole numbers.
🔗
Take each value of \(y\) in turn.
  • \(y = 1\text{:}\) \(6x \le 30\) and \(2x \le 19\text{,}\) so \(x \le 5\text{:}\) \(x = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • \(y = 2\text{:}\) \(6x \le 25\) and \(2x \le 12\text{,}\) so \(x \le 4\text{:}\) \(x = 1, 2, 3, 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • \(y = 3\text{:}\) \(6x \le 20\) and \(2x \le 5\text{,}\) so \(x \le 2\text{:}\) \(x = 1, 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • \(y = 4\text{:}\) \(2x \le -2\text{,}\) impossible.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
There are \(11\) plans: \((1, 1)\text{,}\) \((2, 1)\text{,}\) \((3, 1)\text{,}\) \((4, 1)\text{,}\) \((5, 1)\text{,}\) \((1, 2)\text{,}\) \((2, 2)\text{,}\) \((3, 2)\text{,}\) \((4, 2)\text{,}\) \((1, 3)\) and \((2, 3)\text{.}\)
