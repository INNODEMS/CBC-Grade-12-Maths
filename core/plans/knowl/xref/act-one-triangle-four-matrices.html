Learner Experience 5.1.3 One Triangle, Four Matrices.

Triangle \(ABC\) has vertices \(A(1, 1)\text{,}\) \(B(3, 1)\) and \(C(1, 2)\text{.}\) Here are four matrices:
\begin{equation*} M_1 = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad M_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad M_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad M_4 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
  1. Write the coordinates of \(A\text{,}\) \(B\) and \(C\) as the columns of a \(2 \times 3\) matrix. Multiply it by each of \(M_1\text{,}\) \(M_2\text{,}\) \(M_3\) and \(M_4\) (the \(2 \times 2\) matrix on the left). Each column of the answer is the image of one vertex.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(M_1 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 3 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \amp -3 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) \(M_2 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 3 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \amp -1 \amp -2 \\ 1 \amp 3 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) \(M_3 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 3 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp 6 \amp 2 \\ 2 \amp 2 \amp 4 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) \(M_4 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 3 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \amp 2 \\ 1 \amp 3 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)

    Solution.

  4. Where does the origin \(O(0, 0)\) go under each matrix?
    🔗
    Solution.
    It stays at \(O\) every time: any matrix times \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) is \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Work out \(M_2 \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(M_2 \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Compare your answers with the columns of \(M_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{:}\) these are the first and second columns of \(M_2\text{.}\)
