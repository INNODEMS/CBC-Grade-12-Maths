Example 8.4.8 An Angle in a Right-Angled Triangle.

The cuboid \(ABCDEFGH\) has \(AB = 12\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) \(BC = 5\,\text{cm}\) and \(AE = 7\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Find the angle between the space diagonal \(AG\) and the edge \(AB\text{.}\)
🔗

Solution.

Step 1: the triangle. The lines meet at \(A\text{;}\) their other ends are \(G\) and \(B\text{.}\) So the angle is angle \(GAB\) in triangle \(ABG\text{.}\)
🔗
Step 2: a right angle? \(AB\) is at right angles to the face \(BCGF\text{,}\) and \(BG\) lies in that face, so angle \(ABG = 90^{\circ}\text{.}\)
🔗
Step 3: the sides. \(BG^2 = 5^2 + 7^2 = 74\text{,}\) and \(AG^2 = 12^2 + 74 = 218\text{,}\) so \(AG = \sqrt{218} \approx 14.76\,\text{cm}\text{.}\)
🔗
Step 4: the angle. \(AB\) is next to angle \(GAB\) and \(AG\) is the hypotenuse:
\begin{equation*} \cos GAB = \frac{12}{\sqrt{218}} \end{equation*}
so angle \(GAB \approx 35.6^{\circ}\text{.}\)
