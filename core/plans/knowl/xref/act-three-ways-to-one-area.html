Learner Experience 14.4.2 Three Ways to One Area.

A plot in Machakos is drawn on a grid in which one unit is \(10\,\text{m}\text{.}\) It is bounded by a straight road (the \(x\)-axis), two straight fences \(x = 0\) and \(x = 4\text{,}\) and a footpath along the curve \(y = x^2 + 3\text{.}\)
🔗
  1. Use the trapezoidal rule with \(4\) strips to estimate the area under the curve, in grid units.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(h = 1\text{;}\) ordinates \(3, 4, 7, 12, 19\text{;}\) \(A \approx \tfrac{1}{2}(3 + 19 + 2 \times 23) = 34\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Use the mid-ordinate rule with \(4\) strips.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Mid-ordinates at \(x = 0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5\text{:}\) \(3.25, 5.25, 9.25, 15.25\text{;}\) \(A \approx 1 \times 33 = 33\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Find the exact area by integration.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\displaystyle\int_0^4 (x^2 + 3)\,dx = \left[\frac{x^3}{3} + 3x\right]_0^4 = \frac{64}{3} + 12 = \frac{100}{3} \approx 33.33\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Which estimate is too big and which is too small? Explain why, looking at the chords and at the corners of the rectangles in Figure 14.4.3.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The trapezoidal estimate is too big and the mid-ordinate estimate too small. The curve bends upwards, so every chord lies above the curve and each trapezium includes a thin sliver above it. Each rectangle loses more area on the side where the curve rises steeply than it gains on the other side.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Use the interactive in Figure 14.4.6 with \(8\) strips. What happens to the two estimates?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(T = 33.5\) and \(M = 33.25\text{:}\) both are closer to \(33.33\text{,}\) and still on the same sides.
