Example 6.3.30 Which Method Fits?

Choose a method for each situation.
  1. The outline of a lake traced from a county map onto squared paper.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The depth of a river measured at both banks and every \(2\,\text{m}\) across.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The width of a plot measured at the middle of each \(10\,\text{m}\) strip along a road.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. The area under a curve whose equation is given.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗

Solution.

  1. Counting squares: there is an outline but no measurements.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The trapezoidal rule: the depths are at the edges of equal strips, including both ends.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The mid-ordinate rule: the widths are at the middles of the strips.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Either rule: the equation gives the height anywhere, at the edges or at the middles.
    🔗
    🔗
