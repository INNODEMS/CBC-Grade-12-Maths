Exercise 6. Challenge: Two Possible Cubes.

A cube has vertices at \((3, 2, 0)\text{,}\) \((8, 2, 0)\) and \((3, 7, 0)\text{.}\) Find the other five vertices.
🔗
Hint.
Is there only one such cube?
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\((8, 7, 0)\text{,}\) and either \((3, 2, 5)\text{,}\) \((8, 2, 5)\text{,}\) \((8, 7, 5)\text{,}\) \((3, 7, 5)\text{,}\) or the same four points with \(z = -5\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
The three given vertices lie in the \(xy\)-plane, and the edges from \((3, 2, 0)\) are \(5\) long, so one face is the square with fourth corner \((8, 7, 0)\text{.}\) The cube can stand on this square, with its other face at \(z = 5\text{,}\) or hang below it, with its other face at \(z = -5\text{.}\) Both cubes have edge \(5\text{,}\) so there are two answers.
