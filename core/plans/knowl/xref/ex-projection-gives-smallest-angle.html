Exercise 6. Challenge: The Projection Gives the Smallest Angle.

In a cuboid \(ABCDEFGH\text{,}\) show that angle \(GAC\) is smaller than angle \(GAB\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Angles \(ACG\) and \(ABG\) are both right angles. Compare \(\cos GAC = \dfrac{AC}{AG}\) with \(\cos GAB = \dfrac{AB}{AG}\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(AC \gt AB\text{,}\) so \(\cos GAC \gt \cos GAB\text{,}\) so angle \(GAC\) is smaller.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
Triangle \(ACG\) has a right angle at \(C\) and triangle \(ABG\) has a right angle at \(B\text{,}\) so \(\cos GAC = \dfrac{AC}{AG}\) and \(\cos GAB = \dfrac{AB}{AG}\text{.}\) \(AC\) is the hypotenuse of triangle \(ABC\text{,}\) so \(AC \gt AB\text{,}\) and therefore \(\cos GAC \gt \cos GAB\text{.}\) For acute angles a larger cosine means a smaller angle, so angle \(GAC\) is smaller than angle \(GAB\text{.}\)
