Exercise 6. Challenge: Combining the Rules (Extension).

For \(y = x^3\) from \(x = 0\) to \(x = 2\) with \(2\) strips, \(T = 5\) and \(M = 3.5\text{.}\) Show that \(\dfrac{T + 2M}{3}\) is exactly the area. Explain why this works for every polynomial of degree at most \(3\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Use the second error pattern: the mid-ordinate error is \(-\frac{1}{2}\) of the trapezoidal error.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
\(\frac{5 + 7}{3} = 4\text{,}\) the exact area.
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\(\frac{5 + 2 \times 3.5}{3} = 4\text{.}\) If the exact area is \(A\) and \(T = A + E\text{,}\) then \(M = A - \frac{E}{2}\text{,}\) so \(T + 2M = 3A\) and the errors cancel. This combination is called Simpson’s rule; it is beyond the KICD Grade 12 design.
