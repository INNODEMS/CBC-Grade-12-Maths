Learner Experience 10.6.2 Points on a Straight Road.

A county map of the area near Nakuru has a grid in kilometres. A straight road passes through the points \(A(1, 2)\) and \(B(4, 4)\text{.}\) A school is at \(C(10, 8)\) and a dispensary at \(D(7, 7)\text{.}\) A footpath runs from a borehole at \(F(2, 5)\) to a shop at \(G(5, 7)\text{.}\)
🔗
  1. Write \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) and \(\overrightarrow{BC}\) as column vectors. How is \(\overrightarrow{BC}\) related to \(\overrightarrow{AB}\text{?}\) Is the school on the road?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\overrightarrow{BC} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = 2\overrightarrow{AB}\text{.}\) The two vectors are parallel and both pass through \(B\text{,}\) so \(A\text{,}\) \(B\) and \(C\) are on one straight line: the school is on the road.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Find \(\overrightarrow{BD}\text{.}\) Is the dispensary on the road?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\overrightarrow{BD} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Comparing with \(\overrightarrow{AB}\text{:}\) \(3 \div 3 = 1\) but \(3 \div 2 = 1.5\text{,}\) so \(\overrightarrow{BD}\) is not a multiple of \(\overrightarrow{AB}\text{.}\) \(D\) is off the road.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Use your answer to (a) to give the ratio \(AB : BC\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\overrightarrow{BC} = 2\overrightarrow{AB}\text{,}\) so \(BC\) is twice as long as \(AB\text{:}\) \(AB : BC = 1 : 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Find \(\overrightarrow{FG}\) and compare it with \(\overrightarrow{AB}\text{.}\) Then find \(\overrightarrow{AF}\text{.}\) Is the footpath part of the road?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\overrightarrow{FG} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \overrightarrow{AB}\text{,}\) so the footpath is parallel to the road. But \(\overrightarrow{AF} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\) is not a multiple of \(\overrightarrow{AB}\text{,}\) so \(F\) is not on the road. The footpath is a parallel path, not part of the same road.
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 10.6.4.

    This is the key point of the lesson: parallel vectors on their own do not show that points are collinear. Ask learners to name the extra fact that made (a) work (the shared point \(B\)).
