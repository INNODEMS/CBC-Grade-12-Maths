Exercise 6. Challenge: Building a Vector from Two Others.

\(\mathbf{p} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{q} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
  1. Find numbers \(s\) and \(t\) with \(s\mathbf{p} + t\mathbf{q} = \begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ 5 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Show that no numbers \(s\) and \(t\) give \(s\mathbf{p} + t\mathbf{q} = \begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ 5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Hint.
Use two components to find \(s\) and \(t\text{,}\) then test the third.
🔗
🔗
Answer.
(a) \(s = 3\text{,}\) \(t = 2\) (b) the first two components force \(s = 3\text{,}\) \(t = 2\text{,}\) which give a third component of \(5\text{,}\) not \(0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
(a) The first two components give \(2s + t = 8\) and \(-s + 4t = 5\text{.}\) From the first, \(t = 8 - 2s\text{;}\) then \(-s + 32 - 8s = 5\text{,}\) so \(s = 3\) and \(t = 2\text{.}\) Check the third: \(3(3) + 2(-2) = 5\text{.}\) Correct.
🔗
(b) The first two components are the same equations, so again \(s = 3\text{,}\) \(t = 2\text{.}\) But then the third component is \(5\text{,}\) not \(0\text{.}\) No \(s\) and \(t\) work.
