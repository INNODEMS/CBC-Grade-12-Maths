    Subsection 9.4.2 Translating Waves and Combining Transformations

    Upon completion of this lesson, learners will be able to:
    1. Know: \(y = f(x + c)\) and \(y = f(x) + d\) are translations; \(\sin(bx + c) = \sin b\left(x + \dfrac{c}{b}\right)\text{;}\) the order of transformations matters for the translation.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Do: give translation vectors; describe and apply a sequence of transformations; write the equation after a sequence; find images of points.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Apply: recognise waves around us.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Teacher Resource 9.4.19 Problem-Solving and Discovery.

    Suggested Time
    🔗
    14 minutes
    🔗
    Objective
    🔗
    Learners slide a traced wave and discover the sign rule and the shift \(\dfrac{c}{b}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Teacher’s Role
    🔗
    Use tracing paper again. After part (a) ask: “Why does \(+60^\circ\) move the wave to the left?” Pairs do part (c) together and compare with the next pair.
    🔗
    🔗

    Learner Experience 9.4.20 Sliding on Tracing Paper.

    1. Trace \(y = \sin x\) and slide the tracing \(60^\circ\) to the left. Where is the first maximum now? Which equation, \(y = \sin(x + 60^\circ)\) or \(y = \sin(x - 60^\circ)\text{,}\) has its maximum there?
      🔗
      Solution.
      At \(x = 30^\circ\text{.}\) \(y = \sin(x + 60^\circ)\text{,}\) since \(30 + 60 = 90\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Slide the tracing \(360^\circ\) to the left. What do you notice? Which equation describes the new position?
      🔗
      Solution.
      The wave lands exactly on itself: \(y = \sin(x + 360^\circ)\) is the same graph as \(y = \sin x\text{,}\) because the period is \(360^\circ\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Trace \(y = \sin 2x\text{.}\) How far must you slide it to get \(y = \sin(2x + 70^\circ)\text{?}\) Test with the maximum.
      🔗
      Solution.
      \(35^\circ\) to the left: the maximum of \(y = \sin 2x\) at \(45^\circ\) must move to where \(2x + 70 = 90\text{,}\) that is \(x = 10^\circ\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Key Takeaway 9.4.22 Translating and Combining.

    • \(y = f(x + c)\) is the image of \(y = f(x)\) under the translation \(\begin{pmatrix} -c \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{:}\) the graph moves the opposite way to the sign.
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(y = f(x) + d\) is the image under the translation \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ d \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Since \(a\sin(bx + c) + d = a\sin b\left(x + \dfrac{c}{b}\right) + d\text{,}\) the graph of \(y = a\sin(bx + c) + d\) is obtained from \(y = \sin x\) by, in order: a stretch with the \(x\)-axis invariant, stretch factor \(a\text{;}\) a stretch with the \(y\)-axis invariant, stretch factor \(\dfrac{1}{b}\text{;}\) the translation \(\begin{pmatrix} -c/b \\ d \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Inside the bracket, the graph moves by the angle divided by \(b\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Another order also works for the \(x\)-direction: first the translation \(\begin{pmatrix} -c \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) then the stretch with stretch factor \(\dfrac{1}{b}\text{.}\) The size of the translation depends on the order.
      🔗
      🔗

    Example 9.4.26 A Translation Along the \(x\)-Axis.

    Describe the transformation that maps \(y = \sin x\) onto \(y = \sin(x - 45^\circ)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Solution.

    The translation \(\begin{pmatrix} 45^\circ \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) that is \(45^\circ\) to the right. The phase angle is \(-45^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 9.4.27 A Translation Along the \(y\)-Axis.

    Describe the transformation that maps \(y = \cos x\) onto \(y = \cos x - 2\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Solution.

    The translation \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) The mean line moves from \(y = 0\) to \(y = -2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 9.4.28 The Shift \(\dfrac{c}{b}\).

    Describe how \(y = \sin(2x + 70^\circ)\) is obtained (a) from \(y = \sin 2x\) (b) from \(y = \sin x\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Solution.

    (a) \(\sin(2x + 70^\circ) = \sin 2(x + 35^\circ)\text{,}\) so it is the translation \(\begin{pmatrix} -35^\circ \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) of \(y = \sin 2x\) (Figure 9.4.23).
    🔗
    (b) Either a stretch with the \(y\)-axis invariant, stretch factor \(\dfrac{1}{2}\text{,}\) then the translation \(\begin{pmatrix} -35^\circ \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{;}\) or first the translation \(\begin{pmatrix} -70^\circ \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) (giving \(y = \sin(x + 70^\circ)\)), then the stretch with stretch factor \(\dfrac{1}{2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 9.4.29 A Sequence of Transformations.

    Describe a sequence of transformations that maps \(y = \sin x\) onto \(y = 2\sin(3x - 90^\circ) + 1\text{,}\) and follow the point \((90^\circ, 1)\) through it.
    🔗

    Solution.

    \(2\sin(3x - 90^\circ) + 1 = 2\sin 3(x - 30^\circ) + 1\text{.}\) In order: a stretch with the \(x\)-axis invariant, stretch factor 2; a stretch with the \(y\)-axis invariant, stretch factor \(\dfrac{1}{3}\text{;}\) the translation \(\begin{pmatrix} 30^\circ \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \((90^\circ, 1) \mapsto (90^\circ, 2) \mapsto (30^\circ, 2) \mapsto (60^\circ, 3)\text{.}\) Check: \(3(60) - 90 = 90\) and \(2\sin 90^\circ + 1 = 3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 9.4.30 From a Description to an Equation.

    The graph of \(y = \cos x\) is stretched with the \(x\)-axis invariant, stretch factor 3, and then translated by \(\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Write down the equation of the image.
    🔗

    Solution.

    The stretch gives \(y = 3\cos x\text{;}\) the translation subtracts 1: \(y = 3\cos x - 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Waves Around Us.

    Sine and cosine waves describe many things that repeat. When a nyatiti string vibrates, the air pressure near it rises and falls like a sine wave: a tighter string gives a shorter period and a higher note, and a harder pluck gives a bigger amplitude and a louder sound. Kenya’s mains electricity alternates 50 times a second, so the graph of its voltage against time is a wave with period \(\dfrac{1}{50}\) of a second. The tide at Mombasa rises and falls about twice a day. Radio and phone signals, and the waves from an earthquake or a tsunami, are waves too. Changing the amplitude, the period or the phase of a wave is exactly the stretching and translating of this section.

    Answer.
    \(\begin{pmatrix} -15^\circ \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\)

    3.

    Describe a sequence of transformations that maps \(y = \cos x\) onto \(y = 4\cos(2x - 60^\circ) - 3\text{,}\) and find the image of \((0, 1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    Stretch factor 4 with the \(x\)-axis invariant; stretch factor \(\dfrac{1}{2}\) with the \(y\)-axis invariant; translation \(\begin{pmatrix} 30^\circ \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}\text{.}\) Image \((30^\circ, 1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \((0, 1) \mapsto (0, 4) \mapsto (0, 4) \mapsto (30^\circ, 1)\text{.}\) Check: \(4\cos(60^\circ - 60^\circ) - 3 = 1\text{.}\)
    🔗

    4.

    Find the smallest translation to the right, along the \(x\)-axis, that maps \(y = \cos x\) onto \(y = \sin x\text{.}\) Check your answer at three angles.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\begin{pmatrix} 90^\circ \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\text{:}\) the maximum of \(y = \cos x\) at \(0^\circ\) moves to the maximum of \(y = \sin x\) at \(90^\circ\text{,}\) and \(\cos(x - 90^\circ) = \sin x\text{;}\) at \(90^\circ\text{,}\) \(180^\circ\text{,}\) \(270^\circ\) both give \(1, 0, -1\text{.}\)

    7. Challenge: A Reflection That Is a Translation.

    Show that the graph of \(y = -\sin x\) is a translation of the graph of \(y = \sin x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Compare \(\sin(x - 180^\circ)\) with \(-\sin x\) at a few angles, then think about the unit circle.
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\sin(x - 180^\circ) = -\sin x\text{,}\) so the translation \(\begin{pmatrix} 180^\circ \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}\) maps \(y = \sin x\) onto \(y = -\sin x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    On the unit circle the point at \(x - 180^\circ\) is opposite the point at \(x\text{,}\) so its \(y\)-coordinate is \(-\sin x\text{.}\)
