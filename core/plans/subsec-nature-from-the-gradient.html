    Subsection 13.1.2 The Nature of a Stationary Point from the Gradient

    Upon completion of this lesson, learners will be able to:
    1. Know: maximum point, minimum point and point of inflection, and the sign pattern of \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) around each.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Do: classify stationary points with a gradient table.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Apply: say how a model behaves at its stationary points.
      🔗
      🔗

    1. Find the stationary point of each curve: \(y = x^2 - 4x + 7\text{;}\) \(y = 6x - x^2\text{;}\) \(y = x^3 + 2\text{.}\)

      🔗
      🔗
      🔗

      🔗
      🔗

    Key Takeaway 13.1.23 The Nature of a Stationary Point.

    At a maximum point the curve rises to the point and falls after it: the gradient goes from positive to \(0\) to negative. At a minimum point the curve falls then rises: the gradient goes from negative to \(0\) to positive. Maximum and minimum points are called turning points.
    🔗
    At a point of inflection the curve flattens for an instant but carries on the same way: the gradient has the same sign on both sides.
    🔗
    A maximum point need not be the highest point of the whole curve: the curve may go higher elsewhere. That is why it is sometimes called a local maximum. Older Kenyan textbooks spell the third kind “point of inflexion”; it is the same thing.

    Example 13.1.28 Classifying with a Gradient Table.

    Find the stationary points of \(y = 2x^3 - 24x + 5\) and determine their nature.
    🔗

    Solution.

    \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 6x^2 - 24 = 6(x - 2)(x + 2)\text{,}\) which is \(0\) at \(x = -2\) and \(x = 2\text{.}\) Then \(y = -16 + 48 + 5 = 37\) and \(y = 16 - 48 + 5 = -27\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Choose test values either side, but not beyond the other stationary point: \(x = -3\text{,}\) \(x = 0\) (between the two points, so it serves both) and \(x = 3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    So \((-2, 37)\) is a maximum point and \((2, -27)\) is a minimum point.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.1.30 A Point of Inflection.

    Determine the nature of the stationary point \((2, 5)\) of \(y = x^3 - 6x^2 + 12x - 3\) from Example 13.1.10.
    🔗

    Solution.

    \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3(x - 2)^2\text{.}\) At \(x = 1\) it is \(3\text{,}\) and at \(x = 3\) it is \(3\text{.}\) The pattern is \(+, 0, +\text{:}\) the curve rises, flattens for an instant at \((2, 5)\) and rises again. So \((2, 5)\) is a point of inflection.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 13.1.31 A Quartic with a Flat Step.

    Find the stationary points of \(y = x^4 - 4x^3\) and determine their nature.
    🔗

    Solution.

    \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 4x^3 - 12x^2 = 4x^2(x - 3)\text{,}\) which is \(0\) at \(x = 0\) and \(x = 3\text{.}\) The points are \((0, 0)\) and \((3, 81 - 108) = (3, -27)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    So \((0, 0)\) is a point of inflection (the curve falls on both sides) and \((3, -27)\) is a minimum point.
    🔗
    🔗

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    Find the stationary point of \(y = x^2 + 6x - 2\) and determine its nature.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \((-3, -11)\text{,}\) a minimum point
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Find the stationary points of \(y = 3x - x^3\) and determine their nature.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \((-1, -2)\) minimum point; \((1, 2)\) maximum point
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Find the stationary point of \(y = x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x + 4\) and determine its nature.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \((-1, 3)\text{,}\) a point of inflection
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 + 6x + 3 = 3(x + 1)^2\text{,}\) which is \(0\) only at \(x = -1\text{,}\) where \(y = 3\text{.}\) It is positive on both sides, so the point is a point of inflection.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Find the three stationary points of \(y = x^4 - 18x^2 + 5\) and determine their nature. Use the test values \(x = -4, -1, 1, 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \((-3, -76)\) minimum point, \((0, 5)\) maximum point, \((3, -76)\) minimum point
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 4x^3 - 36x = 4x(x - 3)(x + 3)\text{,}\) zero at \(x = -3, 0, 3\text{.}\) At \(x = -4, -1, 1, 4\) the gradient is \(-112, 32, -32, 112\text{,}\) giving the pattern \(-, 0, +, 0, -, 0, +\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    The temperature, \(T\,^{\circ}\text{C}\text{,}\) in Eldoret \(t\) hours after 6 a.m. on a dry day is modelled by \(T = 10 + 4t - \dfrac{t^2}{4}\text{.}\) Find the stationary point, determine its nature and say what it means.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \((8, 26)\text{,}\) a maximum point: the hottest time is 2 p.m., at \(26\,^{\circ}\text{C}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\dfrac{dT}{dt} = 4 - \dfrac{t}{2} = 0\) at \(t = 8\text{,}\) and \(T = 10 + 32 - 16 = 26\text{.}\) The gradient is positive before \(t = 8\) and negative after it, so this is a maximum point.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    Explain why \(y = x^3 + 3x\) has no turning points.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 + 3 \gt 0\) for every \(x\text{,}\) so the gradient is never \(0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    7. Challenge: Make a Point of Inflection.

    Find \(b\) and \(c\) so that \(y = x^3 + bx^2 + cx\) has a point of inflection at \(x = 2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) must be \(3(x - 2)^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(b = -6\text{,}\) \(c = 12\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 + 2bx + c\) must equal \(3(x - 2)^2 = 3x^2 - 12x + 12\text{,}\) so \(2b = -12\) and \(c = 12\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
