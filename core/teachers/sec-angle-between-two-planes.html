      🔗
      🔗

      🔗
      🔗

    Key Takeaway 8.6.2 Angle Between Two Planes.

    Two planes that are not parallel meet along a straight line, their line of intersection (or common line). To find the angle between the planes, choose a point on the common line and draw a line in each plane at right angles to the common line at that point. The angle between these two lines is the angle between the planes, from \(0^{\circ}\) to \(90^{\circ}\text{.}\) Stand on the common line and look along it: the angle you see is the angle between the planes.
    🔗
    For a face \(VBC\) of a right pyramid and its base, the common line is \(BC\text{.}\) With \(M\) the midpoint of \(BC\text{,}\) both \(VM\) and \(OM\) are at right angles to \(BC\text{,}\) so the angle between the face and the base is angle \(VMO\text{,}\) and \(\tan VMO = \dfrac{VO}{OM}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    A line of greatest slope of a sloping plane is a line in it at right angles to its horizontal common line with the ground. Its angle with the horizontal is the angle between the plane and the ground: \(VM\) is a line of greatest slope of the face \(VBC\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Warning 8.6.4 The Midpoint, Not the Corner.

    For the angle between a face \(VBC\) of a right pyramid and its base, use the midpoint \(M\) of the base edge: angle \(VMO\text{.}\) Angle \(VCO\text{,}\) at the corner \(C\text{,}\) is the angle between the edge \(VC\) and the base, which is smaller.
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 8.6.6 Three-Dimensional Geometry Online.

    Free 3D graphing tools on the internet can draw a cuboid or a pyramid and let you turn it to see lines and planes from every side. Use a well-known site that your teacher suggests. You should not need an account: never give your name, phone number, school or other personal details to use such a tool, and ask your teacher before installing anything on a phone or computer.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.6.7 The Slope of a Gazebo Roof.

    The roof of a gazebo at a hotel in Naivasha is a right pyramid \(VABCD\) on a square base of side \(4.8\,\text{m}\text{,}\) with height \(VO = 1.6\,\text{m}\text{.}\) Find (a) the angle between the face \(VBC\) and the base; (b) the angle between the edge \(VC\) and the base.
    🔗
    Solution.
    (a) The face \(VBC\) and the base meet along \(BC\text{.}\) With \(M\) the midpoint of \(BC\text{,}\) \(VM\) and \(OM\) are both at right angles to \(BC\text{.}\) \(OM = 2.4\,\text{m}\text{,}\) half the side. In triangle \(VOM\text{,}\) with the right angle at \(O\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \tan VMO = \frac{1.6}{2.4} \end{equation*}
    so the face slopes at about \(33.7^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) \(OC\) is half the diagonal: \(OC = 2.4\sqrt{2} \approx 3.39\,\text{m}\text{.}\) \(\tan VCO = \frac{1.6}{2.4\sqrt{2}}\text{,}\) so the edge makes about \(25.2^{\circ}\) with the base. The edge is less steep than the face.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.6.9 A Rectangular-Based Pyramid.

    A model is a right pyramid \(VABCD\) on a rectangular base with \(AB = 16\,\text{cm}\) and \(BC = 10\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Its height \(VO\) is \(7\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) \(N\) is the midpoint of \(AB\) and \(M\) the midpoint of \(BC\text{.}\) Find the angle between the base and (a) the face \(VAB\text{;}\) (b) the face \(VBC\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    (a) Face \(VAB\) meets the base along \(AB\text{;}\) the lines at right angles to \(AB\) at \(N\) are \(VN\) and \(ON\text{.}\) \(ON\) is parallel to \(BC\text{,}\) so \(ON = \frac{10}{2} = 5\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) \(\tan VNO = \frac{7}{5}\text{,}\) so the angle is about \(54.5^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) Face \(VBC\) meets the base along \(BC\text{.}\) \(OM\) is parallel to \(AB\text{,}\) so \(OM = \frac{16}{2} = 8\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) \(\tan VMO = \frac{7}{8}\text{,}\) so the angle is about \(41.2^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Opposite faces slope at equal angles; neighbouring faces of a rectangular-based pyramid do not. The face on the longer side is the steeper one.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.6.10 A Plane Through a Cuboid.

    A packing crate is a cuboid \(ABCDEFGH\) with \(AB = 16\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) \(BC = 10\,\text{cm}\) and \(AE = 12\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) A sheet of card is fitted along the plane \(ABGH\text{.}\) Find the angle between (a) the plane \(ABGH\) and the base \(ABCD\text{;}\) (b) the plane \(ACGE\) and the base.
    🔗
    Solution.
    (a) The plane \(ABGH\) and the base meet along \(AB\text{.}\) At \(B\text{,}\) the edge \(BC\) lies in the base and is at right angles to \(AB\text{;}\) the line \(BG\) lies in the plane and is also at right angles to \(AB\) (it lies in the face \(BCGF\text{,}\) and \(AB\) is at right angles to that face). So the angle is angle \(CBG\text{.}\) In triangle \(BCG\text{,}\) with the right angle at \(C\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \tan CBG = \frac{12}{10} \end{equation*}
    so the angle is about \(50.2^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) The plane \(ACGE\) contains the vertical edges \(AE\) and \(CG\text{,}\) so it stands upright: the angle between it and the base is \(90^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Three-Dimensional Geometry Around Us.

    At the start of this chapter we asked: where do we use the lengths and angles of solid shapes in everyday life? You can now find any length or angle in a solid by finding the right triangle and flattening it.
    🔗
    • A fundi building a roof needs the length of each rafter and the slope of each face. County building plans state the roof pitch, which is the angle between a roof face and the horizontal.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Solar panels near the equator are tilted only a little, about \(10^{\circ}\) to \(15^{\circ}\text{,}\) so that they face the sun well and rain still washes off the dust.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Ramps for wheelchair users must not be too steep: the guideline is about the steepest line on the ramp, as in Example 8.5.9.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Tents and greenhouses are prisms; the tent maker needs the slope of the sides and the length of each guy rope.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Surveyors and drone pilots measure angles of elevation to a line in space, and 3D design and printing programs work out the lengths and angles of every edge of a model.
      🔗
      🔗

    1.

    A right pyramid has a square base of side \(14\,\text{cm}\) and height \(9\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Find the angle between a face and the base.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(52.1^{\circ}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(OM = 7\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) so \(\tan VMO = \dfrac{9}{7}\) and the angle is about \(52.1^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    A right pyramid \(VABCD\) has a rectangular base with \(AB = 18\,\text{cm}\) and \(BC = 12\,\text{cm}\text{,}\) and each sloping edge is \(15\,\text{cm}\) long. Find the height, and the angle that each face makes with the base.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Height \(\sqrt{108} \approx 10.39\,\text{cm}\text{;}\) the faces on the \(18\,\text{cm}\) sides make \(60.0^{\circ}\text{,}\) the faces on the \(12\,\text{cm}\) sides \(49.1^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(AC^2 = 324 + 144 = 468\text{,}\) so \(OA^2 = 117\) and \(VO^2 = 225 - 117 = 108\text{:}\) the height is \(\sqrt{108} \approx 10.39\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Face \(VAB\) (on an \(18\,\text{cm}\) side): \(ON = 6\text{,}\) \(\tan VNO = \dfrac{\sqrt{108}}{6}\text{,}\) so the angle is \(60.0^{\circ}\text{.}\) Face \(VBC\) (on a \(12\,\text{cm}\) side): \(OM = 9\text{,}\) \(\tan VMO = \dfrac{\sqrt{108}}{9}\text{,}\) so the angle is about \(49.1^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    The grain store of Exercise 8.4.5 is a cuboid \(ABCDEFGH\) with \(AB = 15\,\text{m}\text{,}\) \(BC = 9\,\text{m}\) and \(AE = 5\,\text{m}\text{.}\) Find the angle between the base and (a) the plane \(CDEF\text{;}\) (b) the plane \(BCHE\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(29.1^{\circ}\) (b) \(18.4^{\circ}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    (a) The common line is \(CD\text{.}\) At \(C\text{,}\) \(CB\) (in the base) and \(CF\) (in the plane) are at right angles to \(CD\text{,}\) so the angle is angle \(BCF\text{:}\) \(\tan BCF = \dfrac{5}{9}\text{,}\) about \(29.1^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) The common line is \(BC\text{.}\) At \(B\text{,}\) \(BA\) and \(BE\) are at right angles to \(BC\text{,}\) so the angle is angle \(ABE\text{:}\) \(\tan ABE = \dfrac{5}{15}\text{,}\) about \(18.4^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    For the tent in Figure 8.1.11 (\(AB = 2.4\,\text{m}\text{,}\) \(CA = CB = 1.5\,\text{m}\text{,}\) \(AD = 3\,\text{m}\)), find the angle between (a) a sloping side \(ACFD\) and the ground; (b) the two sloping sides.
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(36.9^{\circ}\) (b) \(73.7^{\circ}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    (a) The common line is \(AD\text{.}\) In the front end, \(AC\) and \(AN\) are at right angles to \(AD\text{,}\) so the angle is angle \(CAN\text{:}\) \(\cos CAN = \dfrac{1.2}{1.5}\text{,}\) about \(36.9^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) The common line is the ridge \(CF\text{.}\) \(CA\) and \(CB\) are at right angles to it, so the angle comes from angle \(ACB \approx 106.3^{\circ}\) (Example 8.2.8). The angle between two planes is not obtuse, so it is \(180^{\circ} - 106.3^{\circ} = 73.7^{\circ}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    A solar panel \(1.6\,\text{m}\) by \(1.0\,\text{m}\) on a flat roof in Garissa is hinged along its long lower edge and tilted at \(12^{\circ}\) to the roof. How high above the roof is its top edge?
    🔗
    Answer.
    About \(0.21\,\text{m}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The hinge is the common line. The short side, \(1.0\,\text{m}\text{,}\) is at right angles to the hinge, so it makes \(12^{\circ}\) with the roof. The height is \(1.0 \times \sin 12^{\circ} \approx 0.21\,\text{m}\text{.}\)

    6. Challenge: A Corner Cut From a Cube.

    A cube \(ABCDEFGH\) has edges of \(6\,\text{cm}\text{.}\) Find the angle between the plane \(BDG\) and the base \(ABCD\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    The common line is \(BD\text{.}\) The line from \(C\) at right angles to \(BD\) meets it at the centre \(X\) of the base.
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(54.7^{\circ}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    At \(X\text{,}\) \(XC\) lies in the base and is at right angles to \(BD\) (the diagonals of a square cross at right angles). Triangle \(BGD\) is equilateral, so \(XG\text{,}\) from \(G\) to the midpoint of \(BD\text{,}\) is also at right angles to \(BD\text{.}\) The angle is angle \(GXC\text{,}\) with the right angle at \(C\text{.}\) \(XC = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{72} = 3\sqrt{2}\text{,}\) so \(\tan GXC = \dfrac{6}{3\sqrt{2}}\) and the angle is about \(54.7^{\circ}\text{.}\)
