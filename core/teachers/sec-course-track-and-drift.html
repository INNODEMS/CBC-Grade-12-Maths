    Key Takeaway 11.5.2 Course, Track and Drift.

    Directions are three-figure bearings, measured clockwise from north, from \(000^\circ\) up to (but not including) \(360^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    • The course is the direction in which a boat or aircraft is steered (pointed). Its speed through the water or air (the water speed or airspeed) goes with it.
      🔗
      🔗
    • A current is moving water, and a wind moving air. A current flowing towards \(120^\circ\) carries a boat towards \(120^\circ\text{.}\) A wind blowing from \(300^\circ\) pushes an aircraft towards \(120^\circ\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • The track is the direction in which the boat or aircraft actually moves over the ground or the sea bed. Its speed along the track is the ground speed.
      🔗
      🔗
    • The drift (or drift angle) is the angle between the course and the track.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    The course is where the boat points; the track is where it goes. In the velocity triangle the current arrow starts at the tip of the course arrow, and the track arrow (drawn with a double head) joins the start to the end:
    \begin{equation*} \text{course velocity} + \text{current velocity} = \text{track velocity}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Solve the triangle with Pythagoras and \(\tan\) when the current is at right angles to the course, and with the cosine and sine rules otherwise.
    🔗
    Sailors also use “set and drift” to mean the direction and speed of a current. In this book “drift” always means the drift angle.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 11.5.5 Crossing the Tana River.

    At Garsen a boat is steered straight across the Tana River at \(2.4\,\text{m/s}\) through the water. The river flows at \(0.7\,\text{m/s}\) and is \(120\,\text{m}\) wide. Find the ground speed, the drift angle, the time to cross and how far downstream the boat lands.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The current is at right angles to the course, so the velocity triangle is right-angled with the ground speed as its hypotenuse.
    \begin{align*} \text{ground speed} \amp = \sqrt{2.4^2 + 0.7^2} = \sqrt{6.25} = 2.5\,\text{m/s}\\ \tan d \amp = \frac{0.7}{2.4}, \quad d = 16.3^\circ \end{align*}
    The speed across the river is still \(2.4\,\text{m/s}\text{,}\) so the crossing takes \(120 \div 2.4 = 50\,\text{s}\text{.}\) In that time the current carries the boat \(50 \times 0.7 = 35\,\text{m}\) downstream.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 11.5.6 Wind on a Flight from Wilson Airport.

    A light aircraft leaves Wilson Airport, Nairobi, on a course of \(090^\circ\) with an airspeed of \(160\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) A wind of \(28\,\text{knots}\) blows from \(000^\circ\text{.}\) Find the ground speed, the drift angle and the track.
    🔗
    Solution.
    A wind from \(000^\circ\) (from the north) blows towards \(180^\circ\text{,}\) at right angles to the course.
    \begin{align*} \text{ground speed} \amp = \sqrt{160^2 + 28^2} = 162.4\,\text{knots}\\ \tan d \amp = \frac{28}{160}, \quad d = 9.9^\circ \end{align*}
    The wind pushes the aircraft south of east, so the track is \(090^\circ + 9.9^\circ = 099.9^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Check with column vectors (east, north): \(\begin{pmatrix} 160 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -28 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 160 \\ -28 \end{pmatrix}\text{,}\) which has length \(162.4\) and points \(9.9^\circ\) south of east.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 11.5.7 A Dhow off Lamu.

    A dhow off Lamu is steered on a course of \(040^\circ\) at \(8\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) A current flows towards \(160^\circ\) at \(3\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) Find the ground speed, the drift angle and the track (see Figure 11.5.3).
    🔗
    Solution.
    The directions \(040^\circ\) and \(160^\circ\) differ by \(120^\circ\text{,}\) so inside the triangle the angle between the course arrow and the current arrow is \(180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 1: cosine rule.
    \begin{equation*} \text{ground speed}^2 = 8^2 + 3^2 - 2 \times 8 \times 3 \cos 60^\circ = 64 + 9 - 24 = 49 \end{equation*}
    so the ground speed is \(7\,\text{knots}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 2: sine rule.
    \begin{equation*} \sin d = \frac{3 \sin 60^\circ}{7}, \quad d = 21.8^\circ \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Step 3: track. The current pushes the dhow clockwise from its course, so the track is \(040^\circ + 21.8^\circ = 061.8^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Type decimals with a point, for example 36.8. Type angles in degrees as numbers, without the degree sign. Choose N, S, E or W from each list.

    1.

    A boat is steered straight across a river at \(3.5\,\text{m/s}\) through the water. The current is \(1.2\,\text{m/s}\text{.}\) Find the ground speed and the drift angle.
    🔗
    Answer.

    2.

    An aircraft flies on a course of \(180^\circ\) with an airspeed of \(210\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) A wind of \(36\,\text{knots}\) blows from \(090^\circ\text{.}\) Find the ground speed, the drift angle and the track.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(213.1\,\text{knots}\text{;}\) \(9.7^\circ\text{;}\) \(189.7^\circ\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The wind blows towards \(270^\circ\text{,}\) at right angles to the course. \(\sqrt{210^2 + 36^2} = 213.1\,\text{knots}\text{;}\) \(\tan d = 36 \div 210\text{,}\) \(d = 9.7^\circ\text{.}\) Pushed west of south, so the track is \(180^\circ + 9.7^\circ = 189.7^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    A ship is steered on a course of \(300^\circ\) at \(14\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) A current flows towards \(030^\circ\) at \(5\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) Find the ground speed, the drift angle and the track.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(14.9\,\text{knots}\text{;}\) \(19.7^\circ\text{;}\) \(319.7^\circ\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(030^\circ\) is \(90^\circ\) clockwise from \(300^\circ\text{.}\) \(\sqrt{14^2 + 5^2} = 14.9\,\text{knots}\text{;}\) \(\tan d = 5 \div 14\text{,}\) \(d = 19.7^\circ\text{;}\) track \(300^\circ + 19.7^\circ = 319.7^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    A ship is steered on a course of \(120^\circ\) at \(9\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) A current flows towards \(060^\circ\) at \(4\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) Find the ground speed, the drift angle and the track.
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(11.5\,\text{knots}\text{;}\) \(17.5^\circ\text{;}\) \(102.5^\circ\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The directions differ by \(60^\circ\text{,}\) so the angle inside the triangle is \(120^\circ\text{.}\) \(\text{ground speed}^2 = 9^2 + 4^2 - 2 \times 9 \times 4 \cos 120^\circ = 133\text{,}\) ground speed \(11.5\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) \(\sin d = 4 \sin 120^\circ \div 11.53\text{,}\) \(d = 17.5^\circ\text{.}\) The current pushes anticlockwise, so the track is \(120^\circ - 17.5^\circ = 102.5^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. Challenge: Time to Cross.

    A river is \(80\,\text{m}\) wide. A boat is steered straight across at \(1.6\,\text{m/s}\) through the water, and the current is \(0.9\,\text{m/s}\text{.}\) How long does the crossing take, and how far downstream does the boat land?

    Key Takeaway 11.5.12 Steering a Course.

    When the track is known and the course is not, draw the current arrow from the start, then the course arrow from its tip so that its length is the boat’s speed and its end lies on the track line. If \(\alpha\) is the angle between the track and the direction of the current, the sine rule gives the drift angle \(d\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \sin d = \frac{\text{current speed} \times \sin \alpha}{\text{boat speed}}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Steer \(d\) degrees on the upstream (upwind) side of the track. The ground speed is then
    \begin{equation*} \text{ground speed} = \text{boat speed} \times \cos d + \text{current speed} \times \cos \alpha \end{equation*}
    (the parts of the two velocities along the track). When the current is at right angles to the track, \(\sin d = \text{current speed} \div \text{boat speed}\) and the ground speed is \(\sqrt{\text{boat speed}^2 - \text{current speed}^2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 11.5.13 Straight Across the Likoni Channel.

    A ferry crosses the Likoni channel at Mombasa at \(2.8\,\text{m/s}\) through the water. The current flows at \(0.7\,\text{m/s}\) along the channel. At what angle upstream must the ferry be steered to go straight across, and what is its ground speed?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The current is at right angles to the required track.
    \begin{equation*} \sin d = \frac{0.7}{2.8} = 0.25, \quad d = 14.5^\circ \end{equation*}
    upstream. The ground speed is \(\sqrt{2.8^2 - 0.7^2} = \sqrt{7.35} = 2.7\,\text{m/s}\text{.}\)

    Example 11.5.14 Flying Due North.

    A pilot must fly on a track of \(000^\circ\) (due north). The airspeed is \(150\,\text{knots}\) and a wind of \(25\,\text{knots}\) blows from \(270^\circ\text{.}\) Find the course to steer and the ground speed.
    🔗
    Solution.
    A wind from \(270^\circ\) (from the west) blows towards \(090^\circ\text{,}\) at right angles to the track.
    \begin{equation*} \sin d = \frac{25}{150}, \quad d = 9.6^\circ \end{equation*}
    The wind pushes east, so steer west of north: course \(360^\circ - 9.6^\circ = 350.4^\circ\text{.}\) Ground speed \(\sqrt{150^2 - 25^2} = 147.9\,\text{knots}\text{.}\)

    Example 11.5.15 Making Good a Track of \(060^\circ\).

    A cargo ship must make good a track of \(060^\circ\text{.}\) Its speed through the water is \(12\,\text{knots}\text{,}\) and a current flows towards \(180^\circ\) at \(3.5\,\text{knots}\text{.}\) Find the course to steer and the ground speed.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The angle between the track \(060^\circ\) and the current \(180^\circ\) is \(\alpha = 120^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 1: sine rule.
    \begin{equation*} \sin d = \frac{3.5 \sin 120^\circ}{12}, \quad d = 14.6^\circ \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Step 2: course. The current pushes the ship clockwise from the track, so steer anticlockwise: course \(060^\circ - 14.6^\circ = 045.4^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Step 3: ground speed.
    \begin{equation*} 12 \cos 14.6^\circ + 3.5 \cos 120^\circ = 11.61 - 1.75 = 9.9\,\text{knots} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Note 11.5.16 Safety on the Water.

    Currents are strongest at river mouths, in narrow channels such as the Likoni channel and when the tide turns. Anyone crossing water in a small boat should wear a life jacket and check the current first: a boat that cannot beat the current will be carried away however it is steered. Pilots work out a wind-corrected course before every flight so that they reach their destination with enough fuel.

    5. Challenge: A Current Too Strong.

    A fisherman’s dugout moves at \(1.1\,\text{m/s}\) in still water. After heavy rain the river flows at \(1.3\,\text{m/s}\text{.}\) Explain why the dugout cannot reach the point straight opposite.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Work out \(\sin d\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\sin d = 1.3 \div 1.1 = 1.18\text{,}\) which is impossible.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    To go straight across, \(\sin d = 1.3 \div 1.1 = 1.18\) (to 2 d.p.). The sine of an angle is never more than \(1\text{,}\) so no course works: the current is faster than the dugout, which is always carried downstream.
